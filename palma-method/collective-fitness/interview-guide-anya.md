# Interview Guide — Anya (member)

Duration: approximately 30–40 minutes
Format: semi-structured
Status: draft
Participant status: current TCF member, weakly connected to other members
Related sources: SC-043, SC-044 (Miguel interviews); SC-095 (Francisco interview
— this guide is designed to re-test two things his interview left unresolved);
interview-guide-members.md (base template)

## Why this interview matters

Francisco (SC-095) is also a weakly-connected current member, but two things
compromised his data: he is a personal friend of the interviewer (so any
question touching that relationship is unreliable), and two of the interview's
strongest-looking findings — the "small cell" framing and the recalled
microphone favor — turned out to be answers he confirmed after the
interviewer supplied the frame, not things he volunteered unprompted. Anya has
no personal relationship with the interviewer, which makes her the first
clean opportunity to re-run the same two open questions: (1) is weak
connection at TCF better explained by something structural about the gym, or
by the member's own disposition (Francisco offered "small social battery" as
a competing explanation, unprompted — worth checking whether that is
idiosyncratic to him or a pattern); and (2) is Miguel's centrality real or
not — Francisco's own answer contradicted itself (said he'd still feel part
of the community without Miguel, but also named Miguel as one of only three
people he identifies with), and the question was never followed up.

## Opening — 0–5 minutes

- Confirm purpose, consent, recording preference, and confidentiality.
- Ask Anya to describe TCF in her own words, before introducing any Palma
  terminology.

## Arrival and return — 5–10 minutes

1. How did you first hear about TCF, and what made you try it?
2. What made you come back a second time, a third time?

## Connection level — 10–20 minutes

3. How connected do you feel to the other members, day to day?
4. **Ask before offering any explanation of your own:** why do you think that
   is — is it something about how TCF works, or more about you and how you
   generally are with new people? (Do not suggest "structural" or
   "dispositional" as options — let her generate her own explanation first.
   Only if she goes blank, ask: is it more about the gym, or more about you?)
5. Is that level of connection something you'd want to change, or are you
   fine with it? (Do not imply that more connections would be better.)
6. Who at TCF would you call "your people"? Not necessarily friends — who
   would you message yourself, and who would message you?

## Miguel — 20–30 minutes

7. What's your relationship with Miguel like, day to day?
8. If you take away the workouts themselves, what — if anything — would be
   left of your connection to TCF?
9. Imagine Miguel disappears from the gym for three months. Would you still
   feel the same way about TCF? **Do not accept a one-word answer — always
   follow up:** what specifically would still be there, or specifically
   wouldn't be? Why?

## Reciprocity — 30–37 minutes

10. What do you usually do for other people at TCF, and what do they do for
    you? Give her time to recall on her own before offering any example
    categories (repairs, meals, coffee, small favors, information).
11. Have you met anyone through the gym you now talk to or see independently
    of it? And the reverse — is there anyone you've seen for a long time but
    never actually connected with?

## Boundaries and norms — 37–44 minutes

12. Are there unspoken rules at TCF that regulars understand but a newcomer
    wouldn't know yet?
13. How does a newcomer become "one of them"? Just showing up enough times?
    Talking? Does someone need to introduce them?
14. Were there moments you felt like an outsider, not fully included — even
    if the overall experience has been good?

## Design vs. organic — 44–48 minutes

15. What does TCF do deliberately that helps people connect, and what just
    happens on its own between people?

## Closing test — 48–52 minutes

16. If TCF closed tomorrow, what would you actually lose — the workouts
    themselves, or something else?
17. What would have to disappear for you to stop thinking of TCF the way you
    do now?
18. What have I not asked that matters?
19. Confirm permissions: may your name and this account be used, in what
    form, and are there parts that must stay off the record? (Real first and
    last names never appear in manuscript text regardless of her answer —
    standing project rule — but still ask, so she knows what to expect and
    can flag anything she wants excluded entirely.)

## Interviewer reminders

- Ask for concrete episodes, not general impressions.
- On questions 4 and 9 specifically: resist supplying a frame, category, or
  term before she answers in her own words. This is the main methodological
  fix relative to the Francisco interview — two of that interview's best
  material turned out to be confirmations of the interviewer's own framing,
  not spontaneous answers, and question 9's disconfirmation of hub-dependency
  was a bare "yes" with no follow-up.
- Avoid collecting health or other sensitive personal information unless
  essential and explicitly volunteered.
- Confirm permission separately for any exact quote or identifiable story
  before manuscript use. Name handling depends on which rule applies: if Anya
  turns out to be a personal acquaintance of the author (friend, neighbour,
  etc. — not yet established at the time this guide was written), the
  standing no-real-names-of-personal-acquaintances rule applies absolutely,
  the same as Francisco, regardless of her consent. If she is an ordinary
  case-study participant with no personal relationship to the author, she is
  handled like Luciana and Rami — her first name may be used in manuscript
  text, but only with her own explicit, on-record consent via question 19,
  never assumed. Confirm which situation applies before drafting any
  manuscript text from this interview.
