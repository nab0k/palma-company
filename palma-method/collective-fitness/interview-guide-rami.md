# Written Questionnaire — Rami (ex-member)

Format: self-administered, written (no live interviewer)
Estimated time to complete: 20–30 minutes
Status: draft
Participant status: former TCF member (no longer attending)
Related sources: SC-043, SC-044 (Miguel interviews); interview-guide-members.md (base template)

## Why this interview matters

Every account of TCF collected so far is either the founder's or a current
member's. A current member has a live interest in describing TCF well. An
ex-member does not. Rami is the first source who can speak to what TCF
actually was without needing it to still be true — and the first who can
answer the founder-dependence and audience-vs-community questions from the
losing side: what happened when the relationship stopped being renewed by
attendance.

## Note on written format

This is a written exchange, not a recorded conversation — there is no
interviewer present to ask follow-up questions in real time. Each question
below has been written to stand on its own, with the usual live probes
folded in as prompts so Rami can address them directly in his answer. If a
reply is too brief to use, a short written follow-up may be sent afterward.

## Cover note to send to Rami

> I'm collecting a few accounts for a book about how communities actually
> form (or don't) around organizations — TCF is one of the cases. You're one
> of a small number of people I'm asking, and the fact that you're no longer

>
> Answer in as much or as little detail as feels right — concrete moments

## Questions

1. How did you first hear about TCF, and what made you try it?

2. What made you keep coming back in the first few weeks — was it the
   training itself, or something else?

3. Was there a moment when TCF stopped feeling like just a gym to you? Or
   did that never really happen? If there was a moment, what was it?

4. Who did you actually interact with there — before, during, and after
   training? Describe a typical visit, if you can remember one.

5. Was there anyone at TCF you would have contacted for something unrelated
   to fitness — a favor, information, an introduction? Did that ever
   actually happen? If so, what was it?

6. Were you part of a smaller group or regular time-slot crowd, or did you
   mostly train alongside whoever happened to be there that day?

7. Of the people you knew at TCF, is there anyone you still talk to now that
   you've stopped going? What keeps that going, if anything?

8. What actually made you stop going? Please walk through the real decision
   — not just the general reason, but what happened around the time you
   stopped.

9. Was leaving a single moment or a fade? Did anyone at TCF — Miguel,
   coaches, or other members — notice or reach out when you stopped showing
   up? What did that look like, if anything?

10. Looking back, was there something that could have kept you coming that
    never happened — an invitation, a connection, a reason beyond the
    workout itself?

11. Compare TCF to wherever you train now, or to not training in a group at
    all — what's actually different in how people treat each other?

12. If TCF closed tomorrow, would that matter to you personally, beyond
    nostalgia for a place you used to go? Why or why not?

13. Is there anything I haven't asked that you think matters?

## Administrator notes

- The written format removes live probing, so if an answer is too general
  to use (e.g., question 9 answered with just "no one reached out"), send
  one targeted follow-up rather than re-asking the whole questionnaire.
- The test this questionnaire exists to run: did anything happen at TCF
  that Rami still carries now that attendance has stopped? That is the
  difference between an audience relationship (ends when the service ends)
  and a community relationship (persists past it).
- Avoid collecting health or other sensitive personal information unless
  essential and explicitly volunteered.
- Confirm permission separately for any exact quote or identifiable story
  before manuscript use.
