# Cross-case synthesis — nine TCF member perspectives

## Scope

This synthesis covers nine people across eight member interview sources, including
one joint couple interview. It is purposive, relationship-influenced, uneven in
format, and not representative. Founder interviews provide context but are not
counted as member corroboration. TCF is a field case, not evidence that the Palma
Method works.

## Central result

The interviews do not describe one uniform “community.” They describe a layered
place in which the same infrastructure produces durable ties for some people,
familiarity without contact for others, and almost no new relationship for at least
one regular user. The useful unit is not the word *community* but the observable
sequence: repeated exposure, joint activity, direct tie, action, response, and
continuation. Cases stop at different points.

## Mechanisms supported, with limits

### 1. Repeated joint practice creates opportunity, not a guaranteed tie

Pairing, team work, and small subgroups recur across positive cases. SC-099 shows
that membership plus solitary or rotating attendance can leave this route unused.
SC-142 and SC-152 add that convenience and schedule can produce repeated exposure
without deliberate social coordination.

### 2. Micro-time matters

Several accounts locate meaningful contact in minutes before or after class, coffee,
Saturday decompression, or walking home. These are interaction openings, not outcomes
by themselves. They matter when the same people use them repeatedly.

### 3. Rituals require a ladder, not a label

The material contains four different things:

1. **Operational routines:** clean shoes, replacing equipment, cleaning mats.
2. **Personal habits:** arriving ten minutes early, using a preferred bar.
3. **Interaction openings:** reception coffee, paired work, waiting or walking together.
4. **Shared recurring practices:** Saturday team work followed by coffee.

Only the latter two plausibly create relational opportunity, and even then the
interviews do not show that every participant uses it.

### 4. Merchandise can be conversational infrastructure

Across SC-099, SC-152, and SC-046, merchandise appears as a topic through which a
founder and participants can have a slightly less transactional exchange. In SC-152,
a color suggestion was reportedly incorporated and followed by a purchase. SC-142
places merchandise inside a wider premium-service perception.

The defensible claim is narrow: asking for views and visibly responding can offer a
low-risk way to speak and contribute. The evidence does **not** establish shared
governance, causal “ownership,” loyalty, or relationship formation. Merchandise also
remains a commercial product.

### 5. Couples can bridge and insulate

SC-143 shows a possible second-order network extension through a spouse. SC-152 makes
the dual mechanism explicit: attending together can lower the entry barrier and
generate introductions, but it can also remove the need to approach anyone else.
Couple attendance should therefore not be coded as automatically more connected.

### 6. Founder dependence is graded

Across cases, the founder ranges from nearly irrelevant to a specific relationship,
to important for tone and brokerage, to central to arrival and daily experience.
SC-152 expects the infrastructure to survive a temporary absence while still missing
the founder as a person. This is dependence on different functions, not one binary
organizational property.

### 7. Persistence is clearest at the boundary

SC-096 and one side of SC-143 report contact after relocation or reduced attendance.
These are the best tests of whether a network survives without frequent access to the
place, although one chronology remains low-confidence. Other positive accounts remain
mostly place-dependent and should not be upgraded to durable friendship.

## Negative and disconfirming evidence

- The low-regularity case forms no new TCF tie despite pre-existing acquaintances.
- The most enthusiastic long-tenured participant reports stronger conversion to
  outside friendship and more organized events at a Brazilian gym.
- A high-regularity participant says timing, not the booking list, usually determines
  class choice.
- A regular early-morning participant knows many faces but not their names.
- Professional help may be commercial exchange; merchandise purchase may be product
  preference; coffee may be convenience. None is self-interpreting.
- Proximity, training quality, schedule, personality, culture, migration, life stage,
  and partner routine remain live alternative explanations.

## Sensitive post-loss material: research only

One interview supports only the general statement that TCF participants helped a
member with grief and helped the affected family. Separate project context reports a
dog-care and merchandise/food-fund sequence. The second sequence is not contained in
the member transcript, and prior manuscript wording is not independent verification.

No death, dog, merchandise design, collection, proceeds, or caregiving detail may be
quoted, paraphrased, or alluded to in the manuscript until the affected participant
separately consents to each category and the factual sequence is checked with directly
involved adults. The episode's research value is the observable action before and
after loss, not drama.

## What the corpus supports now

- Joint activity plus repeated exposure can create routes for direct ties.
- Short unprogrammed windows and low-stakes participation can widen access to talk.
- Concrete action, response, and continuation are documented in some cases, but not
  at equal strength.
- Boundaries persist inside the same facility: subgroup, slot, language, couple,
  regularity, and disposition all shape access.
- Founder warmth, founder brokerage, and network survival are separate questions.

## What the corpus does not support

- That TCF is a single community for everyone.
- That attendance frequency alone causes friendship or retention.
- That rituals or merchandise automatically create belonging.
- That the founder is either wholly necessary or irrelevant.
- That the Palma Method caused anything observed at TCF.

## Questions for the next interview

For Cathal, prioritize missing contrasts rather than repeating general praise:

1. Which recurring TCF routines does he actually participate in, and which does he
   ignore?
2. Ask for one relationship sequence: first interaction, later action, response, and
   what happens outside training.
3. Has he ever given feedback on merchandise, schedule, format, or the space? What
   changed, if anything, and did that affect later behavior?
4. Does he know people across time slots or mainly within one subgroup? Who remains a
   familiar stranger?
5. What would continue if Miguel were absent for a month, and what would stop?
6. What happened during a period of reduced attendance, travel, or absence?
7. What makes a couple, newcomer, immigrant, or irregular attendee easier or harder
   to include from his perspective?
