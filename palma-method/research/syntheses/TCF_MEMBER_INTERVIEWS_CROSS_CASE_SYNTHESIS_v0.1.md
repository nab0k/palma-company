# Cross-case synthesis — five TCF member interviews

**Scope warning, stated once and binding for the whole document:** these are **five**
interviews out of a gym with, per SC-044/098, thousands of visits and dozens of
regular members. They were not randomly sampled — four were selected partly because
the author already knew them, and the fifth specifically as a designed contrast case.
Nothing below should be read as "TCF members generally..." — only as "these five
people, asked comparable questions, said the following."

## Confirming, contradicting, and null cases

- **Confirming cases** (repeated co-presence in *structured, joint* activity produces
  connection): Francisco, Anya, and Luciana all describe connection forming through
  shared training specifically, not mere shared membership.
- **Contradicting/complicating cases**: Luciana's Brazil comparison is the project's
  first genuine disconfirming signal — from its most enthusiastic, best-connected
  informant — that TCF converts membership to friendship *less* than a comparable
  organization she has direct experience of. This should be reported as a real
  complication, not smoothed into the otherwise-positive pattern.
- **Null case**: SC-099 (low-regularity) is the clean negative case — same domain,
  some pre-existing ties, but the structured-practice condition met too rarely to
  produce anything. This is the strongest single piece of evidence in the whole set
  for the domain/community/practice distinction, because it isolates the variable
  (frequency and format of joint practice) rather than comparing across different
  organizations.
- **Competing explanations that survive scrutiny**: dispositional/cultural threshold
  (most explicit in SC-099, present in weaker form in SC-095's "social battery" and
  SC-096's performance-gating) coexists with, and does not replace, the structural
  account. The honest reading across all five cases is that both operate together —
  no case here isolates one from the other cleanly.

## Mechanisms that recur across cases

1. **Reputation precedes access** (Ch3): Francisco, Anya, and Luciana all did some
   form of pre-signup vetting or recognition of Miguel specifically, before any
   relationship existed — a real, repeated pattern across independent arrival stories,
   not just the founder's own account of it.
2. **Founder-dependency is graded, not binary**: three genuinely different
   per-participant answers (Francisco flat-no, Rami middling, Anya graduated) to the
   same counterfactual. This is itself a finding — hub-centrality should be measured
   per relationship, not asserted as a single organization-level property.
3. **Stated connectivity understates actual reciprocity**: both Francisco's and (to a
   lesser extent) SC-099's participant's general self-assessments ("they do nothing
   for me" / low effort to connect) turned out to understate or fully match specific,
   concrete episodes or absence when checked — a methodological point about asking
   for episodes, not impressions, that held in the one case where it was tested
   (Francisco) and was confirmed rather than contradicted in the null case.
4. **Structure explains itself as accident, not design**: both Francisco and Rami
   independently attribute the same design feature (small in-class subgroups) to
   Miguel solving a practical constraint, not a deliberate community-building choice —
   convergent testimony from two unconnected interviews.

## Limits of the data

- Five interviews, four recruited partly through the author's own acquaintance,
  cannot establish base rates for TCF as a whole.
- No corroboration yet from Miguel or Margarida on any of the design-vs-emergent
  claims, the barefoot-safety norm, or the "greet the customer" framing.
- Two interviews (Rami, Anya) have no on-record consent for use at all; this synthesis
  itself should not be read as clearing them for manuscript use.
- Wodify (attendance/scheduling data) has not been checked against any of these
  qualitative claims — see open questions below.

## Conclusions that can be drawn

- The domain/community/practice distinction already argued in Chapter 2 holds up
  under a genuine attempt to find a disconfirming case (SC-099) and gets a clean
  positive/negative contrast from within one organization.
- Founder-dependency is not a yes/no property of TCF; it varies by relationship, with
  at least three distinct positions now on record.
- Reputation-precedes-access is a repeated pattern across ordinary members' own
  arrival stories, not only the founder's telling of it.

## Conclusions that cannot be drawn

- That TCF is (or is not) "a community" in any unified sense — the five accounts
  actively disagree on this, which is itself the more interesting and honest finding.
- That the Palma Method produced any of TCF's outcomes — TCF predates the Method and
  none of these interviews test a Method intervention.
- That the regularity/practice threshold identified in SC-099 is a general law —
  it is one clean case, not a tested threshold.
- That Luciana's unfavorable Brazil comparison generalizes to a defect in TCF's
  design — it is one person's comparison across two specific organizations she
  happened to belong to.

## Questions worth checking against Wodify

- Do the pairs/small groups that Francisco, Anya, and Luciana describe as friends show
  up as increasingly correlated attendance times in Wodify over the relevant period?
- Does SC-099's participant's attendance data (one weekly class + ~2 solo sessions)
  actually show the group-class slot rotating among different people each week, which
  would independently support her "not grouped with the same people" account?

## Questions for remaining/future interviews

- A sixth interview with a member who has *not* previously known the author would help
  separate recruitment-selection effects from genuine variance in the TCF population.
