core_claim: >
  In their early years, Uber and Airbnb achieved massive consumer popularity and strong user-level
  reputation (people loved using them, adoption grew explosively) while simultaneously being
  declared illegal or banned outright by regulators and cities: Uber was banned in Vancouver and
  Austin (among others); Airbnb was banned in cities including Irvine and Temecula, California, and
  restricted heavily in New York and San Francisco after complaints that it enabled "illegal
  hotels." Taxi drivers and hoteliers filed formal complaints framing both companies as unlicensed,
  illegitimate competitors. Reputation (how users felt about the product) and legitimacy (whether
  regulators and incumbents accepted the companies as appropriate, legal actors) moved in opposite
  directions during this period.

  A clean, well-documented, real-world illustration of Suchman's reputation/legitimacy distinction

  eyes of the regulatory and institutional gatekeepers who governed whether the companies could
  legally operate at all - the two constructs are shown moving independently, not merely
  correlated.
