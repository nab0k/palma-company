# The Contribution of Migration to Economic Development in Holland, 1570-1800

## Status

**Checked, not yet verified.** Traced to a real, citable journal article and

improvement over the original "по оценкам" claim. Still short of `verified`:

## What the search found

The figure Serhii recalled traces to two layers of scholarship:

1. **Jan and Leo Lucassen**, migration historians whose demographic
   reconstruction of the Dutch Republic is the likely source of the
   underlying "quantitative turn" figures Serhii remembered - their work
   established that roughly 600,000 foreigners settled in the cities of the
   province of Holland across the 16th-17th centuries.
2. **Foldvari, van Leeuwen, and van Zanden (2013)**, "The Contribution of
   Migration to Economic Development in Holland, 1570-1800," *De Economist*
   161(1): 1-18 - a growth-accounting paper that builds directly on the
   Lucassens' reconstruction to argue migration had a measurable, positive
   effect on Dutch Golden Age income per capita.

Independent sources (a Leiden University research-project page, University
of Groningen demographic-history papers, and academic summaries of the
Lucassens' work) converge on:

- ~600,000 foreign settlers in Holland's cities, 16th-17th centuries - matches Serhii's recollection closely.
- ~40% of Amsterdam's population foreign-born circa 1650 and again circa 1700 - matches closely.
- ~50% of Haarlem and Leiden's population as immigrants from Brabant and Flanders specifically - close to, but not an exact match for, the ~55% Serhii recalled, and for a narrower category (one region of origin, not "foreigners" broadly).
