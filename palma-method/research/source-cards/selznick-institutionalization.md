# Selznick — institutionalization

## Priority given to primary text / official data

Primary 1957 text (quote and page number cross-checked across multiple independent
secondary sources including a dedicated academic volume on Selznick's legacy).

## Specific fragments supporting the claim

The quoted definition ("infuse with value beyond the technical requirements") and the
expendability test are both directly Selznick's own formulation, page 17 of the
original.

## Why this source deserves a place in the book

It gives Chapter 21 a distinct, well-established theoretical account of
institutionalization - focused on meaning and value, not (like Ostrom, already used
in the same chapter) on governance rules - which lets the chapter make a different,
complementary point about what "handover" really tests.

## What is original in it

Selznick's own foundational distinction between organization and institution, and the
specific "infusion with value" definition, are his own contribution.

## Limitations / competing interpretation

DiMaggio & Powell's institutional-isomorphism account offers a different primary
mechanism for organizational persistence (external pressure toward similarity, not
internal value infusion) - a genuine competing framework, not cited in the manuscript
but worth knowing exists.

## Permissible level of claim

"Selznick's framework offers one well-established way to describe what changed in
TAK.Shtab by 2023-2024" - supportable, framed as an interpretive lens. "TAK.Shtab
passed Selznick's expendability test" as a proven fact - overclaims, since no actual
test of whether participants would discard the structure for a more efficient
alternative occurred; the manuscript already hedges this ("судя по всему").

## Forbidden overgeneralization

Do not present Selznick's framework as the only or definitive account of
institutionalization - competing accounts exist (see limitations).
