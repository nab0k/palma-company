# Dynes & Quarantelli — emergent-groups typology

## Priority given to primary text / official data

DRC's own institutional materials and preliminary papers, cross-checked against an
independent academic secondary summary (Strandh 2018, peer-reviewed journal).

## Specific fragments supporting the claim

The four-category typology (established/expanding/extending/emergent) is stated
consistently across DRC's own materials and the independent academic summary.

## Why this source deserves a place in the book

It gives Chapter 19 a precise, named sociological category for what TAK.Shtab was at
founding - a group with neither pre-existing structure nor mandate - rather than
leaving "emergent organization" as an unattributed descriptive phrase.

## What is original in it

The four-part typology itself is Dynes and Quarantelli's original contribution,
foundational to the disaster-sociology subfield they helped create via the DRC.

## Limitations / competing interpretation

Original sample is natural-disaster response, not wartime humanitarian logistics;
later work (Strandh 2018) shows the categories are not perfectly clean in practice.

## Permissible level of claim

"Dynes and Quarantelli's typology names TAK.Shtab's founding condition as an
'emergent' organization" - supportable, with the extension from natural disaster to
wartime context acknowledged as an analogy, not a direct citation of a wartime case
from their own research.

## Forbidden overgeneralization

Do not present the typology as proof that all emergent groups succeed, endure, or
outperform established organizations - it is a descriptive category, not a
performance claim.
