core_claim: >
  Dormant ties - relationships that existed in the past (often strong ones) but have gone inactive
  for years through simple inattention, happenstance, and inertia rather than deliberate rupture -
  can be surprisingly valuable to reconnect with. In a study prompting Executive MBA students to
  consult a dormant contact about an important work project, the advice received compared favorably
  to advice from current active ties. Reconnected dormant ties combined benefits usually associated
  with weak ties (efficiency, novel information/perspective) AND benefits usually associated with
  strong ties (trust, shared context) simultaneously - a combination the authors argue neither pure
  weak nor pure strong active ties reliably deliver alone.
summary: >
  The seminal, most-cited paper specifically on dormant ties - directly answers the gap this
  project's research gap flagged: existing corpus covers tie strength/formation (Granovetter) and
  structural position (Burt) well, but nothing on what happens to ties that go inactive and whether
  they retain value. This paper's central, counterintuitive finding - dormant ties can outperform
  active ties precisely because they've had time to diverge, accumulating new, non-redundant
  knowledge and perspective while the underlying trust from the original relationship persists -
  gives Palma a genuinely surprising, well-evidenced claim rather than an intuitive platitude.
key_evidence: >
  Field study: Executive MBA students identified a dormant tie (a past strong relationship, now out
  of touch), reconnected with them to seek advice on a real, current work project, and rated the
  value of that advice against advice from current active ties. Reconnected dormant ties matched or
  exceeded current ties on advice usefulness while requiring comparatively little effort to
  reactivate.
important_quote: >
