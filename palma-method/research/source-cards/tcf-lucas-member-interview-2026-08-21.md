# TCF member interview — regular early-morning participant

## Evidence layers

### Participant statements

- Proximity, schedule, coaching quality, and social atmosphere all contributed to attendance.
- Paired work, Saturday sessions, coffee, and walking home created more conversation than rushed
  weekday arrivals alone.
- Attending with a spouse sometimes eased introduction and sometimes reduced the need to meet others.
- Many early-morning regulars remained familiar faces rather than named contacts.
- The founders sought opinions on a T-shirt; a spouse's color idea was incorporated and he bought two.
- A temporary founder absence would be noticeable but would not, in the participant's view, dissolve
  the routines and relationships already in place.

### Project interpretation

The merchandise sequence is evidence of **low-stakes participation and conversational access**. It
is not evidence that members govern the organization, that the feedback caused belonging, or that a
purchase demonstrates reciprocity. The couple account is similarly two-sided: a partner can be a
bridge to another person and an alternative to reaching outward.

## Ritual classification

The account mentions recurring operational and personal routines: clean shoes, arriving early,
preparing equipment, a preferred bar, replacing equipment, cleaning mats, four workout blocks,
coffee, and the Saturday format. These should not be collapsed into one category. Some are personal
habits, some are institutional rules, and only some create repeated interaction or a shared topic.
