# Brehm — psychological reactance

## Priority given to primary text / official data

Foundational original text (1966), cross-checked against contemporary secondary
summaries (The Decision Lab, Psychology Today, academic reviews) that consistently
describe the same mechanism.

## Specific fragments supporting the claim

The core mechanism (autonomy threat → motivated resistance, potentially reversing the
suggested action) is consistent across every source checked.

## Why this source deserves a place in the book

It gives Chapter 20 a specific psychological mechanism, distinct from Schein's
organizational-consulting distinction, for why unsolicited advice fails - useful
because it operates even when the advice is correct, which sharpens the chapter's
point that giving advice well is not primarily about being right.

## What is original in it

Brehm's own foundational theoretical contribution, one of social psychology's
best-established frameworks.

## Limitations / competing interpretation

Individual variation in reactance-proneness is real; some people are more resistant
to reactance than others, and the theory alone does not predict who.

## Permissible level of claim

"Unsolicited advice can trigger reactance, a defensive response independent of the
advice's quality" - well supported. "All resistance to advice is reactance" -
overclaims; ordinary disagreement based on the advice's actual merits is a separate,
non-reactance explanation the manuscript should not collapse into this one.

## Forbidden overgeneralization

Do not imply reactance theory alone explains every instance of someone not taking
advice - it is one mechanism among several (including simply disagreeing on the
merits).
