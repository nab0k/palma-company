  In venture-capital fundraising, warm introductions convert to a first meeting at roughly 20-30%,
  versus 1-2% for cold email outreach to the same investors - a 10-20x gap. The gap is graded by
  relationship type, not binary: intros from a VC's own portfolio founders convert at 30-40%, from
  angels/advisors at 15-20%, from friends of the founder at only 3-5%. In broader B2B sales, cold
  email reply rates cluster around 2-5% (with an average near 3-4% across 2024-2025 benchmarks),
  while warm/referred outreach reaches 10-34% response and 40-60% meeting rates - a consistently
  reported 5-10x efficiency gap between warm and cold channels.
summary: >
  A well-corroborated, if secondary-sourced, pattern across the fundraising and B2B sales industries:
  having a channel to a specific person (a cold email that technically reaches their inbox) converts
  far worse than having a channel plus a trusted intermediary vouching for you - and the size of the
  effect scales with how much the introducer's own credibility is genuinely on the line (a portfolio
  founder vouching converts much better than a founder's personal friend with no track record with
  the investor). This is a clean, numbers-based illustration of the book's access-vs-trust
  distinction: technical reachability (a working email address) is necessary but nowhere near
  sufficient - the response is governed by whether the channel carries real trust.
