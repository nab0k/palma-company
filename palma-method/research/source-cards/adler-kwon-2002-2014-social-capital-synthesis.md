core_claim: >
  Social capital must always be assessed relative to a named "focal actor" (an individual, group,
  or organization) and a specific outcome - the same network position can simultaneously benefit
  one actor and create a liability for another (e.g., an employee's external network benefits the
  employee's career and can create a dependency risk for the employer). Social capital carries
  benefits, risks, and contingencies; it is not an unconditional asset. By 2014, the authors argue
  the field had matured from general "connections matter" claims toward specific mechanisms and
  boundary conditions.

  it forces any claim about social capital's value to specify whose value is being discussed.
