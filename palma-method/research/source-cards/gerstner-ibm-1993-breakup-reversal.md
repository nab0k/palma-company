# Gerstner / IBM 1993 — diagnosis-under-consensus case

## Priority given to primary text / official data

Gerstner's own memoir (primary first-person account) plus IBM's own corporate history
page, cross-checked against independent business-press retrospectives.

## Specific fragments supporting the claim

The 1993 breakup discussion (investment bankers valuing prospective spin-offs) and its
cancellation in favor of a unified, services-led strategy is consistently reported
across all sources checked.

## Why this source deserves a place in the book

It is a large-scale, well-documented, independently verifiable case of the exact
mechanism Chapter 17 argues for at a personal scale: a diagnosis endorsed by many
experts (the board, analysts, bankers already pricing the pieces) that turned out to
be wrong when someone looked at the same facts differently.

## What is original in it

Not a novel finding - a well-known business-history case, used here as an
illustration, not as a source of new theory.

## Limitations / competing interpretation

See limitations and counterarguments above - IBM's recovery had multiple causes; the
services pivot itself is not universally read as an unqualified success in all later
accounts.

## Permissible level of claim

"The board's diagnosis was near-unanimous and Gerstner reversed it, building IBM
Global Services" - supportable. "Gerstner's reversal alone saved IBM" - overclaims
causal weight the case doesn't cleanly support.

## Forbidden overgeneralization

Do not present this as proof that contrarian diagnosis is generally correct - it is
one case where the near-consensus turned out to be wrong, not evidence that consensus
diagnoses are usually wrong.
