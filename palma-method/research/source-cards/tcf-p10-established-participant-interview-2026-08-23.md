# TCF member interview — established participant (TCF-P10)

## Use and consent boundary

The participant allowed anonymous paraphrase, not direct quotation or use of her real name. Before
anything from this interview is proposed for the book, she must be shown the exact paraphrase, fact,
or scene and approve it. This card does not itself clear any material for publication. A separate
private source map must preserve the connection between `TCF-P10` and the raw source; GitHub does not.

## Evidence layers

### Participant reports

- The initial reasons to join were structured training, proximity, expertise, accountability, and
  not having to design each session; social connection was a secondary benefit.
- Participation moved through three self-described phases: habit-building, intensive attendance, and
  later rebalancing because of travel, business demands, illness, and relationships outside the gym.
- Founder expertise, autonomy, and personal attention contributed to technical and interpersonal trust.
- A female coach reportedly improved instruction quality for women and confidence in the coaching team.
- The participant greets others, learns names in assigned groups, helps organize confused groups, and
  sometimes asks coaches clarifying questions to make it easier for less confident participants to ask.
- She sometimes notices longer absences and reaches out, and uses coffee, stretching, and small gifts
  as occasions for interaction.
- She reported a small number of close active ties, a wider set of reachable contacts, and selected
  friendships that continued after members left or moved.
- She reported professional introductions, advisory or work opportunities, invitations, trips, and
  knowledge exchange arising from relationships associated with TCF.
- She described occasional discomfort involving staring, unsolicited instruction, or inappropriate
  approaches, alongside informal norms around distance, eye contact, volume, language, and humor.

### Project interpretation

The strongest candidate mechanism is not the word “community” but a sequence of observable actions:
someone notices confusion, makes it discussable, helps coordinate the group, and reduces immediate
participation friction. This may distribute a small part of the social infrastructure beyond the
founder. The account also suggests that different functions have different dependencies: technical
trust, being heard, and social tone may rely more heavily on the founder than selected peer ties do.

### Rival explanations and contrary material

- The participant's own initiative and social confidence may be doing more causal work than the gym.
- Convenience, coaching quality, and training structure can explain attendance without social ties.
- Time and consistency create exposure, but exposure alone does not demonstrate friendship or trust.
- Small groups can deepen interaction while also creating separate bubbles and boundaries.
- Migrant or expatriate circumstances may increase demand for connection independently of TCF design.
- Reported professional and social value may reflect a few unusually compatible relationships rather
  than a general member experience.
- Gendered discomfort and informal boundary violations contradict any simple claim of universal safety
  or inclusion.

## Restricted or unsupported material

The following items must not be used as factual book claims without separate evidence:

- estimated counts for close ties, reachable people, or faces recognized;
- a predicted response to a month-long founder absence;
- broad cultural or personality-type generalizations;
- claims about exercise hormones, body-language percentages, or a fixed friendship timeline;
- names or identifiable details of friends, former members, coaches, or business contacts;
- details of gendered discomfort beyond a participant-approved generic formulation.
