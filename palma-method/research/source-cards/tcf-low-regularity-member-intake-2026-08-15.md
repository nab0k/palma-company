# TCF non-connected member case (low-regularity attendee) — 15 August 2026

## What the account contributes

Every other TCF interview in this project so far (SC-095 Francisco,
SC-096 Rami, SC-097 Anya, SC-098 Luciana) comes from a member who formed
real connections at the gym, in varying density. This is the first case
of a member who formed *none* - and the absence is not for lack of
domain (same gym) or even lack of some pre-existing relationships in the
room (she already knew several members before joining, including the
author himself). What's missing is specifically the repeated, synchronized
co-presence of the group-class format: she attends that format only once
a week, supplementing it with solo "open gym" sessions where members
train independently, often with headphones, with minimal ambient
interaction.

This maps directly onto the book's own domain/community/practice test
(Chapter 2, applied to TCF via SC-044): domain is present, a
neighbouring condition (pre-existing personal ties) is present, but the
practice condition - repeated joint activity - is not met at sufficient
frequency, and community accordingly did not form for her. It is the
project's first genuine negative case from within the same organization
as its positive cases, which makes it more useful than a negative case
from an unrelated context would be.

## Follow-up interview — completed 14 August 2026 (answers)

All six original follow-up questions were asked in a recorded ~12.5-minute
conversation and answered directly:

1. **Dispositional rival explanation, outside TCF.** She does form
   connections elsewhere, but characteristically through pre-established
   shared context first (online chat with future colleagues before
   meeting in person; university group projects) - "a lot of my friends
   started out that way." In school, she made friends within her
   assigned class but almost never across grade levels despite years of
   shared physical space - an unprompted domain-vs-practice distinction
   from her own biography, not from the interview frame.
2. **Open-gym acknowledgment.** With Miguel: "we say hi to each other."
   With everyone else at open gym: "no, never, like, no, nothing" -
   confirmed as a literal, not just relative, zero.
3. **Pre-existing relationships at the gym.** Even relationships that
   predate TCF (including with the author) go quiet there: "we always
   interact less because we are doing our own stuff... we just say hi
   hello, we don't have a real conversation there."
4. **Nature of the Miguel relationship.** Explicitly transactional in her
   own framing: "he owns a business, you greet the customer... sometimes
   it turns a bit more" - she references the Roxy T-shirt fundraiser
   (see SC-049/Chapter 14) as an example of that "a bit more," but
   otherwise describes it as surface-level.
5. **Does she want more connection?** "I wouldn't mind to have more
   connection, that would be nice. It's just not something I even... I
   never thought to put some effort into it." Not dissatisfaction - see
   also her closing remark that she already has "a good social life" and
   "enough friends."
6. **Would higher frequency alone change things?** She refines the
   hypothesis herself: raw frequency ("three times a week") would only
   plausibly work if she were also grouped with the same specific
   people, and only with some active intention on her part - "if I'm
   just very passive about it, it's probably not gonna happen."

An unprompted seventh thread, opened by the interviewer asking a
deliberately broad "what am I missing" question, surfaced a substantial,
volunteered cultural account: raised in the Philippines, she describes
approaching strangers as not a normalized behavior growing up (her own
example: her mother talking to strangers on public transport read to her
as "weird"); within Filipino social settings specifically she describes
the opposite - openly warm and friendly - but not toward strangers
outside that in-group; the same cross-group-friendship scarcity she
reports for TCF and open gym also shows up in her account of school
(rarely befriending students outside her own grade) and in how she
rebuilt a social circle after leaving an office job (joining a
pre-existing interest group - travel - rather than making friends
ambiently). She names this as likely cultural rather than purely
personal, though she is not certain why, and separately notes that years
living abroad have loosened the norm for her somewhat without removing
it: "it would have to be a skill that I actively develop."
