# CrowdStrike outage / Delta response — business-case anchor for Chapter 19

## Priority given to primary text / official data

Microsoft's own incident disclosure for the device count; Delta's own lawsuit filing
and public statements for the cancellation count and loss estimate; independent
business-press coverage for cross-checking.

## Specific fragments supporting the claim

8.5 million devices (Microsoft's figure); ~7,000 Delta cancellations over five days;
~$500 million Delta-claimed loss; the October 2024 lawsuit - all consistently reported.

## Why this source deserves a place in the book

It supplies a real, checkable, large-scale case of the exact phenomenon Chapter 19
closes on: an unplanned failure where recovery capacity, not the plan, determined the
outcome - directly parallel to the TAK.Shtab convoy scene at a completely different
scale and domain.

## What is original in it

Not original theory - a real business event used as illustrative evidence.

## Limitations / competing interpretation

The *reason* Delta's recovery took longer than peer airlines is genuinely disputed
between Delta and CrowdStrike in ongoing litigation - the manuscript correctly avoids
asserting a specific cause and sticks to the observable outcome difference.

## Permissible level of claim

"The same failure produced markedly different recovery times across airlines, with
Delta's own claimed loss at roughly $500 million" - supportable, sourced to Delta's
own figures. "Delta's IT was worse than other airlines'" or any other causal claim
about *why* - NOT supportable; that is precisely what the litigation disputes.

## Forbidden overgeneralization

Do not adjudicate the Delta/CrowdStrike legal dispute in the manuscript's favor either
way - use only the undisputed facts (same trigger, different recovery times, Delta's
own public loss figure and lawsuit).
