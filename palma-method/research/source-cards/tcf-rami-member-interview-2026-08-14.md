## What the account contributes

The standout finding is Rami's unprompted rejection of "community" in favor
of "collective" - not a throwaway line but a developed distinction (in/out
alienation vs. activity-based coexistence) that lands directly on the book's
central boundary-work question, delivered by a member of a business
literally named "The Collective Fitness." This deserves real weight as an
edge-case or false-case example when the book defines what counts as
community, alongside Francisco's independently-reached "just a gym."

On founder-dependency, Rami's answer sits between Francisco's clean
disconfirmation and a full confirmation: TCF would continue without Miguel,
but be "less personal, less social, and less fun" - useful precisely because
it resists collapsing into either a clean "yes" or "no" for the book's
hub-dependency argument.

The relocation material is the most concrete: contrary to the interview
guide's assumption that Rami had stopped attending, he still trains at TCF
opportunistically when work brings him to Lisbon, and visiting TCF members
reach out to meet him roughly once a month - a real, dated instance of tie
reactivation across a geographic break, not tie death, directly relevant to
the project's existing dormant-ties research (SC-090, SC-091). At the same
time, he is explicit that the relationships depend on the physical space and
would not survive without it, and that occasional organized events (a
Christmas party) would not substitute for daily low-effort contact - a
useful boundary on how far "the relationship persists" actually extends.
