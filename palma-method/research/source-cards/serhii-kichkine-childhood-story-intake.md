## Recoverable account

Serhii grew up in Gaspra on the southern coast of Crimea. His mother worked at
the Kichkine tourist base, which was connected first with the Soviet and later
with the Ukrainian defence system. Many employees also lived in the same few
apartment buildings and used the same schools, clinics, transport, and local
services. Work relations, neighbourhood relations, and family life therefore
overlapped.

As a child, Serhii moved between the different parts of the tourist base. He
spent time with drivers, electricians, warehouse staff, guides, managers,
canteen staff, and other employees. He could join an excursion, accompany a
driver, visit a storeroom, or sit with someone at work. His phrase “son of the
regiment” is explicitly figurative: the group, rather than one assigned adult,
made room for the child in its daily life.

The wider childhood story contains a separate, sensitive scene in which a
teacher voluntarily offered to take responsibility for Serhii after a family
loss. It may illustrate how dense local ties can expand a person's sense of
obligation beyond a formal job. The event is deeply personal and remains
blocked from manuscript use until Serhii explicitly approves the wording and
decides which family details may be public.

## Editorial value and limits

This is currently a rich social setting with one potentially strong scene. It
can illustrate overlapping networks, recognition, informal care, and the
benefits and pressures of closure. It should not be romanticized: the same
density that enables help also produces surveillance, limited privacy, and
dependence on a small circle.

## Required work

1. Confirm the official spelling and institutional history of Kichkine.
2. Select one scene rather than list every employee and location.
3. Separate remembered fact from later interpretation.
4. Decide whether the teacher scene may be published and how the people are
   anonymized.
5. Ask what the same dense network made difficult, not only what it made
   possible.
