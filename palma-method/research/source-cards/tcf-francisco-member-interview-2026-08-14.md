## Safety and consent — read before any further use

Two items from the completed transcript change how this material must be
handled, and both outrank the interview's research value.

**1. The name question was asked, but the answer is not usable consent.**
Near the end, the author directly asked the participant, on record, whether
to use his real name in the book. The participant's actual response was
hesitant, self-contradicting, and closed with conditional acquiescence
rather than a clear yes: he moved from "no, I don't think so" through "if
you really need names, okay." This reads as a friend not wanting to make the
interviewer's project harder, not as informed, freely given consent - the
exact dynamic the project's standing no-real-names rule exists to route
around. This card follows that rule regardless of the exchange: his real
name must never appear in manuscript text.

**2. He disclosed a personal safety concern, not just a privacy preference.**
Asked (in the context of a hypothetical Ukrainian-market edition) whether a
Ukrainian version would be a problem, he said yes, for two reasons - he
doesn't need it, and: "I'm telling a lot of things that I would not share...
I don't know because you know, I moved here during the war... it's not a
good idea to be public." **Correction (2026-08-14, per author review of the
full speaker-separated transcript): the humanitarian-organization story that
follows in the raw transcript ("I organized a humanitarian organization here
during the war, and it worked, it was voluntary, based on the community
itself") was misattributed in the first version of this card. That is the
author's own TAK.Shtab story (see SC-049), offered by the author as a
parallel example mid-interview - not a disclosure by the participant.** What
the participant did disclose, and what remains the operative safety concern,
is narrower: he relocated during the war, prefers not to be publicly
identifiable, does not want a Ukrainian-market edition, and said he is
"telling a lot of things" he would not want shared - without specifying what
those things are. Treat that stated preference as the safety boundary:
nothing about his wartime relocation or any unspecified sensitive material
should appear in the manuscript, even genericized, unless he is asked again,
specifically and separately from this interview, exactly what (if anything)
he would be comfortable having described, and confirms that in writing.

## What the account contributes

Unlike the founder interview (SC-044), which describes TCF's community
mechanics from the person who built them, this interview tests those
mechanics from a specific, structurally under-connected member's point of
view - and the results are mixed rather than confirmatory, which is itself
useful.

The participant explicitly rejects "community" as his own word for TCF,
preferring "just a gym," while granting that other members might describe it
differently and pointing to one subgroup (foreigners who met each other
there) he would call a community. Asked to imagine Miguel absent for three
months, he says he would still feel part of the same group - a direct
disconfirmation of the founder-centrality hypothesis this project has been
carrying since the pre-interview design discussion, at least for this
participant. That result should be kept, not smoothed over.

The interview's strongest material is the gap between the participant's
stated and demonstrated behavior around reciprocity: he first denies that
anything is exchanged in these relationships in a "functional sense," then,
prompted with two specific remembered episodes, confirms both (lending a
microphone; sharing a detailed Japan trip itinerary with several members).
This is a clean illustration of why the interview guide's discipline -
concrete episodes over general impressions - matters in practice, not just
in principle.

He also volunteers an unprompted, specific answer to the design-vs-organic
question: the gym's small in-class subgroups, which he says ease
conversation, likely arose from Miguel solving a space and equipment
constraint carried over from his CrossFit background, not from a deliberate
community-building decision.
