# Author story intake — Kyiv Military Lyceum

The project document preserves the author account, a preliminary fact-check,

## Recoverable account

Serhii entered the Kyiv Military Lyceum named after Ivan Bohun at thirteen and
studied there from 2000 to 2004. His compact description is the strongest
opening: “We were schoolchildren and soldiers at the same time.” Lessons,
physical training, cleaning, duty, meals, preparation, and scarce free time
were organized under one institutional regime.

The first two years in the educational and health complex in Boyarka and the
next two years in Kyiv felt like two different worlds inside one formal
institution. The rules may have been shared, while the forest setting,
distance from the capital, age of the pupils, local events, and unofficial
language produced a distinct culture. Visits by officials and television also
created a visible gap between daily life and the version of the institution
prepared for outsiders.

## Scientific fit

Erving Goffman's work on total institutions offers a disciplined lens: many
parts of life occur in one place, under one authority, in groups, and according
to an imposed schedule. His concepts of batch living, institutional ceremony,
secondary adjustment, and unofficial “underlife” can help distinguish formal
discipline from the peer culture created inside it.

The case must not claim that Goffman studied this lyceum or equate the school
with a prison or psychiatric hospital. It also complicates the book's treatment
of community: intense solidarity and shared ritual can be produced by coercive
or highly controlled conditions. Density is not evidence of voluntariness or
health.

## Required work

1. Recover concrete scenes: first uniform, first formation, first duty,
   inspection day, hidden peer practice, and the move from Boyarka to Kyiv.
2. Verify the exact historical status of the Boyarka site and 2000–2002
   schedule.
3. Treat “children of the forest” or “forest brothers” as oral memory until
   other graduates or contemporary material confirm it.
4. Identify one cost and one benefit of the imposed solidarity.
