# Schein — Process Consultation and Humble Inquiry

## Priority given to primary text / official data

Primary: Schein's own two books, both consulted directly for their central
distinctions (expert vs. process model; the definition of Humble Inquiry). The DEC
consulting relationship (years, outcome) is independently, publicly documented and was
cross-checked via WebSearch rather than taken only from Schein's own account.

## Specific pages/fragments supporting the manuscript's claim

- Expert vs. process model distinction: core argument of *Process Consultation*
  throughout, most concisely stated in the book's own framing of "the process
  consultant's role is to help the client to perceive, understand, and act upon...
  events" rather than supply a diagnosis.
- Humble Inquiry definition: publisher/author framing, quoted above.
- DEC: 26-year relationship (1966-1992), widely reported detail of Schein's career;
  DEC's 1998 acquisition by Compaq is separately, uncontroversially documented.

## Why this source deserves a place in the book

It supplies the book's central vocabulary for the difference between diagnosing *for*
someone and diagnosing *with* someone, at exactly the two points (Diagnose, Advise)
where the manuscript needs a load-bearing distinction rather than a generic
"listening is good" gesture.

## What is original in it

The expert/process distinction and the Humble Inquiry practice are both Schein's own
coinages, not restatements of prior work.

## Limitations

See above - practitioner framework, not an empirical study; DEC's fate is not
evidence either way about the framework's effectiveness.

## Competing interpretation

An expert-model critic could argue that clients in genuine crises need decisive
expert direction, not open-ended inquiry, and that Schein's own framework may
underweight situations where time pressure makes process consultation impractical.
The manuscript's Chapter 20 material (Bombom) implicitly tests this by showing the
author *not* switching to an expert stance even under time pressure.

## Permissible level of claim

"Schein distinguishes X from Y, and personally consulted to DEC for 26 years" - both
fully supportable. "Process consultation works better than expert consulting" -
NOT supportable from this source alone.

## Forbidden overgeneralization

Do not use DEC's eventual fate as evidence that process consultation failed, or that
it would have succeeded had DEC used a different consulting style - no such
counterfactual exists in the record. The manuscript already avoids this; flagging
here so it is not reintroduced.
