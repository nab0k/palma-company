# Weick — Mann Gulch and the collapse of sensemaking

## Priority given to primary text / official data

Primary journal article (Administrative Science Quarterly, 1993), one of
organizational theory's most-cited papers; core claims consistent across every
secondary description checked.

## Specific fragments supporting the claim

The quoted passage above (role differentiation, communication, mutual respect
persisting past the plan) is the article's central mechanism claim, directly
supporting the manuscript's list of what held the TAK.Shtab convoy crew together
(defined roles, an internalized sense of what an absent colleague would do, ordinary
courtesy continuing under stress).

## Why this source deserves a place in the book

It gives Chapter 19 a rigorous, widely-respected theoretical account of exactly the
phenomenon the chapter's central scene depicts - a group facing something no plan
anticipated, staying coherent anyway.

## What is original in it

Weick's sensemaking-collapse framework, built specifically from this case, is his own
original theoretical contribution and one of the founding texts of "sensemaking" as a
subfield of organizational studies.

## Limitations / competing interpretation

Single extreme case (fatal wildfire); applying it to a non-fatal humanitarian-convoy
near-miss is an analogy the manuscript makes explicitly, not a second instance of
Weick's own data.

## Permissible level of claim

"Weick's framework, developed from Mann Gulch, identifies what tends to hold a small
group together under conditions the plan didn't anticipate" - supportable as a
theoretical lens applied to a different case. "This proves the same mechanism
operated in the TAK.Shtab convoy" - overclaims; the manuscript should present it (and
does) as an illuminating parallel, not a proven causal mechanism in the second case.

## Forbidden overgeneralization

Do not imply the Mann Gulch crew's fate (13 of 15 died) is comparable in severity or
outcome to the TAK.Shtab convoy scene (no injuries) - the manuscript already avoids
this by using Weick's framework only for the *mechanism* (what holds a group
together), not as a comparison of stakes or outcomes.
