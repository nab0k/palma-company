## What the account contributes

Where Francisco's interview (SC-095) left two of this project's core
hypotheses genuinely unresolved - is weak connectivity structural or
dispositional, and does founder-absence actually change how connected a
member feels - this interview was designed specifically to re-run both
without repeating the methodological mistakes that weakened Francisco's
data (supplying a frame before the participant answered; accepting a
one-word answer without follow-up).

Both fixes worked. The participant's account of *why* gym co-presence
licenses acknowledging a stranger, where an equally repeated coffee-shop
encounter does not, is entirely her own construction, volunteered without
prompting: shared training creates "a different context" than shared
space alone. This is a candidate mechanism claim this project did not have
before, in the participant's own words rather than confirmed under a
supplied frame.

The founder-dependency question also produced a real result this time,
because the interviewer followed up rather than accepting a bare "yes" or
"no": she would miss Miguel, but the workouts and other relationships would
remain. Read against Rami's middle position (SC-096) and Francisco's flat
disconfirmation (SC-095), this project now has three genuinely different
per-participant answers to the same counterfactual - evidence that
founder-centrality should be treated as a spectrum measured per
relationship, not a single property of the organization.

Her operational "who would you message" test - 14 to 16 named people,
counted live in the interview - is the most densely connected result of
any TCF member interviewed so far, and directly complicates treating her
as a "weakly connected member" case in the same category as Francisco, even
though both were recruited on that basis.

The arrival story is also useful outside the community chapters: her
pre-signup research on Miguel (professional background, a personal
reference from someone who had trained under him before) after ten loyal
years elsewhere is a clean, ordinary-member illustration of Chapter 3's
reputation-precedes-access sequence, distinct from the founder's own
account of the same mechanic.
