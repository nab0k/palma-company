# Vembu / Zoho — distributed succession case

## Priority given to primary text / official data

Business-press reporting at the time of the event (January 2025); no primary company
filing was separately accessed (Zoho is privately held, so no SEC-style disclosure
exists), but the transition was publicly announced and widely, consistently reported.

## Specific fragments supporting the claim

28-year tenure without external investment; January 2025 CEO-to-Chief-Scientist
transition; distributed (not single-successor) leadership restructuring — consistent
across sources checked.

## Why this source deserves a place in the book

It closes Chapter 21 with a large-scale, very recent, real example of exactly the
distinction the chapter draws: handover as testing whether an organization can hold on
many people's capability, not finding one replacement founder.

## What is original in it

Not original theory - a real, current business event used as illustration.

## Limitations / competing interpretation

Too recent to know whether the distributed structure endures; should be presented as
a choice made, not a proven outcome.

## Permissible level of claim

"Vembu chose a distributed rather than single-successor handover in January 2025" -
supportable. "This proves distributed succession works better" - not supportable;
too early to know, and the manuscript does not make this claim.

## Forbidden overgeneralization

Do not present Zoho's transition as a completed success story - it is a documented
choice at the moment of transition, and its outcome is unknown as of this writing.
