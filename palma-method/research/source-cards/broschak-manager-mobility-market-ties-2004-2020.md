core_claim: >
  When a boundary-spanning manager (e.g., an advertising account manager) leaves a firm, the
  market tie between that firm and the manager's clients becomes significantly more likely to
  dissolve - but this effect is role-specific, not universal: departures of "technical core"
  managers without the same boundary-spanning client relationship do not show the same effect. A
  2020 follow-up (Broschak, Block, Koppman & Adjerid) extends this to show that managerial
  migration and client-tie circulation between firms are related over time, indicating
  relationship-specific resources can be portable across firm boundaries.
summary: >
  Longitudinal observational study of advertising agencies and their clients (1986-1998 for the
  2004 paper). The precise, verified finding is narrower and more useful than a blanket "manager
  exits hurt client relationships" claim: it is specifically account managers - who hold the
  client-facing, boundary-spanning role - whose departure predicts tie dissolution; creative
  managers, who occupy the agency's technical core without the same client-facing relationship, do
  not show the same effect. The 2020 companion paper (different data: New York City advertising
  agencies) shows the same portable-relationship logic operating over time as managers migrate
  between firms.
