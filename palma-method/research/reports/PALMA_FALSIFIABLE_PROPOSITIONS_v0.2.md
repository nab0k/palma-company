## Section B — three new propositions from this task's sweep

### Proposition 7 — Transactive-memory concentration predicts disruption on departure

- **Grounded in:** Wegner (1987, SC-128).
- **Statement:** In a group with an unevenly distributed transactive-memory
  index (a small number of members disproportionately known as "who to ask"
  across multiple domains), that group's task/coordination continuity will
  be more disrupted when a high-index member departs than when a
  low-index member departs, controlling for the departing member's direct
  task load.
- **Unit of analysis:** the group (e.g., a TAK.Shtab team, a TCF cohort),
  observed across member departures.
- **Independent variable:** the departing member's estimated share of the
  group's transactive-memory index (measured via a "who would you ask
  about X" survey administered before departure).
- **Dependent variable:** post-departure disruption, operationalized as
  time-to-resolution for tasks previously routed through the departed
  member, and self-reported "didn't know who to ask" incidents in the
  following period.
- **Observable signals:** a spike in "does anyone know how/who" questions
  in group communication channels immediately after a high-index departure;
  no comparable spike after a low-index departure.
- **Rival explanations:** the departing member's direct task-execution
  skill (not their knowledge-routing role) explains the disruption;
  general group size or workload at the time of departure is a confound.
- **What would refute it:** if disruption is statistically unrelated to
  pre-departure index share once direct task load is controlled for.
- **Research design needed:** a prospective, multi-group study with a
  pre-departure transactive-memory survey and post-departure disruption
  tracking - not retrospective reconstruction, which the exploratory TCF
  coding pass (TASK_CLAUDE_0026) already showed is hard to code cleanly
  after the fact.

### Proposition 8 — Founding-era structural choices persist independent of current leadership's stated intent

- **Grounded in:** Stinchcombe (1965, SC-129).
- **Statement:** A group's current structural features (who holds decision
  rights, how roles are assigned, what gets formally recorded) correlate
  more strongly with the structural choices made at the group's founding
  than with its current leadership's stated intentions for how it should
  operate.
- **Unit of analysis:** the organization or group, compared at founding and
  at a later time point.
- **Independent variable:** founding-era structural choices (coded from
  early documents, early role assignments, or founder interviews).
- **Dependent variable:** current structural features, coded the same way,
  at a later time point (e.g., after a leadership transition).
- **Observable signals:** decision rights or role-assignment patterns that
  persist despite an explicit, documented current-leadership intention to
  change them.
- **Rival explanations:** external environmental constraints (not founding
  inertia) explain the persistence; the group has genuinely changed but the
  coding instrument fails to detect it.
- **What would refute it:** if current structural features correlate more
  strongly with current leadership's stated intent than with founding-era
  choices, once environmental constraints are controlled for.
- **Research design needed:** a comparative case study design across
  multiple groups at varying distances from their founding, or a single
  longitudinal case tracked through an actual leadership transition (TAK.
  Shtab, given its founder-role change already described in the manuscript,
  is a plausible single-case candidate, though a single case cannot
  establish a general pattern).

### Proposition 9 — Experiential-knowledge peer ties predict unprompted peer-initiated help, beyond general cohesion

- **Grounded in:** Borkman (1976, SC-135).
- **Statement:** Members who have an experiential-knowledge tie to a peer
  (i.e., have personally lived through a problem or transition the peer is
  currently facing) are more likely to unprompted-initiate help toward
  that peer than members without such a tie, even after controlling for
  general group cohesion or tenure.
- **Unit of analysis:** the dyad, within a group (e.g., TCF members, TAK.
  Shtab volunteers).
- **Independent variable:** presence and strength of an experiential-
  knowledge match between two members (self-reported: "have you personally
  been through what they're currently facing").
- **Dependent variable:** frequency of unprompted, peer-initiated help
  directed at that specific peer (as opposed to generic group-directed
  helpfulness).
- **Observable signals:** help offered without being asked, specifically
  referencing the helper's own past experience with the same situation.
- **Rival explanations:** general cohesion or friendship strength (not
  the specific experiential match) explains the helping behavior; simple
  proximity/availability explains who helps whom.
- **What would refute it:** if unprompted help rates show no relationship
  to experiential-knowledge matching once general cohesion/tenure and
  proximity are controlled for.
- **Research design needed:** a survey-plus-behavioral-observation design
  within an existing group (TCF is a plausible candidate given its
  existing interview base, SC-095 through SC-099 and later), asking members
  to self-report experiential matches and then tracking or self-reporting
  subsequent unprompted-help incidents over a defined period.

## What none of these nine propositions currently support

None of the nine propositions in this document, in either version, has
been tested by a study meeting this project's own stated bar (pre-
registered, adequately powered, with a comparison condition). The single
exploratory coding pass reported in v0.1's addendum remains the only
empirical work attempted on any of them, and it was explicitly a five-case,
single-coder, non-random, descriptive pass on Proposition 1 only. This
document does not change that status for any proposition, including the
three new ones added here - it makes them precise enough that they could,
in principle, be tested, which is the specific bar the founder's task
specification asked this document to clear.
