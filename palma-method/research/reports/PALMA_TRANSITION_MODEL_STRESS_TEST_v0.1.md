# Palma Transition Model — Stress Test v0.1

Task: TASK_CLAUDE_0025
Owner: Claude
Status: draft for editorial review (Codex / Serhii)

Candidate proposition under test: "Repeated encounters become durable relational
infrastructure when participants begin initiating consequential actions for and
with one another, and those actions create routes for further interaction that
do not need to be recreated or initiated by the original organizer."

Candidate transition model under test: repeated encounters → mutual recognition
and shared memory → participant-initiated reciprocal action → routes of
continuation → distributed relational infrastructure → readiness to respond.

Treated throughout as a candidate, not an approved finding, per the requester's
own framing.

---

## Part 1 — Attempting to falsify the integrated-model novelty claim

This project's existing prior-art work (`PALMA_METHOD_PRIOR_ART_MATRIX_v0.1.md`)
already checked every individual component this candidate draws on - Feld's
foci, Collins's ritual chains, Wenger's communities of practice, social/
relational capital theory, network formation, institutionalization - and found
each to be established, correctly cited, non-novel work. That is not
re-litigated here. What was not yet checked is whether the *specific integrated
six-step causal sequence*, as a whole, already exists somewhere as one named
model. Three genuine candidate equivalents were searched for and checked
against the full sequence, not just a single step:

### Candidate equivalent 1 — Wenger, McDermott & Snyder (2002), community-of-practice lifecycle stages

**The model:** potential → coalescing → maturing → stewardship →
transformation, describing how a community of practice develops from a latent
shared interest through active community-building to a mature, self-sustaining
practice.

**Where it overlaps:** this is the closest match in *form* found in this
search - a staged model describing a community moving from convener-initiated
formation toward a more autonomous, sustained state, ending (in "stewardship")
with the community actively maintaining its own relevance rather than depending
on its original organizers.

**Where it differs, specifically:** the WMS model describes the community's
condition at each stage (energy level, clarity of boundaries, sustained
relevance) but does not isolate, as its central causal mechanism, whether
continuation specifically requires the convener's re-initiation or not. A
community could reach "stewardship" in the WMS model while every activity is
still convened by the same person - the model does not make organizer-
independence the operational test the way the Palma candidate does. It also
operates entirely at the community level; it does not specify a dyadic-level
mechanism (a "Palma Moment," a specific counter-action) as the unit that
accumulates into the community-level outcome.

**Verdict:** a genuine, close nearest-neighbor in structure, not an equivalent
in content. **Novelty claim survives this comparison, but only for the specific
dyadic-mechanism-to-organizer-independence pivot the WMS model doesn't make.**

### Candidate equivalent 2 — Knapp (1978), relational stages ("coming together": initiating, experimenting, intensifying, integrating, bonding)

**The model:** a staged escalation model of how two people move from first
contact to a bonded, exclusive relationship, through progressively more
intimate and self-disclosing communication.

**Where it overlaps:** the general "staged transition" form, and the intuition
that repeated interaction changes what participants expect from each other -
close to the Palma candidate's "mutual recognition and shared memory" step.

**Where it differs, specifically:** Knapp's model is dyadic and domain-specific
to close/romantic relationship formation; it says nothing about group-level
infrastructure, convener dependence, or a transition to distributed,
organizer-independent routes of continuation - the entire second half of the
Palma candidate's chain (routes of continuation, distributed infrastructure)
has no counterpart in Knapp's model at all.

**Verdict:** relevant only to the candidate's early steps, and only as a
structural analogy from an unrelated domain. **Does not weaken the novelty
claim for the model as a whole.**

### Candidate equivalent 3 — Community-organizing practice tradition (mobilization → organization development → campaign implementation → consolidation → turnover/phase-out)

**The model:** a practitioner-tradition staged process (distinct from academic
social-capital theory) describing how an organizer builds a group's capacity to
act and eventually hands leadership over to community members.

**Where it overlaps:** this is the closest match found for the *end* of the
Palma chain specifically - "turnover and phase-out" is conceptually close to
"distributed relational infrastructure" and "readiness to respond" (the
capacity being handed over is, in effect, readiness to act together).

**Where it differs, specifically:** this tradition is built around deliberate,
issue-based campaigns with formal leadership-development training - it assumes
an explicit organizing intent from the start, whereas the Palma candidate is
built to explain infrastructure that can emerge from *any* repeated encounter,
including ones (like a gym class or a neighborhood café) with no organizing
intent at all. It also has no dyadic-level mechanism comparable to a Palma
Moment - the unit of analysis throughout is the campaign and the group, not a
specific two-person exchange.

**Verdict:** a genuine nearest-neighbor for the outcome end of the chain, from
a different professional tradition than the rest of the candidate's grounding.
**Does not weaken the novelty claim; if anything, strengthens the case that
Palma's chain deliberately spans two literatures (dyadic interaction-ritual
mechanics and organizer-succession practice) that are not normally integrated
into one model.**

### Conclusion of the falsification attempt

**No equivalent integrated model was found.** All three candidates checked are
genuine nearest-neighbors, each matching part of the chain closely, and none
matching the whole. This is reported as a negative-search finding, not proof of
worldwide originality - a reasonable, bounded search found no match, which is
the standard this task itself sets. **The novelty claim for the integrated
six-step sequence, as a specific combination, is not falsified by this
search and may proceed to formalization** - but see Part 4 below for exactly
how narrow the surviving claim actually is once stated precisely.

## Part 2 — What, precisely, the model adds beyond its eight named component literatures

The requester asked this directly: does the model add anything beyond Feld,
Collins, Wenger, social/relational capital theory, collective efficacy, network
formation/interventions, community organizing, and institutionalization/
succession? Checked one at a time:

- **Beyond Feld:** yes - Feld explains why repeated co-presence happens; it
  says nothing about what happens after co-presence, which is the entire
  subject of the Palma chain's remaining five steps.
- **Beyond Collins:** yes - Collins explains why a *single* ritual succeeds or
  fails and produces emotional energy; the Palma chain's specific claim is
  about what turns a *series* of individually successful (or even average)
  interactions into infrastructure that survives the convener's absence -
  Collins's own ritual-chain concept describes repetition building emotional
  energy over time, but does not address organizer-independence at all.
- **Beyond Wenger:** yes, per Part 1 above - Wenger (and WMS 2002) describe
  community-level maturation without making organizer-independence the
  operational pivot.
- **Beyond social/relational capital theory:** yes - this literature explains
  what accumulates (trust, reciprocity, etc.) and, in places, that it can be
  institutionalized (Selznick), but does not specify the dyadic mechanism (a
  consequential participant-initiated action) or the specific test (a route
  that does not require the organizer to recreate it) this model proposes as
  the marker of the transition.
- **Beyond collective efficacy (Sampson, Raudenbush & Earls):** yes - collective
  efficacy is a measured *outcome* (a neighborhood's demonstrated capacity to
  act together); it is not itself a model of how a group gets there through a
  specific sequence of dyadic events, and the manuscript's own use of this
  source (Chapter 16) already states this distinction directly.
- **Beyond network formation and network interventions (Valente-family
  literature, not independently source-carded in this project's corpus - a
  genuine, not-yet-closed gap flagged here as it was in
  `PALMA_METHOD_PRIOR_ART_MATRIX_v0.1.md`):** likely yes, though this
  comparison is weaker than the others because the underlying literature was
  not independently searched in this task; network-intervention research
  typically targets deliberate structural change (adding/removing ties for a
  behavior-change goal), not organic transition from convener-mediated to
  peer-mediated ties, but this should be treated as a provisional finding
  pending the closure of the gap already flagged.
- **Beyond community organizing:** yes, per Part 1 above - the organizing
  tradition assumes deliberate organizing intent from the outset; the Palma
  chain is built to apply to encounters with no such intent.
- **Beyond institutionalization/founder-succession literature:** yes -
  Selznick's institutionalization theory and general succession-planning
  practice (the latter also flagged as an under-researched gap in
  `PALMA_METHOD_PRIOR_ART_MATRIX_v0.1.md`) describe the *organizational* end
  state; neither specifies the dyadic-level generative mechanism the Palma
  chain proposes as the process that produces that end state.

**The one precise claim this comparison supports:** the model's original
contribution is not any single step, but **the specific causal linkage between
a dyadic-level mechanism (a consequential, participant-initiated action) and a
group/organizational-level outcome (distributed infrastructure, defined
specifically as routes that do not require the convener to recreate them)**,
integrated across literatures that do not currently cross-reference each other
in this project's search. This is a real, narrow, defensible claim - not "Palma
discovered that relationships matter," and not even "Palma discovered a new
stage model of community development," but specifically: naming the
organizer-independence test as the operational marker of the transition, and
proposing a dyadic mechanism (not just a community-level condition) that
produces it.

## Part 3 — Formal Palma Transition Model specification

### Constructs

1. **Repeated encounter** - a bounded occasion of co-presence recurring on some
   schedule, organized around a shared focus (per Feld).
2. **Mutual recognition and shared memory** - the point at which participants
   in a repeated encounter can identify each other as specific, known
   individuals (not merely "someone who was also there") and recall specific
   prior interactions with each other, independent of the organizer's
   introduction.
3. **Participant-initiated reciprocal action** - a consequential action one
   participant takes toward another, not scripted or requested by the
   organizer, that the other participant responds to in a way that changes
   what either side expects from the next interaction (this is the manuscript
   and tool-spec's own "Palma Moment," reused here as this construct's
   operational definition).
4. **Route of continuation** - a specific, nameable channel or occasion through
   which two or more participants can interact again without the organizer
   re-initiating, scheduling, or brokering that specific instance.
5. **Distributed relational infrastructure** - a state in which multiple
   routes of continuation exist among multiple participant pairs or subgroups,
   such that a significant share of the system's total interaction does not
   require the organizer's direct involvement.
6. **Readiness to respond** - the book's own central outcome variable
   (Introduction, lines 31-33): the demonstrated willingness of participants to
   answer, return, help, warn, introduce, or act together when something is
   actually asked of them.

### Boundaries between constructs

- *Repeated encounter* is necessary for *mutual recognition* to plausibly form
  through the mechanism this model proposes, but the two are not the same
  thing - repeated encounters can occur (per Chapter 11's own example, the
  colleague who trained for months without becoming known to others) without
  mutual recognition forming.
- *Mutual recognition* is necessary but not sufficient for *participant-
  initiated reciprocal action* - two people can recognize and remember each
  other without either ever taking a consequential, unscripted action toward
  the other.
- *Participant-initiated reciprocal action* (a single event, a Palma Moment) is
  distinct from a *route of continuation* (a durable channel) - one successful
  reciprocal action does not by itself create a standing route; it is evidence
  the route is *possible*, per the tool-spec's own existing caution that "one
  good exchange demonstrates the mechanism is possible, not that it will
  recur."
- *Distributed relational infrastructure* is a portfolio-level property (many
  routes, many pairs) and is explicitly not reducible to any single route,
  consistent with DECISION_0003's dyadic/portfolio distinction.
- *Readiness to respond* is the outcome the whole chain is proposed to predict
  - it is conceptually and observably distinct from *distributed
  infrastructure* itself (infrastructure is a structural fact about the
  network; readiness is a behavioral disposition observed only when something
  is actually asked).

### Unit of analysis

Two units operate simultaneously and must not be collapsed: the **dyad** (for
constructs 2-3, mutual recognition and reciprocal action) and the **system or
portfolio** (for constructs 4-6, routes, infrastructure, and readiness). The
model's specific claim is that dyadic-level events (construct 3) aggregate,
across many dyads, into a system-level property (constructs 5-6) - a
cross-level claim that itself needs to be tested, not assumed.

### Causal sequence (as proposed, not established)

Repeated encounter → [necessary but not sufficient] → mutual recognition and
shared memory → [necessary but not sufficient] → participant-initiated
reciprocal action (a Palma Moment) → [necessary but not sufficient, and only
if the action's specific form creates a durable channel] → route of
continuation → [aggregated across multiple dyads/routes] → distributed
relational infrastructure → [observed only under an actual demand/request] →
readiness to respond.

### Necessary vs. facilitating conditions

- **Necessary:** repeated co-presence organized around a shared focus (without
  it, mutual recognition has no basis to form through this specific mechanism);
  at least one participant-initiated reciprocal action per route claimed (a
  route cannot be said to exist without at least one instance of use
  independent of the organizer).
- **Facilitating, not necessary:** emotional intensity or ritual success
  (Collins's mechanism) - the model does not claim high emotional energy is
  required, only that it plausibly raises the odds of a qualifying reciprocal
  action occurring; group size, physical co-location, and formal roles are
  similarly facilitating, not necessary, per the manuscript's own Chapter 13
  discussion of channels/roles/rhythms as levers that raise but do not
  guarantee the outcome.

### Boundary conditions

- The model is proposed to apply to bounded, recurring social systems (a class,
  a workplace team, a neighborhood, a client community) - it is not proposed to
  apply to one-off encounters or to large, low-repetition audiences (e.g., a
  newsletter list), which the manuscript's own Chapters 1-2 already treat as a
  structurally different case.
- The model does not specify a time window - how long the chain takes to
  complete, or whether it must complete within any particular period, is left
  open and should be treated as a further boundary condition requiring
  empirical work, not assumed.
- The model does not claim every repeated encounter should or will complete
  the chain - per the manuscript's own Chapter 16 caution (line 549), stopping
  at an earlier link (a well-functioning audience, a temporary group) is
  explicitly not framed as a failure case.

### Alternative explanations

- **Selection, not transformation:** participants who were already inclined to
  initiate reciprocal action with strangers may simply be more likely to join
  and remain in recurring-encounter systems in the first place - the chain
  could describe a selection effect (who stays) rather than a transformation
  effect (what staying does to people).
- **Confounded by system age/size** (already named in
  `PALMA_METHOD_FALSIFIABLE_PROPOSITIONS_v0.1.md`, Proposition 5): older,
  larger systems could show more routes of continuation and more readiness to
  respond simply because they've had more time and more people, independent of
  whether the specific causal mechanism proposed here is operating.
- **Reverse causation at the readiness-to-respond stage:** systems where
  participants already had higher baseline readiness to respond (for reasons
  unrelated to this specific chain - e.g., shared prior community membership,
  as in the TCF "foreigners in a new city" subgroup) may be more likely to
  generate the earlier steps of the chain in the first place, rather than the
  chain producing the readiness.

### Observable indicators

- Repeated encounter: attendance/participation records showing recurrence.
- Mutual recognition: named recall of a specific other participant and at
  least one specific shared episode, elicited independently of the organizer's
  prompting.
- Reciprocal action: a specific, nameable action met by a specific, nameable
  counter-action, each independently confirmed by both parties where possible.
- Route of continuation: contact, coordination, or interaction between two
  participants occurring without the organizer's direct involvement in that
  specific instance.
- Distributed infrastructure: the proportion of a system's total observed
  interaction that occurs via such organizer-independent routes.
- Readiness to respond: an actual observed response to an actual request or
  need, not a stated intention or self-reported willingness.

### Counterexamples

- The manuscript's own material already contains at least one partial
  counterexample worth naming honestly: the TCF participant (Chapter 11,
  lines 367-372) who has attended for nearly six months, has repeated
  encounters and presumably some mutual recognition with Miguel, but reports
  no participant-initiated reciprocal action or route of continuation with
  anyone else - the chain, for this specific dyad-set, appears to have stalled
  at construct 2, which is consistent with the model's own "not every
  encounter completes the chain" boundary condition but is also exactly the
  kind of case a rigorous test needs to explain, not explain away.
- A hypothetical stronger counterexample the model should be tested against:
  a system with extensive participant-to-participant routes and infrastructure
  that nonetheless shows low readiness to respond when something is actually
  asked (e.g., a friendly, chatty group that does not show up for an actual
  request for help) - no such case currently exists in this project's material,
  and its absence should not be read as evidence it wouldn't occur.

### Falsifiable propositions

See Part 4 below for P1-P5 as specified by the requester; two further,
model-level propositions are added here since they test the *integration*
specifically, not any single step:

- **P6 (new, integration-level):** systems with a high rate of construct-3
  events (reciprocal actions) but few resulting construct-4 routes (i.e.,
  reciprocal actions that don't turn into standing channels) will show lower
  construct-5/6 outcomes (infrastructure, readiness) than systems with fewer
  but more route-generating reciprocal actions - this would falsify a naive
  "more Palma Moments is always better" reading and support the model's more
  precise claim that the *route-forming* quality of a reciprocal action matters,
  not just its frequency.
- **P7 (new, integration-level):** the model predicts that distributed
  infrastructure (construct 5) mediates the relationship between reciprocal
  action (construct 3) and readiness to respond (construct 6) - i.e.,
  reciprocal action alone, without resulting infrastructure, should not predict
  readiness as strongly as reciprocal action that does produce infrastructure.
  Evidence that reciprocal-action frequency alone (regardless of resulting
  infrastructure) predicts readiness equally well would weaken the model's
  specific claim that infrastructure is the necessary intervening step, not
  just a byproduct.

## Part 4 — P1 through P5, tested individually

### P1. Participant-initiated actions outside the organizer's script predict persistence better than attendance frequency, satisfaction, or emotional intensity alone.

- **Established/synthesis/new:** genuinely new but untested - no source in this
  project's corpus makes this specific comparative claim (that unscripted
  participant action outpredicts the three named alternatives). It is
  consistent with, but not established by, Collins's emotional-energy
  mechanism or Feld's opportunity-allocation mechanism.
- **What would disprove it:** a finding that attendance frequency alone
  predicts persistence at least as well as the presence of unscripted
  participant-initiated action, once both are measured for the same cases.
- **TCF status:** illustrate only. The current interviews (SC-095, SC-096,
  SC-097) show participant-initiated action co-occurring with continued
  attendance in a handful of cases, but no comparison against attendance-only
  or satisfaction-only cases has been constructed - there is no control group
  in the existing material.
- **Additional data needed:** a dataset tracking, for the same set of
  participants, attendance frequency, a satisfaction or emotional-intensity
  measure, presence/absence of unscripted participant-initiated action, and a
  later persistence outcome (continued attendance, or continued relationship
  after attendance stops).

### P2. An encounter produces a Palma Moment only when one party's action elicits consequential action by another party and changes the probability or available route of future interaction.

- **Established/synthesis/new:** this is a definitional restatement of the
  Palma Moment construct itself (already specified in
  `PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_v0.2.md`), not an independently falsifiable
  empirical claim as worded - it defines the construct rather than predicting
  an external outcome. To make it testable, it needs to be read as a
  measurement-validity claim: that trained observers can reliably distinguish
  a qualifying Palma Moment from a non-qualifying pleasant exchange.
- **What would disprove the testable (measurement) version:** low
  inter-rater agreement among independent observers coding the same
  interactions for whether a Palma Moment occurred.
- **TCF status:** illustrate only - the co-signing (SC-097) and microphone/
  itinerary (SC-095) episodes are single-rater (the interviewer's own)
  classifications; no second, independent rater has coded these or any other
  episode.
- **Additional data needed:** at least two independent raters coding the same
  set of observed or reported interactions against the Palma Moment
  definition, with agreement statistics computed before any substantive claim
  about frequency or predictive value is made.

### P3. A relationship begins to outlive its original social focus when participants create an independent route of continuation.

- **Established/synthesis/new:** synthesis - this restates, in the model's own
  terms, a distinction already implicit in the manuscript's own TCF material
  (Chapter 9, lines 306-308: some TCF acquaintances "continued without needing
  him [Miguel] as an intermediary"). The general intuition (a relationship
  outlives its original context when it becomes independently self-sustaining)
  is not new; naming "route of continuation" as the specific, checkable marker
  is this model's contribution.
- **What would disprove it:** relationships that show no identifiable
  independent route of continuation but nonetheless persist after the original
  focus ends (e.g., through pure chance re-encounter, which would suggest
  persistence doesn't require a *created* route).
- **TCF status:** can explore, closer to testing than P1/P2 - Chapter 9 already
  documents at least one specific case (a former TCF regular who moved away
  and later reconnected with another member outside the gym) that plausibly
  meets this proposition's definition, though it remains a single, retrospectively-
  identified case, not a systematically tracked cohort.
- **Additional data needed:** prospective tracking of a cohort of participants
  as their original focus (e.g., the gym) becomes less available to them
  (relocation, schedule change), recording whether an independent route
  existed beforehand and whether the relationship persisted afterward.

### P4. A relational system becomes more transferable when routes, roles, rhythms, channels and memory are distributed among participants rather than concentrated in the organizer.

- **Established/synthesis/new:** evidence-informed synthesis - directly
  consistent with the network-science robustness/dependency literature already
  well-cited in this project (Barabási-family research, Chapter 9's "test of
  removal"), extended here specifically to Chapter 13's five/six design
  elements. The general network-science claim (distributed structure survives
  node loss better than centralized structure) is established for large
  networks; its application to this specific five/six-element inventory at
  organizational scale is the untested extension.
- **What would disprove it:** a system with highly distributed routes/roles/
  rhythms/channels/memory that nonetheless fails to transfer (collapses or
  reverts to founder-dependency) when the organizer steps back, at a rate no
  better than concentrated systems.
- **TCF status:** illustrate only - no organizer-absence period has been
  observed for TCF (see `PALMA_METHOD_FALSIFIABLE_PROPOSITIONS_v0.1.md`,
  Proposition 5, which covers closely related ground). TAK.Shtab's evolution
  is a stronger single-case illustration but not a comparative test.
- **Additional data needed:** the same comparative, multi-organization
  longitudinal design named in `PALMA_METHOD_FALSIFIABLE_PROPOSITIONS_v0.1.md`.

### P5. Dense participant-to-organizer relationships can coexist with weak participant-to-participant infrastructure; high engagement with the organizer does not by itself demonstrate a self-sustaining network.

- **Established/synthesis/new:** established, at the level of general
  principle - this is a direct, correctly-scoped restatement of the
  hub-dependency finding already well-documented in this project (Chapter 9's
  entire argument, Bombom's own case in Chapter 8-10 where warm owner-to-
  customer relationships coexisted with largely undocumented customer-to-
  customer ties). This is the one of the five propositions closest to already
  being demonstrated, not merely hypothesized, within this project's own
  existing case material.
- **What would disprove it:** a finding that dense organizer-relationships
  reliably predict dense participant-to-participant infrastructure too (i.e.,
  that the two are not actually separable in practice, only in theory).
- **TCF status:** can explore, closest of the five to being testable with
  existing material - Bombom's case (Chapter 8-11) already documents exactly
  this pattern (warm owner ties, largely unknown customer-to-customer ties)
  as a real, if single, instance. TCF's low-regularity participant (SC-099)
  is a second, independent instance of the same pattern (some organizer
  contact, zero new peer contact).
- **Additional data needed:** systematic measurement, across multiple
  participants in the same system, of both organizer-tie strength and peer-tie
  count/strength, to establish the actual (not merely illustrated)
  correlation - or lack of one - between the two.

## Part 5 — Scientific contribution package

### Position recommendation, weighed against all four options

1. **No meaningful novelty** - rejected. Part 1's falsification attempt found
   no equivalent integrated model after checking three genuine candidates in
   three different traditions. Rejecting novelty entirely would require either
   not having searched (which this task did) or dismissing the specific
   dyadic-to-system causal linkage as trivial, which the comparison in Part 2
   does not support.
2. **Practitioner novelty only** - rejected, but only barely, and this is the
   most serious competing position. A practitioner-novelty verdict would say:
   this is a useful synthesis for consultants, with no claim to add anything a
   researcher would recognize as a new theoretical proposition. This is
   close to correct for most of the *individual* Palma tools (see the prior
   audit's classification of the five tools as practitioner heuristics). It
   undersells this specific model, though, because P6 and P7 (Part 3) are
   genuinely falsifiable, non-trivial empirical claims about mediation and
   about which *kind* of reciprocal action matters - these are the kind of
   propositions a researcher, not only a practitioner, could take up.
3. **Defensible conceptual novelty with untested propositions** - **selected.**
   This matches the evidence precisely: a real, narrow, falsifiable
   integration (Part 2's finding) exists; none of it has been tested against
   real data in a way that would support a stronger position; every
   proposition in Part 4 is currently at "illustrate" or "explore" status, not
   "test" status, against this project's own existing material.
4. **Preliminary empirical contribution** - rejected, and rejected clearly, not
   narrowly. This position would require some actual data analysis - even a
   small, informal one - to have been performed against the model's
   propositions. None has. Every TCF/TAK.Shtab/Bombom reference in this
   document and in `PALMA_METHOD_FALSIFIABLE_PROPOSITIONS_v0.1.md` is a
   single-interview illustration, not a coded dataset, a comparison group, or
   an inter-rater-checked measurement. Claiming "preliminary empirical
   contribution" status would be the single most likely overclaim this entire
   audit exists to prevent.

**Selected position: 3 - defensible conceptual novelty with untested
propositions.**

### Draft language

## Part 6 — Minimal preregistration protocol for testing this model against TCF data

Specified in advance, before any new examination of TCF data beyond what
existing, already-dated source cards (SC-095 through SC-099) document. This
protocol governs any *future* coding pass, not a reanalysis of impressions
already formed.

**Hypotheses to be tested (pre-specified):**
- H1 (= P1, restated for coding): interviews reporting an unscripted,
  participant-initiated action will also report continued attendance or
  continued relationship at a higher rate than interviews reporting no such
  action.
- H2 (= P5, restated for coding): interviews reporting strong organizer-
  contact will not systematically also report strong peer-contact - the two
  will show low correlation across interviews, not a strong positive one.

**Unit of analysis:** the individual interview (one participant's account),
not "TCF" as an aggregate - consistent with this project's own standing
discipline.

**Variables and coding rules, fixed in advance:**
- *Unscripted participant-initiated action* (binary): present if the
  interview describes a specific, named episode where the participant or
  another member took an action toward another member that was not requested
  or organized by Miguel/Margarida, and that a reasonable independent coder
  would judge as "consequential" (something with a real cost or commitment,
  e.g., lending an item, organizing a trip, co-signing a document) rather than
  purely social pleasantries (a greeting, small talk). Ambiguous cases are
  coded "unclear," not forced into present/absent.
- *Continued relationship* (binary): present if the interview describes
  contact, activity, or relationship with another member occurring outside
  scheduled class time and not brokered by Miguel/Margarida in that specific
  instance.
- *Organizer-contact strength* (three-point: low/medium/high): based on the
  participant's own described frequency and depth of interaction with
  Miguel/Margarida specifically.
- *Peer-contact strength* (three-point: low/medium/high): based on the
  participant's own described number and depth of relationships with other
  members, excluding Miguel/Margarida.

**Exclusions, fixed in advance:**
- Interviews conducted for a different purpose where the relevant questions
  were not asked at all are excluded from that specific variable's coding
  (not coded as "absent") - this applies to SC-099, which already documents
  effectively zero new gym-formed contacts but should be checked against
  whether the interview actually probed for unscripted action specifically,
  not just contact count.
- Any material flagged for privacy restriction in its own source card is
  excluded entirely from this protocol, per this project's standing privacy
  rules.

**Time period:** all currently-collected interviews as of this protocol's
date (2026-08-16) - SC-095 through SC-099. Any interview collected after this
date is treated as a separate, second wave, not folded into this analysis
without a new, dated protocol amendment.

**Alternative explanations to be checked, not assumed away:**
- Interview-order or interviewer-priming effects: whether earlier interviews'
  content shaped how later interviews were conducted or interpreted (a real
  risk given all five interviews were conducted by the same person who also
  built this protocol).
- Selection into being interviewed at all: whether the five interviewees
  differ systematically (e.g., more socially embedded, more willing to talk to
  the author) from TCF members who were not interviewed - this protocol cannot
  correct for this with only five cases, and any result should state this
  limitation explicitly rather than generalize to "TCF members" as a class,
  consistent with this project's standing discipline.

**What this protocol can and cannot deliver:** with five interviews, this
protocol can produce, at most, a descriptive tabulation (how many of five show
each pattern) - it cannot support a statistical test of H1 or H2, and no
p-value, correlation coefficient, or significance claim should be produced or
implied from it. Its value is narrowing exactly where the current material
does and does not already bear on the model, ahead of any decision to collect
more data.
