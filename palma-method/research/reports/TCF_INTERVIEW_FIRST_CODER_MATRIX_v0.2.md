## P9 coding memo

- `T1`: several relations are stronger than recognition, but their independence and
  continuation are uneven; no external messaging was documented for one prominent tie.
- `Q1/R1`: a peer noticed a physical problem and the participant later used her
  professional service. This is consequential action and acceptance, but may be a
  commercial service rather than mutual aid.
- `C=U`: the interaction continued beyond a scheduled class but apparently within or
  around the same facility. The codebook does not cleanly distinguish “outside class”
  from “outside place,” so forcing C1 or C2 would hide a construct problem.
- `I1`: the founder helped open at least one interaction, after which participants
  communicated directly. Mixed mediation is better supported than full independence.
- `D1`: the founder is described as central to tone and socializing, while a temporary
  absence is not expected to dismantle the existing infrastructure.
- `H1`: direct relational channels exist, but no participant-created group channel is
  established.
- `L1`: many regular morning faces remain unnamed.

## What v0.2 adds

1. Couple participation is treated as both a bridge and a possible insulator.
2. Merchandise feedback is kept outside peer reciprocity codes.
3. Operational routines, personal habits, interaction openings, and shared rituals
   are no longer treated as interchangeable.
4. P9 exposes a location ambiguity in `C`; the codebook should later split “outside
   scheduled activity” from “outside the physical place.”
5. The new case reinforces a graded founder-dependence model and the boundary between
   familiar faces and direct ties.

## Claims still prohibited

- TCF generally causes friendship, reciprocity, retention, or community.
- Merchandise input produces ownership, loyalty, or shared governance.
- A service relationship is automatically mutual aid.
- A founder-dependent opening proves the later tie remains founder-dependent.
- The Palma Method produced any observed outcome.
