## What is safely preserved

The repository now retains the interview date, source locator, narrative
mechanisms, candidate chapter uses, verification work, consent limits, and
prohibited material for each located source. Losing a chat or a Notion search
result should no longer erase the editorial knowledge extracted from these
three interviews and four located author cases.

Raw audio and transcripts remain private. This is deliberate: GitHub is the
canonical editorial record, but the repository is public and is not an archive
for confidential recordings or personal data.

## What the material can currently do in the book

- **Chapter 2:** Bombom distinguishes a warm customer network from a community;
  TCF provides candidate evidence of member-to-member ties and shared ritual.
- **Chapter 5:** TCF contrasts occasional events with infrastructure created by
  schedule, recurring classes, coffee, and recognition.
- **Chapters 6–7:** Bombom shows that different ties can supply different
  resources, and that rescue capital may create new obligations.
- **Chapter 10:** both cases can expose exclusion and concentration risks.
- **Chapters 12, 16–17:** the interviews can support diagnosis, mapping, and the
  distinction between communication activity and community infrastructure.
- **Prospective field note:** TCF can still become the forward-looking case if
  participant interviews, consent, one decision, and one bounded test are added.
