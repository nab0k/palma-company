## Section 1 — The one publication-blocker-level finding

### 1.1 The phase-architecture divergence (Chapters 17-21)

- **Chapter:** 17-21, and by extension any future statement of "the Method"
  anywhere in the book.
- **Exact current passage:** the chapter titles themselves - Глава 17. Диагноз;
  Глава 18. Дизайн; Глава 19. Запуск; Глава 20. Совет; Глава 21. Передача.
- **Problem:** the manuscript's own five-chapter sequence is currently
  compatible with two different, disagreeing external architectures - the
  Founder-approved DECISION_0005 (six phases; Chapter 20/Advise is a phase,
  Chapter 21/Handover is the cross-cutting principle) and the tool-spec drafts
  (four stages; Chapter 21/Transfer is a stage, Chapter 20/Facilitate is the
  cross-cutting principle). See
  `PALMA_METHOD_NOVELTY_AND_SCIENTIFIC_STATUS_AUDIT_v0.1.md`, section A.3, for
  the full analysis. As written, the manuscript itself does not resolve this,
  because it never names either architecture explicitly - it simply tells the
  five-chapter story. The risk is not in the current chapter text, which reads
  fine on its own terms; the risk is in any future summary, cover copy, or "how
  the Method works" statement that has to pick one architecture and currently
  has two disagreeing sources to pick from.
- **Proposed editorial action:** before any public-facing statement of "the six
  phases" or "the four stages" is written (cover copy, website, a methods
  appendix, or a rewritten introduction/conclusion), the Founder or Chief
  Editor needs to either (a) issue a new decision reconciling DECISION_0005's
  phase-to-chapter mapping with the tool-spec's stage/principle assignment, or
  (b) explicitly instruct that the tool-spec drafts be revised to match
  DECISION_0005 as written. This is not a manuscript edit and is out of this
  task's authority to perform - it is flagged as the one item in this whole
  audit that should block any confident public description of "the Method's
  architecture" until resolved.
- **Evidence/source-card basis:** DECISION_0005 (governance file); `PALMA_METHOD_
  TOOL_SPEC_v0.2.md`; direct chapter-by-chapter read of the snapshot.
- **Priority: publication blocker** (specifically for any *public description
  of the Method's own architecture* - not for the five existing chapters,
  which do not need to change).
- **Expected word-count effect:** zero in the current five chapters; effect on
  any new architecture-summary material is undetermined until the underlying
  decision is made.

## Section 2 — Important items

### 2.1 The Method's five tools have no manuscript presence except the Palma Moment

- **Chapter:** none currently - this is an absence, not a passage to fix.
- **Problem:** `PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_v0.2.md` fully specifies five tools
  (Relationship Capital Map, Infrastructure Canvas, Response Signals, Palma
  Moment, Handoff Test). Only the Palma Moment (Ch5, Ch14) currently appears in
  the manuscript by name. A reader of the finished book, as currently drafted,
  would never encounter "Response Signals" or "Handoff Test" at all, and would
  encounter the mapping and design-element ideas in different, differently-
  structured forms (Ch12's five maps; Ch13's six design elements - see 2.2 and
  2.3 below).
- **Proposed editorial action:** decide whether the five tools are meant to
  enter the manuscript as named, explicit tools (likely in Part IV, Chapters
  17-21, or in a dedicated appendix) or whether they remain editorial/consulting
  material outside the book's own prose. Either is defensible; leaving it
  undecided risks a mismatch between how "Palma Method" is described externally
  (via the tool-spec, if used for that purpose) and what a reader actually
  finds inside the book.
- **Evidence/source-card basis:** `PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_v0.2.md`; direct
  snapshot read confirming zero occurrences of "Response Signals" or "Handoff
  Test" equivalents.
- **Priority: important.**
- **Expected word-count effect:** none to current chapters; potentially
  substantial (a new appendix or Part IV expansion) if the tools are brought
  into the manuscript - not estimated here, since the decision has not been
  made.

### 2.2 Chapter 12's "five maps" vs. the tool-spec's "Relationship Capital Map"

- **Chapter:** 12 (Пять карт одного зала), lines 385-416.
- **Exact current passage:** "Так появляются пять разных изображений одной
  системы: заинтересованные стороны, отношения с ними, связи между ними,
  потоки по этим связям и границы микрогрупп" (line 406).
- **Problem:** this five-part structure (stakeholders / organization's
  relationships / network / flow / boundaries) is a different taxonomy than the
  tool-spec's Relationship Capital Map (a two-layer dyadic/network structure).
  Both are coherent on their own terms; presented together to a reader (e.g., in
  external materials referencing "the Relationship Capital Map" while the book
  itself teaches five different maps) they would read as inconsistent.
- **Proposed editorial action:** if the tool-spec's Relationship Capital Map is
  ever brought into the book, it should either replace Chapter 12's five-map
  structure or be explicitly reconciled with it in the chapter's own text (e.g.,
  "these five maps together answer the questions the Relationship Capital Map
  tool organizes into two layers"). No action is needed if the tool-spec is not
  brought into the book.
- **Evidence/source-card basis:** direct snapshot read; `PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_
  v0.2.md`, tool 1.
- **Priority: important**, contingent on 2.1's decision.
- **Expected word-count effect:** zero unless reconciliation is undertaken, in
  which case a short bridging paragraph (~150-250 words) would suffice - no
  chapter rewrite is needed.

### 2.3 Chapter 13's six design elements vs. the tool-spec's five-element Infrastructure Canvas

- **Chapter:** 13 (Дружба не входит в расписание), lines 417-446.
- **Exact current passage:** "каналами, ролями, ритмами, ритуалами, правилами и
  обменом ценностью" (line 426).
- **Problem:** six named elements (channel, role, rhythm, ritual, rules, value
  exchange) vs. the tool-spec's five (routes, roles, rhythms, channels,
  memory). "Ritual" and "rules"/"value exchange" are in the book but not the
  tool-spec; "memory" is in the tool-spec but the book instead discusses

  (line 17), not as one of Chapter 13's six elements.
- **Proposed editorial action:** same as 2.2 - reconcile only if and when the
  Infrastructure Canvas tool is brought into the book by name; otherwise no
  action needed, since Chapter 13's own six-element structure is internally
  coherent and well-supported (Weiss's theory of change, line 424; Ostrom,
  line 433; Fine's idioculture, line 438).
- **Evidence/source-card basis:** direct snapshot read; `PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_
  v0.2.md`, tool 2.
- **Priority: important**, contingent on 2.1's decision.
- **Expected word-count effect:** zero unless reconciliation is undertaken.

### 2.4 Chapter 21's TAK.Shtab institutionalization-test claim

- **Chapter:** 21 (Передача), line 620.
- **Exact current passage:** "Проверка на это, по Селзнику, простая: выбросили
  бы участники штаба его нынешнее устройство, найдись завтра способ делать то
  же самое эффективнее? К 2023 году штаб, судя по всему, эту проверку прошёл."
- **Problem:** this states, in the author's own voice, that TAK.Shtab
  "apparently passed" a named test. TASK_CLAUDE_0021 established a binding
  restriction against describing TAK.Shtab as having undergone a formal
  institutionalization test. The hedge "судя по всему" (apparently/by all
  appearances) provides some distance, but a reader could plausibly read this
  as a claim that a test was conducted and passed.
- **Proposed editorial action:** editorial judgment call, not resolved by this
  audit. One option, offered only as an example of the kind of change that
  would resolve the tension without losing the sentence's content: reframe from
  "прошёл проверку" (passed the test) to something like "по этим признакам
  соответствовал" (matched these features/criteria) - shifting from a
  pass/fail test-completion frame to a features-observed frame, consistent with
  how `PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_v0.2.md` itself insists this example must be
  described ("by 2024 the structure showed these features," not "a Handoff Test
  was performed"). This is offered as an illustrative option, not a directive -
  the actual editorial call belongs to Serhii or the Chief Editor.
- **Evidence/source-card basis:** TASK_CLAUDE_0021 dispatch (binding
  restriction); `PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_v0.2.md`, Handoff Test section; direct
  snapshot read.
- **Priority: important.**
- **Expected word-count effect:** near zero (a phrase-level edit, not a
  passage rewrite).

## Section 4 — Where the book already supports the Method well (no action needed)

This section exists because Stream 5 asked explicitly where the book *already*
supports the Method, not only where it doesn't - and because a synchronization
map that only lists problems would misrepresent how much of the manuscript
already works.

- **Chapters 1-10** teach Relationship Capital's five dimensions and their
  interactions precisely, consistently with DECISION_0003, with no drift found
  anywhere in the read-through.
- **Chapters 11-16** deliver exactly the discrete practices Canon v1.0 and the
  tool-spec both describe (listening, mapping, hypothesis/design, activation,
  measurement, ownership/communication), each grounded in correctly-cited,
  independently-verified sources.
- **The epistemic discipline around TCF and TAK.Shtab** - every claim traced to
  a specific named interview, TAK.Shtab correctly flagged as the one
  independently-corroborated case - is maintained consistently across every
  chapter checked, and is, on its own, one of this project's more defensible
  claims to methodological seriousness (see
  `PALMA_METHOD_DEFENSIBLE_CLAIMS_PACKAGE_v0.1.md`).
- **The VI-004 caveat** (large-network findings not directly measured at small
  organizational scale) is honored consistently everywhere Barabási-family
  research is cited (Chapters 4, 7, 9).

## Section 5 — What Chapters 17-21 must do after Chapters 11-16, and what the (future) conclusion must state

Per the task brief's explicit request: Chapters 17-21, as currently written,
correctly assemble the discrete practices of Chapters 11-16 into a sequential
client-engagement narrative (diagnose a real client situation, design a bounded
response, launch it for real, advise without deciding for the client, and test
what continues) - this sequencing works on its own terms and does not need
rewriting. What it does not yet do, and cannot do until Section 1's architecture
question is resolved, is state unambiguously which formal phase model (six-phase
DECISION_0005, or four-stage tool-spec) it is an instance of - it currently works
as a good story without committing to either formal skeleton, which is fine for
narrative purposes but insufficient for any future precise methodological
claim. The still-unwritten conclusion must, at minimum: (a) answer the
introduction's own question in its own terms; (b) state plainly what has been
established through the TCF/TAK.Shtab/Bombom material and what has only been
illustrated by it, consistent with
`PALMA_METHOD_DEFENSIBLE_CLAIMS_PACKAGE_v0.1.md`'s recommended language; and (c)
not claim, even implicitly, that Palma Method has been validated.
