## Editorial decision at this stage

The next critical action is the sixty-minute founder interview. Further theory
work or additional expansion of the Founder's personal account is not the
current bottleneck.

The interview must distinguish:

- what Miguel intended;
- what participants actually do;
- what emerged without deliberate design;
- what still depends on Miguel;
- where the friendly account fails or has limits;
- what one small next experiment might later test.

The interview is for the book first. It may end with a light bridge to a later
experiment and communication-strategy working session, but it must not design
those outputs during the interview.

## Evidence and privacy boundaries

- The case is prospective, not proof that the Palma Method works.
- The case cannot claim that TCF caused health, retention, trust, community, or
  commercial outcomes.
- Personal medical, relationship, and client material remains restricted unless
  the Founder approves a specific use.
- Miguel may fact-check names, dates, roles, and quotations. He does not control
  the independent editorial interpretation.
- Participants consent independently. Silence is not consent.
- Three participant interviews provide contrasting accounts, not
  representativeness.

# Miguel interview — 60 minutes

## Interview objective

Understand how TCF functions as a social place before discussing what it could
become. Obtain concrete incidents, mechanisms, limits, and counterexamples that
can support a bounded prospective book case.

## 0–3 minutes — Opening and consent

> Miguel, our first conversation was an opening. Today I want to understand how
> TCF actually works as a social place, not only as a gym.
>
> This is primarily for the Palma Method book. I am not trying to prove that
> TCF is successful or that it causes particular outcomes. I am interested in
> what you intended, what emerged by itself, and what we still do not know.
>
> Concrete situations are more useful than general answers. “I don’t know” is
> a useful answer. Before anything is published, I will send you the factual
> details and proposed quotations for checking.

Confirm:

- May the conversation be recorded?
- May Miguel's name, role, and The Collective Fitness name be used?
- Which topics are off the record?
- May independent participants be approached?
- May direct quotations be proposed for later approval?

## 3–10 minutes — Original intention

### 1. When you started TCF, what did you want to create besides a place where people exercise?

Probes:

- What did you want people to feel?
- Was community part of the original idea, or did that language come later?
- What were you deliberately trying to avoid?
- Did another gym, club, café, or community influence you?

### 2. Think about the most recent person who came to TCF for the first time. What happened from the moment they entered until they left?

Probes:

- Who greeted them?
- Were they introduced to anyone?
- How did they understand what to do?
- What might have felt uncomfortable or confusing?
- Which parts of that experience were consciously designed?

## 10–25 minutes — Everyday mechanics

### 3. What do you personally do to help a new person stop feeling like a stranger?

Probes:

- How do you learn and use names?
- Do you introduce participants to one another?
- How do you notice that someone has become a regular?
- Do you notice when a regular participant disappears?
- Tell me about the last new person you saw becoming a regular. When did you notice the change?

### 4. How do you form small groups during strength blocks, and what happens socially inside those groups?

Probes:

- Do you choose groups intentionally?
- How do people decide order and weights?
- What happens when experience levels are very different?
- Have you had to reduce competition or tension?
- When do participants begin helping one another without a trainer?

### 5. Where did the high-five ritual come from?

Probes:

- Why do you do it after training?
- What do you think it communicates?
- Did participants always high-five one another, or did that emerge later?
- Have you ever skipped it, and did anything feel different?
- Is it important to you, or simply a habit?

### 6. What role do coffee and the time before and after training play in TCF?

Probes:

- Why did you invest in good coffee and equipment?
- Did you expect people to arrive early, stay, or work from the gym?
- What interactions happen during this unstructured time?
- Would relationships be different if everyone arrived exactly on time and left immediately?
- Is coffee part of the business, part of the culture, or both?

### 7. The booking app shows who is coming to a class. Do people choose sessions partly because of the other names they see?

Probes:

- Have participants told you this?
- Do stable groups form around particular times?
- Does the app show a booking or actual attendance?
- Can visibility create motivation, care, or pressure?
- Are there privacy or exclusion risks?

## 25–37 minutes — Evidence of community

### 8. Tell me about the last time TCF members created value for one another without you organising it.

Possible areas to probe:

- helping a newcomer;
- sharing knowledge;
- meeting outside the gym;
- practical help;
- invitations;
- sport or work connections;
- support during a difficult period.

Follow-up:

> Who was involved? What exactly happened? How did you learn about it?

### 9. What relationships that started at TCF now continue outside the gym?

Probes:

- Which are friendships, sport connections, neighbourhood relationships, or work connections?
- Which of these relationships would probably survive if TCF closed tomorrow?
- Which examples are typical, and which are exceptional?

### 10. Do you personally call TCF a community?

Probes:

- What observable evidence makes you use that word?
- What would have to be true for you to stop using it?
- Is it one community, several groups, or a gym with friendly relationships?
- Do participants themselves use the word?

## 37–47 minutes — The Founder's participant experience

Do not introduce the Founder's personal account until Miguel has given his
independent version.

Suggested transition:

> I want to share part of my own experience because it may become the author’s
> entry point into the case. I did not join because I was looking for a
> community. I needed a structure I could enter without first organising a
> social life. Over time, I began choosing classes partly by the familiar names
> in the app. Later I began arriving early, working here, learning the coffee
> machine, and sometimes making coffee for other people. At some point, I
> stopped feeling like only a customer.

### 11. When did you notice that I had become a regular part of the place?

Probes:

- Did you notice when I was absent?
- Did you consciously encourage my involvement, or did it mostly emerge?
- What did it mean when you were comfortable stepping out while I remained?
- Why did you mention my 200 training sessions at the Christmas party?
- Was I the most frequent participant that year, and how was that counted?
- What part of my experience did TCF help enable, and what part was my own initiative or circumstance?

Any medical or other sensitive personal context may be disclosed only by the
Founder and should initially be treated as off the record unless he explicitly
changes that status.

## 47–55 minutes — Limits and dependence

### 12. Tell me about someone who came several times but did not stay.

Probes:

- Did they explain why?
- Who might feel uncomfortable at TCF?
- Can friendliness among regulars look like a closed group to a newcomer?
- Have there been conflicts, exclusion, or excessive competition?
- What is difficult for people who do not speak Portuguese or English confidently?

### 13. What would change if you stepped away from TCF for one month?

Probes:

- Which rituals would continue?
- Who would welcome newcomers?
- Who would connect people?
- What currently depends too heavily on you?
- What already belongs to participants rather than to you?

### 14. Has the community ever created a problem for the business or for you personally?

Probes:

- boundaries between friends, clients, and staff;
- informal access and responsibility;
- unpaid help;
- an inner group making newcomers feel outside;
- enforcing rules with friends;
- expectations that Miguel is always available.

## 55–60 minutes — Bridge to the next step

### 15. What is the most important thing about TCF’s social life that you still do not understand?

Follow-ups:

- What would you genuinely like to learn from three participant interviews?
- Is there one small aspect you might be willing to test for 30 days?
- What result would make you continue, change, or stop it?
- What should Palma never turn TCF into?
- What have I misunderstood or framed too neatly?
- What fact or tension would make the account honest even if it is uncomfortable?
- May I invite three participants for independent conversations?

Do not select the experiment during this interview. Record candidate questions
and return to them after the participant interviews.

## Priority if time is short

Ask Questions 2, 3, 6, 8, 10, 11, 12, and 13.

The central question is:

> Tell me about the last time TCF members created value for one another without
> you organising it.

It distinguishes member-to-member activity from a pleasant atmosphere centred
on a charismatic founder.

## Immediate sequence after the interview

1. Transcribe and complete speaker and quote checks.
2. Separate incidents, observations, interpretations, and hypotheses.
3. Record convergence and disagreement with the Founder's account.
4. Revise the participant guide around the strongest open questions.
5. Interview three contrasting participants if feasible.
6. Only then select a bounded experiment and prepare the communication-strategy
   working session.
