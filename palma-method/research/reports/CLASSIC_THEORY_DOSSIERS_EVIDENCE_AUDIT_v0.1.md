# Classic theory dossiers — evidence audit

## Note on input

The task specified two separate files. What was received is **one combined
document** whose own Part I covers social/relational capital (dossier 1's topic)
and whose Part II covers Feld, Collins, and Lave/Wenger (dossier 2's topic) in
full. Both required topic areas are present. Treated as satisfying the two-dossier
input requirement; flagged here for the record rather than blocking on file count.

## Method

Per the task's explicit instruction, this audit does not repeat broad research from
scratch. It: (a) cross-checks the dossier's claims about five authors against this
project's own, independently-verified existing source cards (Bourdieu SC-001,
Coleman SC-002, Portes SC-016, Collins SC-027, Wenger SC-007) — a strong,
low-cost verification path since those cards were built from primary-source
material before this dossier existed; (b) spot-checks four claims that are new to
this project and load-bearing (the employee-carried-client-tie cluster and the Nour
trademark question) via targeted WebSearch; (c) accepts the dossier's own
self-reported status/confidence for claims neither independently held nor
spot-checked, since the dossier already discloses its own verification tier for
each and no evidence was found suggesting that self-reporting is unreliable.

## Classification key

- **Established finding** — primary source available and genuinely supports the
  claim as stated.
- **Partial support** — primary source supports part of the claim; the claim as
  written is broader or more specific than the source actually shows.
- **Secondary-only** — claim rests on a secondary description of the primary work,
  not a confirmed reading of the primary text itself.
- **Interpretation** — a reasonable synthesis across sources, not a single author's
  own stated finding.
- **Unsupported** — no source in the dossier actually backs the claim as stated.

### Required check: Bourdieu vs. Coleman vs. Portes vs. Nahapiet/Ghoshal vs. Adler/Kwon

The dossier's own comparative table (its §10) accurately separates these five
traditions by research question and unit of analysis, and this project's
independently-verified cards for three of the five (Bourdieu, Coleman, Portes)
confirm the dossier's characterization of each. The distinction is genuine and
should be preserved in any manuscript use: Bourdieu asks about resource
distribution and power; Coleman asks what structural properties enable action;
Portes audits the concept against overclaiming and catalogs its costs;
Nahapiet & Ghoshal bridge to organizational knowledge creation specifically;
Adler & Kwon insist on naming a focal actor and outcome before invoking "social
capital" at all. None of these five is a restatement of another.

### Required check: scientific "relational capital" vs. authorial "Relationship
Capital," and the Nour conflict

Confirmed as a real, pre-existing academic term (Kale, Singh & Perlmutter 2000,
independently verified this session) applied specifically to interorganizational
alliance contexts — not a term the manuscript's practitioner construct can claim to
have originated. Separately and more seriously, "Relationship Capital" is a
**registered U.S. trademark** held by David Nour's consulting firm, independently
confirmed this session, which the dossier itself flagged as an open legal question
("trademark scope not researched") and which this audit now resolves as
**registered, not merely used**. This is a real branding-risk finding, appropriate
to flag for KDP title/positioning decisions but outside this task's scope to
resolve (not a research-content question).

### Required check: Feld as a theory of foci, without causal overreach

Confirmed. Both this project's own prior verification and the dossier agree Feld's
contribution is an *organizing* theory (foci allocate opportunity for contact), not
a demonstrated causal mechanism for friendship formation. Any manuscript use should
keep this framing.

### Required check: Collins and the limits of emotional-energy transfer

Confirmed as a genuine limitation, now better-evidenced than in the existing SC-027
card thanks to the Baker (2019) addition (see Stream 2) — emotional energy is a
theoretically rich construct with comparatively underdeveloped empirical
measurement, and visible behavioral proxies (smiling, high-fives, attendance) do
not themselves establish that EE occurred.

### Required check: Lave/Wenger and the impermissibility of treating "community of
practice" as a universal definition of "community"

Confirmed as the dossier's own explicit discipline (§20, the sports-gym/café/
humanitarian-group worked comparison) and consistent with this project's existing
SC-007 card and with the manuscript's own established distinction (Chapter 2's
domain/community/practice test, already in the locked snapshot). No conflict found.
