# International Cases Shortlist v0.1

## Method

12 candidates evaluated against the founder's own six qualifying tests
(well-known/memorable; mechanism relates directly to relationships,
counter-action, peer-to-peer structure, community, or transfer; factual
basis available; primary source or strong independent documentation
exists; cannot be replaced by another company without losing meaning; adds
a new mechanism rather than repeating one already told). Already-rejected
cases (Bombom, Dewey & LeBoeuf, CrowdStrike/Delta, Zoho, Chief, Abercrombie
& Fitch, Nvidia/OpenAI) were not reconsidered, per instruction, and do not
appear below. 7 of the 12 are recommended - within the requested 6-8 range.

## Recommended (7)

### 1. Harley-Davidson / H.O.G. (Harley Owners Group)

- **Status:** already in the manuscript's Chapter 8 as of the latest
  upstream commit (`MANUSCRIPT_V05_TRANSFER_AND_EVIDENCE_LOG_v0.1.md`
  confirms it forms a "bounded contrast" with Google+). **Recommendation:
  keep.**
- **Mechanism:** a company deliberately builds centralized brand-community
  infrastructure (organized chapters, events, rituals) around an existing
  customer base.
- **Chapter:** 8 (From First Users to Community).
- **Evidence status:** strong - McAlexander, Schouten & Koenig's peer-
  reviewed brand-community research (already carded) plus extensive
  independent documentation.
- **Causality limitations:** self-selection (people who buy a Harley may
  already be predisposed to joining a group identity) cannot be fully
  separated from the infrastructure's own causal effect.
- **Why irreplaceable:** it is the paradigm case in the academic brand-
  community literature itself (Muñiz & O'Guinn cite it); substituting
  another brand would sever the case from its own primary research base.

### 2. CrossFit affiliate licensing model — NEW

- **Status:** new candidate, source card SC-136 created this task.
  **Recommendation: add.**
- **Mechanism:** cheap decentralized licensing (~$3,000/year, far below
  franchise norms) with minimal centralized control, letting thousands of
  independent operators build local community largely from scratch - a
  structurally different mechanism from Harley-Davidson's centralized
  build.
- **Chapter:** 8 (From First Users to Community), with a direct structural
  echo in Chapter 5 (The Collective Fitness) since TCF is itself
  structurally a CrossFit-style gym.
- **Evidence status:** checked, not verified to primary-study standard -
  affiliate counts and fee structure corroborated across multiple
  independent business-press sources; no single peer-reviewed primary
  study of the model located.
- **Causality limitations:** self-selection (CrossFit's demanding,
  initiation-like format may attract unusually committed members
  independent of the licensing structure) cannot be ruled out. Honestly
  reported complication: 2020s business press documents widespread
  affiliate closures despite community strength - the same "community
  strength does not guarantee business survival" finding that led to
  Bombom's removal from the manuscript.
- **Why irreplaceable:** no other brand offers this specific structural
  contrast (cheap decentralized enablement) against Harley-Davidson and
  Google+, and none has TCF's own structural resemblance.

### 3. Google+ (2011-2019)

- **Status:** already in the manuscript's Chapter 8 (per the same transfer
  log). **Recommendation: keep.**
- **Mechanism:** a platform launches at scale without built relational
  infrastructure and fails to generate durable engagement - the negative
  case in the Chapter 8 contrast.
- **Chapter:** 8.
- **Evidence status:** strong - existing case card documents Google's own
  acknowledged engagement failure.
- **Causality limitations:** multiple contributing factors (late market
  entry, unclear value proposition, competition from Facebook) make
  "absence of infrastructure" one plausible contributor among several, not
  a proven sole cause.
- **Why irreplaceable:** a rare case of a well-resourced, technically
  capable company failing specifically at community formation despite
  scale - hard to substitute without losing the "scale is not
  infrastructure" point.

### 4. Alcoholics Anonymous — NEW

- **Status:** new candidate, source card SC-137 created this task.
  **Recommendation: add.**
- **Mechanism:** radical decentralization (no central teaching authority,
  rotating local service roles, one-member-one-vote home groups) as an
  alternate route to founder-independent continuation.
- **Chapter:** 1 (Vipassana), as a short contrastive aside, not a full
  second case treatment.
- **Evidence status:** checked, not verified to primary-study standard -
  governance structure well corroborated via AA's own published materials
  and independent coverage. Explicitly scoped away from AA's clinical-
  effectiveness literature, which this project does not draw on.
- **Causality limitations:** N/A in the usual sense (this is a structural/
  governance description, not a causal outcome claim) - but some
  scholarship argues AA's decentralization is less complete in practice
  than its Twelve Traditions claim; not yet reviewed by this project.
- **Why irreplaceable:** the only candidate in this pool that solves the
  same problem as Vipassana (founder-independent continuation) by the
  opposite mechanism (decentralization vs. standardization) - a
  substitution would lose the contrastive pair entirely.

### 5. Apple / Google / Intel / Adobe no-poach agreement (2005-2009)

- **Status:** already in the manuscript with a source card (SC-126, added
  TASK_CLAUDE_0030). **Recommendation: keep.**
- **Mechanism:** coordinated network closure among elite employers,
  suppressing employee mobility and wage competition across ~64,000
  affected employees.
- **Chapter:** 9 (Whom the Community Did Not Notice).
- **Evidence status:** strong - a $415 million 2015 civil settlement is
  documented via institutional primary records.
- **Causality limitations:** the settlement establishes the agreement and
  its scale; it does not, by itself, quantify the exact wage-suppression
  effect on any individual employee.
- **Why irreplaceable:** among the clearest documented cases of harmful,
  coordinated network structure at the elite end of a labor market -
  directly answers **[Q9]** (mechanisms creating capture and pressure on
  participants) at organizational scale.

### 6. Wikipedia / commons-based peer production — NEW

- **Status:** new candidate, source card SC-138 created this task (flagged
  as this task's weakest-sourced card). **Recommendation: add, with the
  sourcing caveat stated wherever it is used.**
- **Mechanism:** even a maximally open, ostensibly non-hierarchical
  participation system develops durable internal power concentration
  among early, long-tenured contributors (the "iron law of oligarchy"
  finding) - a complication, not a confirmation, of the open-participation
  ideal.
- **Chapter:** 9 (Whom the Community Did Not Notice), as a brief, pointed
  reference rather than a full case narrative.
- **Evidence status:** Benkler's peer-production framework is solid and
  independently confirmed; the specific "iron law" empirical studies are
  confirmed to exist but have not yet been individually identified and
  read by this project - do not use for a specific statistical claim
  without further work.
- **Causality limitations:** whether power concentration is inevitable,
  or specific to Wikipedia's particular governance choices, is not
  established by what this project has checked so far.
- **Why irreplaceable:** the best-known real-world example of open peer
  production; using a less familiar platform would lose the "even this
  one" force of the point.

### 7. Airbnb peer trust vs. hotel brand trust

- **Status:** existing card (`airbnb-peer-trust-vs-hotel-brand-trust.md`),
  not yet used in the manuscript. **Recommendation: add.**
- **Mechanism:** peer-review-based trust infrastructure (guest and host
  ratings) functions as a distinct trust mechanism from institutional
  brand trust (a hotel chain's name and standards) - directly relevant to
  Chapter 3's borrowed-access/trust distinctions.
- **Chapter:** 3 (Borrowed Access).
- **Evidence status:** per the existing card (not re-verified in this
  task; see that card for its own status).
- **Causality limitations:** platform-reported trust metrics are not
  independently audited; self-selection among early Airbnb adopters likely
  affected early trust dynamics.
- **Why irreplaceable:** the clearest large-scale example of peer-
  generated trust operating at commercial scale, distinct from brand-based
  trust - substituting a different peer platform would not change the
  mechanism but would lose the case's public familiarity.

## Evaluated and not recommended (5)

### 8. Uber / Airbnb reputation vs. legitimacy (early years)

Existing card, not recommended for inclusion alongside case #7 above -
the two existing Airbnb-related cards in this project's corpus cover
substantially overlapping ground (peer-generated trust and reputation vs.
institutional legitimacy); including both would repeat a mechanism already
told rather than adding a new one, which the founder's own qualifying test
explicitly excludes. If the Founder/Chief Editor wants the reputation-vs-
legitimacy framing specifically rather than the trust-vs-brand framing,
this card should replace, not supplement, case #7.

### 9. Polaroid brand revival after bankruptcy (2008-2017)

Existing card, not recommended. The mechanism here (a brand persisting
through licensing after its original operating company fails) concerns
intellectual-property and brand-licensing survival, not peer-to-peer
structure, relational infrastructure, or community continuation - it does
not clear the founder's own mechanism-relevance test. It could illustrate
a narrower, different point (brand continuity is not the same as relational
continuity) but that point is not central to any of the fifteen chapters as
currently scoped, and three near-duplicate brand-licensing cases already
exist in the corpus (this one, Pan Am, and Triumph) - including any of them
without a clearly scoped, distinct chapter role would be padding.

### 10. Pan Am brand survival through licensing (1991-)

Existing card, not recommended, for the same reason as Polaroid - brand/IP
survival is a different mechanism from relational-infrastructure survival,
and this case would need its own strong, distinct chapter rationale that
does not currently exist.

### 11. Triumph Motorcycles revival under John Bloor (1983-)

Existing card, not recommended, for the same reason as Polaroid and Pan
Am - a third near-duplicate of the same brand-licensing-survival mechanism
would repeat, not add, and none of the three currently has a distinct,
necessary chapter role.

### 12. Domino's Pizza "Pizza Turnaround" (2009-2010)

New candidate, researched this task, **not recommended**. Extremely well
documented (Harvard Business School teaching case; 14.3% same-store sales
jump; stock $10 to $400; #1 US pizza chain by 2017) - but the mechanism is
company-to-customer responsiveness and public-relations turnaround, not
peer-to-peer structure, community formation, or relational-infrastructure
transfer. At most it illustrates "a Palma moment at brand scale" (a public
counter-action to public criticism), which is a narrower fit than the
founder's own case-selection bar requires (mechanism must relate directly
to relationships, counter-action, peer-to-peer structure, community, or
transfer - "counter-action" is present, but the other four tests are not).
Including it risks exactly the kind of borrowed-credibility overclaim
flagged in `PALMA_PRIOR_ART_AND_NOVELTY_STRESS_TEST_v0.2.md`.
