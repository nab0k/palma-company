# TCF interview codebook v0.1

## Status and purpose

This is a **retrospective analysis protocol**, frozen after the interviews were
collected and before the present full-corpus coding pass. It is not a
preregistration. Its purpose is narrower: make one researcher's judgments
visible, reproducible enough to challenge, and suitable for an independent
second-coder check.

The codebook examines a proposed sequence:

> repeated exposure → direct interaction → consequential action → response →
> continuation → organizer-independent route

The sequence is a Palma hypothesis, not an established causal law. A later step
may be observed while an earlier one is undocumented; such a case is not repaired
by inference.

## Units, waves, and sources

- **Unit of analysis:** one participant's account. The organization is never the
  unit for these codes.
- **Wave 1:** SC-095–SC-099, collected by 16 August and previously coded under a
  narrower four-variable protocol.
- **Wave 2:** SC-142 and the two respondents within SC-143. These were collected
  later and are descriptive extensions, not part of the earlier protocol.
- **Founder material:** SC-043, SC-044, and SC-144 are contextual and
  hypothesis-generating. They are never counted as participant outcomes.
- **Evidence used in this pass:** privacy-safe source cards and existing coded
  derivatives. A later primary-source pass may change the calls.

## Universal evidence-state codes

Every substantive call has an evidence state. In the compact matrix, a numbered
level means relevant evidence is present, a zero level means explicit absence
after probing, and the following two missingness codes remain visible:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `U` | Unclear: relevant material exists but does not resolve the definition. |
| `NA` | Not asked, not attributable to this respondent, or excluded by privacy restrictions. |

Silence is `NA`, never a zero. General praise is not evidence of an episode. A
founder's account about participants cannot turn a participant's `NA` into `P`.

## Core codes

### E — Repeated exposure

**Question:** did the respondent repeatedly encounter a partly stable set of TCF
participants?

- `E0`: explicitly no stable repeated exposure.
- `E1`: episodic or irregular exposure.
- `E2`: regular repeated exposure.
- `U/NA`: frequency or stability cannot be separated.

Admissible evidence includes an explicit attendance rhythm, recognition of
recurring people, or a dated period of repeated participation. A membership,
Wodify count, or the word “regular” without a social-exposure account does not by
itself establish repeated exposure to the same people.

### J — Direct joint interaction

**Question:** did the respondent do something interdependent with peers rather
than merely occupy the same room?

- `J0`: only parallel/solitary participation after direct probing.
- `J1`: direct paired or small-group practice is described.
- `U/NA`: the interview does not distinguish parallel from joint activity.

Pairing for an exercise, sharing equipment, solving a class task, or coordinating
an order counts. Seeing familiar faces, greeting, or receiving instruction only
from a coach does not.

### T — Direct peer tie

**Question:** did a relationship formed or substantially activated through TCF
create a direct route between two participants?

- `T0`: explicitly no new direct peer tie.
- `T1`: recognizable acquaintance with a direct contact route.
- `T2`: ongoing friendship, collaboration, or repeated direct contact.
- `U/NA`: origin, directness, or attribution is unresolved.

Pre-existing ties are recorded separately and do not count as TCF-origin ties.
A spouse or friend introduced through a TCF participant is a second-order network
extension, not automatically a TCF participant tie.

### Q — Consequential participant-initiated action

**Question:** does the account contain a specific unscripted action by one
participant toward another that carried time, effort, resources, risk, access, or
responsibility?

- `Q0`: explicitly none after episode-level probing.
- `Q1`: a qualifying episode is described.
- `U/NA`: the action, initiator, recipient, or consequence is unclear.

Examples that can qualify: co-signing a document, sharing a substantial work or
travel resource, making a professional introduction, communicating a housing
lead, arranging concrete support. Greetings, high-fives, coffee purchased as a
commercial service, and attendance at a founder-organized event do not qualify.
An attempted action may count as `Q1`; its reception is coded separately under
`R`.

### R — Consequential response

**Question:** did the other person answer the qualifying action in a way that
changed what happened next?

- `R0`: a qualifying offer/action was explicitly ignored or declined.
- `R1`: the recipient accepted, answered, used, reciprocated, or continued the
  sequence.
- `U/NA`: a qualifying action exists but its reception is unknown, or no action
  was observed.

This code operationalizes the “second hand” in the Palma Moment hypothesis. It
does not require equal repayment. A request followed by a volunteer signing a
document is `R1`; an introduction whose outcome is unknown is `U`.

### C — Continuation beyond scheduled practice

**Question:** how far did the direct peer relationship continue beyond a class?

- `C0`: explicitly no continuation.
- `C1`: only class-adjacent contact (reception, immediate coffee, changing area).
- `C2`: contact or action outside the gym/scheduled class.
- `C3`: continued after one party reduced attendance, left, or relocated.
- `U/NA`: location, timing, or persistence is unresolved.

Online messages about an outside activity count as `C2`. A prediction that a
friendship would survive closure is not `C3`; observed post-exit contact is.

### I — Organizer independence of the documented sequence

**Question:** did the action/continuation use a direct participant route in that
specific episode?

- `I0`: organizer initiated, relayed, authorized, or remained the only route.
- `I1`: mixed or attribution unclear.
- `I2`: participants acted directly without organizer mediation in the episode.
- `NA`: no qualifying sequence exists.

The organizer may have created the original setting and the episode may still be
`I2`. Independence concerns the documented later route, not historical causal
purity.

### D — Dependence on founder presence

**Question:** what does the respondent say would happen to their own participation
or relationships if the founder temporarily stepped away?

- `D0`: little or no expected change.
- `D1`: atmosphere/experience would weaken, but participation or peer ties remain.
- `D2`: participation or relevant ties are expected to substantially stop.
- `U/NA`: no attributable counterfactual answer.

Founder-contact warmth is not founder dependence. Record organizer-contact
strength as a memo, not as a substitute for `D`.

### H — Participant-created channel

**Question:** is there a direct communication route not operated for each exchange
by the founder?

- `H0`: explicitly none.
- `H1`: direct one-to-one participant contact.
- `H2`: participant-created group channel.
- `U/NA`: channel ownership or use is unknown.

The Wodify booking list is not a social channel unless participants actually use
it to coordinate socially.

### L — Boundary, periphery, or non-connection signal

**Question:** does the account document someone not entering the relationship
sequence or identify a boundary affecting entry?

- `L0`: directly probed and none reported.
- `L1`: a specific peripheral/non-connected person or boundary is described.
- `U/NA`: not probed or too general.

Personality, language, training intensity, schedule, migration, prior ties, and
interviewer friendship are coded as rival explanations in a memo, not collapsed
into `L`.

## Episode table required from each coder

Before assigning `Q`, `R`, `C`, or `I`, record one row per candidate episode:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Source | SC ID and safe respondent label |
| Initiator | participant / founder / interviewer / unclear |
| Recipient | participant / founder / third party / unclear |
| Action | one factual paraphrase |
| Response | factual paraphrase or `not reported` |
| Setting | class / class-adjacent / outside / after exit |
| Route | founder-mediated / direct peer / mixed / unknown |
| Evidence | volunteered / prompted / interviewer-supplied / hearsay |
| Privacy | admissible / excluded / requires re-consent |

One vivid episode cannot automatically populate every stage. For example, an
attempted professional introduction can establish `Q1` while leaving `R`, `C`,
and `I` unclear.

## Confidence

Each call receives `high`, `medium`, or `low` confidence:

- **High:** explicit episode/denial, attributable speaker, directly responsive
  question, no material transcript problem.
- **Medium:** clear self-report with limited detail or retrospective ambiguity.
- **Low:** prompted reconstruction, joint-interview attribution problem,
  interviewer supplies a key bridge, or only a derivative summary is available.

Confidence does not turn `U` into a binary call.

## Second-coder procedure

1. Give the second coder this codebook without the first-coder matrix.
2. Give access to the same privacy-safe extracts, or to primary transcripts under
   the same confidentiality restrictions.
3. Randomly select at least three of the eight member respondents (37.5%),
   including one negative/low-connection case and one Wave-2 case.
4. Both coders independently produce the episode table and code calls.
5. Compare exact agreement for `E/J/T/Q/R/C/I/D/H/L`. Do not calculate Cohen's
   kappa at this sample size as if it were stable; report raw agreement and every
   disagreement.
6. Reconcile only after preserving both original calls and the reason for change.

The second coder tests whether definitions travel between readers. They do not
make the sample representative or repair interviewer influence.
