# Memorable research findings for founders v0.1

Stream 5 of `TASK_CLAUDE_0030`. Thirteen findings, not fifteen — a smaller,
tighter list was preferred over padding to a round number, per instruction
10. Each is well-sourced (primary or independently checked in this task),
concrete enough to state without academic background, and mapped to one
chapter. None are offered as a mini-case; each entry states the one idea
the fact makes visible, in the epistemic category it actually earns.

1. **Harley-Davidson's market share fell from 78% to 23% between 1973 and 1983 — and the company's response to the crisis was to give away control, not tighten it.** In September 1982 a Harley marketer proposed a formal owners' infrastructure; the Harley Owners Group launched 1 January 1983 and eventually grew past 1,400 local chapters, each led by owners themselves, not company staff. *Idea made visible (Chapter 8):* infrastructure a company builds does not have to be filled by the company — H.O.G.'s value came from the structure Harley provided plus the practice owners supplied themselves. **Verified fact** (already carded, in-manuscript).

2. **A study of 673 managers at a defense contractor found that each one-unit reduction in "network constraint" — how locked-in someone's contacts were to each other — corresponded to about $681 more in that manager's measured annual performance.** Burt's structural-holes research didn't measure intelligence or effort; it measured position. *Idea made visible (Chapter 4/10):* where you sit in a network is a checkable, even priceable fact, not a personality trait. **Verified fact** (already carded, in-manuscript).

3. **Between 2005 and 2009, four of the most admired companies in tech — Apple, Google, Intel, and Adobe — ran an informal agreement among executives not to recruit each other's engineers, never announced to the roughly 64,000 employees it affected. It cost them $415 million in a 2015 court settlement.** *Idea made visible (Chapter 9):* a dense, closed network among people who already have power can extract real value from a much larger group with zero visibility into the arrangement — the coercive side of closure operating between organizations. **Verified fact** (new card `SC-126`, court record).

4. **The UK's Vipassana Trust — a "free" meditation retreat organization — reported £1.423 million in income and £1.110 million in expenditure for the year ending May 2025, and lists paid employees on its books alongside its volunteers.** *Idea made visible (Chapter 1):* "no fee for the participant" and "no funding" are two entirely different claims — a reproducible system this size runs on real money and, at least here, some real payroll. **Verified fact, independently confirmed against the UK charity register in this task.**

5. **S. N. Goenka's first course, in Bombay in July 1969, had fourteen participants. As of this task's check in 2026, the movement's own directory listed roughly 270 permanent centres and 132 additional teaching locations worldwide — more than a decade after Goenka's own death in 2013.** *Idea made visible (Chapter 1):* the system kept scaling *after* the founder was gone, which is the actual test of whether it was ever separated from him in the first place. **Verified fact**, chronology independently confirmed in this task; directory count consistent with an independent 2025 check, not re-confirmed to the exact day.

6. **In March 1982, at the Burmese Vihara in Bodh Gaya, a ten-day Vipassana course ran for the first time without Goenka teaching it in person — led instead by a newly appointed assistant teacher, using recordings systematically produced starting that same year specifically, in the organization's own words, "to maintain uniformity."** *Idea made visible (Chapter 1):* there was an exact, dateable moment when the founder's personal presence stopped being the bottleneck — most organizations never get to point to a day this precisely. **Verified fact**, independently confirmed in this task.

7. **Beside Dhamma Giri, the movement's flagship center, sits a purpose-built residential village — forty-five bungalow-style units, a shared community center, resident committees, built starting in 1999.** It is a genuine, small permanent community that grew out of a retreat system — but the evidence does not show this is typical; most Vipassana centers have no such village at all. *Idea made visible (Chapter 1), stated with its own limit attached:* a retreat *can* eventually generate a literal residential community, but this should be read as a documented exception, not a promise. **Verified fact, independently confirmed; explicitly not to be generalized.**

8. **In a classic study of how people actually found new jobs, the decisive lead usually came not from a close friend but from someone the job-seeker barely kept in touch with.** Granovetter's finding wasn't that weak ties are nicer — it's that close friends tend to already know what you know, while a rarely-contacted acquaintance is more likely to be standing in a different information circle entirely. *Idea made visible (Chapter 4):* the value of a relationship and its emotional closeness are two different variables. **Verified fact** (already carded, in-manuscript).

9. **Across fifty-one work teams at one manufacturing company, whether a team felt safe enough to admit mistakes predicted whether it actually learned from them — and the team members' individual confidence in their own ability did not.** Edmondson's 1999 finding located the effect at the team level, not the person level. *Idea made visible (Chapter 9):* "we have confident, capable people" and "our people can safely tell us what's going wrong" are not the same organizational property, and only the second one predicted learning in this study. **Verified fact**, existing card, currently under-deployed in-manuscript per this task's own recommendation.

10. **In 1993, IBM's own board had already decided the company was too big and needed to be broken up — investment bankers were pricing the pieces — when a newly arrived CEO argued the opposite: that the size everyone wanted to dismantle was the one thing clients actually valued.** Gerstner reversed the breakup and built IBM Global Services instead. *Idea made visible (Chapter 12):* a unanimous internal diagnosis can still be wrong, and disagreement arriving from one differently-positioned observer is sometimes the whole story. **Verified fact**, the single highest evidence-tier case in the corpus (already carded, `verification_status: verified`).

11. **A 2011-2012 study of Ukrainians in Lisbon found that within a short period, Ukrainians had become the second-largest foreign community in Portugal — a country with no prior historical migration ties to Ukraine at all — and that pioneer migrants' own social networks, not any institution, were the documented reason.** The same study also found the networks' generosity to new arrivals weakened later, as conditions in Portugal worsened. *Idea made visible (Chapter 7):* a migrant network can be simultaneously the reason a whole community became possible and the reason its openness wasn't permanent. **Verified fact** (new card `SC-125`, 306-respondent survey plus 31 interviews).

12. **Large real-world networks with a few highly-connected hubs tend to survive random damage almost unaffected — and fall apart quickly the moment the specific hub nodes are targeted.** This single structural property (from network-topology research on "scale-free" networks) is the same one that makes a hub valuable and the same one that makes it dangerous to depend on. *Idea made visible (Chapter 10):* resilience and fragility are not opposites here — they are the same fact, viewed from two different threats. **Verified fact** (already carded, in-manuscript; a topological finding, not a claim about any one organization).

13. **Selznick's own proposed test for whether a group has actually "institutionalized" is blunt: would its members discard the current structure tomorrow if a more efficient way to do the same work appeared?** Most organizations that describe themselves as having "become real" have never actually been asked this question. *Idea made visible (Chapter 11/15):* institutionalization is a claim that can be tested, not just felt — and the honest answer for TAK.Shtab, per the manuscript's own account, is that the test has never actually been run. **Supported interpretation** — Selznick's test is real and citable; whether any specific organization would pass it is, by definition, untested until asked.
