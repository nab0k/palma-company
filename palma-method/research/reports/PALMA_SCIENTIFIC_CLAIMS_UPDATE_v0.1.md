## 1. The strongest scientific-status statement now supportable

"Palma Method's transition model - the idea that repeated encounters become
durable relational infrastructure through participant-initiated action,
routes of continuation, and reduced organizer-dependence - is a defensible
conceptual proposal, built by combining established research (interaction
ritual theory, social foci, network governance, community-of-practice
development) in a way this project's own targeted search did not find already
combined elsewhere. It has not been empirically tested. A first, small,
exploratory coding pass against five existing field interviews found the
model's basic elements present in most cases and absent in a designed
contrast case - consistent with, but nowhere close to sufficient to confirm,
the model." This status did not change, strengthen, or weaken as a result of
this task's two new streams - it survived closer scrutiny at the same level
TASK_CLAUDE_0025 set it at.

## 2. The narrow novelty statement

"What appears original is not any single mechanism - not that repeated
encounters matter, not that reciprocity builds trust, not that organizations
can outlive their founders - each of those is established research this book
cites by name. What appears original is the specific link between a small,
person-to-person event (an unscripted, consequential action one participant
takes for another) and a larger, structural outcome (whether continuing
without the organizer's involvement). A dedicated search across social-network
methodology, network-governance theory, and community-development research
did not find this specific link already proposed elsewhere." Qualify further,
per the prior-art closure: "the closest existing concept for the
organizer-independence side of this idea is research on network governance
forms (participant-governed vs. lead-organization-governed networks); the
closest existing concept for treating a single dated action as the right unit
to track is relational event modeling, a statistical method, not a
substantive theory making this same claim."

## 3. What TCF allows the book to say

- That in a small, non-random set of member accounts from one gym, most
  members who describe some connection to others also describe at least one
  specific, unscripted, consequential action they or another member took -
  not organized by the founder.
- That the one interview subject specifically selected as a likely contrast
  case (low attendance regularity, no expected peer connection) reported,
  when asked directly, no such action and no connections formed - consistent
  with, though not proof of, the model's basic logic.
- That these accounts illustrate what the model's constructs might look like
  in a real setting - a specific favor recalled only when prompted, a
  co-signed document, a trip-planning file shared unasked.

## 4. What TCF does not allow the book to say

- That the model has been tested, confirmed, or validated in any sense.
- That these five people represent TCF's membership as a whole, or that any
  finding here generalizes to "TCF members."
- That the pattern found (unscripted action more often present than absent
  among four of five participants) is a measured association, a correlation,
  or anything with a numeric confidence attached - it is a count of five
  cases, one coder, no comparison group.
- That "continued peer relationship outside scheduled training" was cleanly
  observed - the coding pass itself found this construct genuinely hard to
  separate from ordinary gym-adjacent contact in two of the five interviews,
  and the book should not claim more precision here than the evidence
  supports.
- Any claim distinguishing which of the five participants said what, by name
  or by any detail that would make them identifiable within TCF's own
  membership, beyond what each source card's own consent status already
  permits.

## 5. Propositions that should be weakened, removed, or redefined

- **P2** (a Palma Moment is identifiable by definition) should be
  explicitly reframed, wherever it appears for a reader, as a *measurement-
  reliability* claim, not a settled fact - this task's own attempt to apply
  it (the exploratory coding pass) found real ambiguity in at least one case
  (Participant A's item-lending episode, coded here as organizer-directed
  rather than peer-directed - a distinction the original interview material
  did not make cleanly either) that a single coder had to resolve alone,
  without a second rater to check against.
- **P6 and P7** (route-quality mattering more than frequency; infrastructure
  mediating the reciprocal-action-to-readiness link) should continue to be
  presented, if used at all, explicitly as untested and not yet even
  exploratorily probed - this task's coding pass could not address either,
  since the interview material lacks the episode-by-episode route detail
  they would require.
- No proposition needs to be *removed* based on this task's findings - none
  was contradicted by anything found in either stream.

## 6. Definitions requiring modification

- **Palma Moment**: no change to the core definition, but the "what cannot
  be claimed" language already present in `PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_v0.2.md`
  should be extended with one addition: a Palma Moment directed at the
  organizer (a participant doing something for the founder) is a different
  event, for this model's purposes, than one directed at another participant
  - this task's coding pass needed to draw that line explicitly and found
  the existing definition did not already make it clearly enough for
  consistent coding.
- **Route of continuation**: no change to the definition, but a coding-level
  clarification is recommended for any future operational use: a route
  should be confirmed by an actual instance of use independent of the
  organizer, not merely inferred from a stated willingness or capacity (e.g.,
  a "who would you contact" list is evidence of a *possible* route, not a
  confirmed one) - this distinction caused two of five ambiguous codes in
  this task's exploratory pass.
- **Distributed relational infrastructure**: no change needed - not directly
  tested by this task.
- **Readiness to respond**: no change needed - not directly tested by this
  task; still requires an actual request/demand to observe, per its existing
  definition.
- **Transfer/Handoff Test**: no change to the test's own definition, but see
  item 7 below - the specific phrase this task was asked to correct
  ("tested through real client work") touches this tool directly, since the
  Handoff Test itself has never actually been run as a formal test against
  TCF or TAK.Shtab, per `PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_v0.2.md`'s own existing,
  correct disclosure.

## 7. Exact claims for Codex to insert, avoid, or qualify during manuscript synchronization

**Insert (if a methods note, appendix, or conclusion is drafted):**
- "This book's transition model - from repeated encounters to relational
  infrastructure - draws on established research and proposes one new link
  between a small, observable event and a larger structural outcome. That
  link has not been tested; a small exploratory check against existing field
  interviews found it consistent with, but far short of confirming, the
  model."

**Avoid entirely, everywhere:**
- Any formulation implying that Palma Method, or any of its five tools, was
  "**tested through real client work**." Per this task's explicit
  instruction, replace with **"developed through real cases and field
  observations"** as the default, unless a specific future passage has
  evidence supporting something more precise than that (e.g., a specific,
  actually-run pilot per `PALMA_METHOD_FALSIFIABLE_PROPOSITIONS_v0.1.md`'s
  validation path, if and when one occurs).
- "Validated," "proven," "scientifically confirmed," or "evidence-based"
  attached to the five tools or the five Relationship Capital dimensions,
  per the standing restriction already established in
  `PALMA_METHOD_DEFENSIBLE_CLAIMS_PACKAGE_v0.1.md` and unchanged by this
  task.
- Any claim that the TCF exploratory coding in this task's Stream 2 measured,
  counted, or established a pattern across "TCF members" - it covers five
  named individuals, non-randomly selected, one coder, no comparison group.

**Qualify, if used:**
- Chapter 21's TAK.Shtab "apparently passed" sentence (flagged in both
  TASK_CLAUDE_0024 and TASK_CLAUDE_0025) - still unresolved, still an
  editorial call, restated here for Codex's visibility since it directly
  touches the Handoff/Transfer construct this task also examined.
- Any future manuscript passage drawing on the "who would you message" style
  operational test (used in the TCF interviews to establish peer-contact
  density) should state plainly that this measures stated willingness or
  recall, not confirmed enacted contact - the distinction this task's own
  coding pass needed and found the underlying interview material did not
  always support cleanly.
