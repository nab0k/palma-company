# Chapters 11-21 — function and overlap map

Analytical document only. No manuscript text was edited to produce this; every
quoted fragment below is verbatim from the current locked snapshot, reproduced only
to identify it precisely.

## Part A — per-chapter function table

### Chapter 11 — "Кафе, которого я не знал" (Listen)

- **Unique function**: establishes listening as a discipline with three independent
  failure conditions (a path to speak, safety/meaningfulness of using it, capacity
  to act on it) — not passive information collection.
- **Unit of analysis**: the organization's overall receptivity system, tested at
  the owner-to-audience level (Bombom) and briefly at the member-visibility level
  (TCF).
- **New mechanism**: McNamara's speak-vs-listen resource allocation finding;
  Morrison's powerlessness-not-fear account of silence; Edmondson's psychological
  safety; Goffman's focused/unfocused interaction; Arnstein's participation ladder
  (voice vs. actual influence).
- **Reader's practical action**: check the three listening conditions before
  claiming an organization "listens."
- **Personal story**: the Sandra/Bombom interview — arriving with a ready diagnosis
  and letting the material complicate it rather than confirm it.
- **Main case**: Bombom.
- **Palma tool connection**: precursor to Diagnose's "name the alternative
  explanation" discipline (Ch17) and to Facilitate's Humble Inquiry principle — this
  chapter is where the book first demonstrates not confirming one's own hypothesis.
- **Repeats from earlier chapters**: Chapter 10's closing point (the most satisfied,
  central participants can't describe the whole system).
- **Repeats in later chapters**: the Arnstein consultation-vs-influence distinction
  recurs in Chapter 16 ("мы уже различали консультацию и влияние"); the Fine
  idioculture concept, defined here, is **redefined again in Chapter 13** (see
  overlap below).

### Chapter 12 — "Пять карт одного зала" (Map)

- **Unique function**: establishes that "the network" is not one map but at least
  five distinct, non-substitutable maps (stakeholders, org-to-person relationship,
  network structure, flow-type, microgroup boundary), each serving a different
  decision.
- **Unit of analysis**: deliberately varies by map — individual stakeholder, dyadic
  tie, whole network, relationship content-type, subgroup boundary — the chapter's
  own point is that the unit must be chosen per question.
- **New mechanism**: Freeman's stakeholder theory; Lamont & Molnar's symbolic vs.
  social boundaries; Fine & van den Scott's temporary communities.
- **Reader's practical action**: name the decision a map must serve before drawing
  it; never merge the five layers into one master diagram.
- **Personal story**: none new — a brief TAK.Shtab aside (logistics contact vs.
  access contact vs. confidential contact as three different maps).
- **Main case**: TCF (all five maps applied hypothetically).
- **Palma tool connection**: direct ancestor of the Relationship Capital Map's
  network layer (structure/bridges/redundancy/bottlenecks) — the phrase "карта
  сети — территория мостов, кластеров, изолированных точек и узких мест" is this
  chapter's own language for what the tool spec later formalizes.
- **Repeats from earlier chapters**: explicit callback to Chapter 9 for the network
  map ("которую мы разбирали в девятой главе") and to Krackhardt & Hanson (also
  Chapter 9).
- **Repeats in later chapters**: the microgroup-boundary material anticipates
  Chapter 16's symbolic/social boundary discussion, which uses the same Lamont &
  Molnar framework again.

### Chapter 13 — "Дружба не входит в расписание" (Design)

- **Unique function**: introduces design as creating *conditions* for connection
  rather than engineering the connection itself; introduces six elements — channel,
  role, rhythm, ritual, rules, value exchange — as the places good intentions most
  often lose contact with real behavior.
- **Unit of analysis**: the designed structure itself (a class format, a schedule,
  a chat) rather than any individual tie.
- **New mechanism**: Carol Weiss's theory of change (the explicit "if X then Y"
  hypothesis discipline).
- **Reader's practical action**: write the "if" hypothesis behind any structural
  decision, so a failure can be diagnosed rather than left mysterious.
- **Personal story**: the pre-war Kyiv office's open-Friday practice.
- **Main case**: TCF (schedule/small-group mechanics); Chief (business case).
- **Palma tool connection**: direct ancestor of the Infrastructure Canvas — **but
  with a real terminology mismatch**, flagged in detail under Overlap 13↔18 below:
  this chapter uses six elements (channel/role/rhythm/ritual/rules/value exchange),
  while the Part IV tool spec's Infrastructure Canvas uses five
  (routes/roles/rhythms/channels/memory). These are not the same list.
- **Repeats from earlier chapters**: explicit callback to Chapter 11 ("Глава о
  слушании уже показала пределы этой логики"); to Chapter 5 (Collins, rhythm); to
  Chapter 10 (Ostrom, rules); **redefines Fine's idioculture concept, already fully
  defined in Chapter 11**, even while saying "мы уже встречали этот термин раньше."
- **Repeats in later chapters**: Weiss's theory of change is reused as the
  verification structure in Chapter 15 ("Теория изменений Кэрол Вайс нужна здесь
  именно как структура проверки").

### Chapter 14 — "Кто продолжит без тебя" (Activate/Participate)

- **Unique function**: distinguishes "activation" as a genuine intervention with an
  unknown result from mere invitation or marketing activation; introduces
  safe-to-fail framing, single-variable intervention discipline, and the
  institutionalization tension (recognition without bureaucracy vs. resources with
  bureaucracy).
- **Unit of analysis**: an individual role or practice's lifecycle, from ad hoc
  action to institutionalized position.
- **New mechanism**: Snowden & Boone's Cynefin (probe-sense-respond under
  uncertainty); Rogers' diffusion of innovations (trusted broker vs. loudest fan).
- **Reader's practical action**: run small, single-variable interventions; ask who
  pays for failure before intervening; distinguish attendance from participation.
- **Personal story**: the TAK.Shtab CBO ("Chief Box Officer") story and the
  volunteer whose role became paid; the Roxy/T-shirt fundraiser detail.
- **Main case**: TAK.Shtab, exclusively — no TCF material in this chapter.
- **Palma tool connection**: **this chapter is where "Palma-момент" is used a
  second time** ("Palma-момент повторяется и постепенно становится не исключением,
  а правдоподобным ожиданием") — see the dedicated note under Overlap 14↔19 below.
  The chapter's small-bet/safe-to-fail material is the direct ancestor of the
  Launch stage's completion criteria in the tool spec.
- **Repeats from earlier chapters**: explicit callback to Chapter 13 ("В
  тринадцатой главе дизайн формулировал ставку"); to Chapter 11 ("Глава о слушании
  предлагает"); to Chapter 9 (brokers/bridges).
- **Repeats in later chapters**: **this is the single largest overlap in Part
  A/B of the book** — see the dedicated Overlap 14↔19 and 14↔21 sections below.

### Chapter 15 — "Сколько весит готовность помочь?" (Measure)

- **Unique function**: measurement discipline — separates volume metrics from
  capacity/capability metrics; argues against collapsing Relationship Capital's five
  dimensions into one score; establishes baseline discipline and names
  metric-gaming risk.
- **Unit of analysis**: explicitly chosen per question — individual, pair,
  subgroup, or whole network — the chapter argues the unit itself must be selected
  deliberately for each measurement.
- **New mechanism**: the Barcelona Principles (communication-measurement
  discipline); Sampson, Raudenbush & Earls' collective efficacy research, cited
  specifically for its *methodological* point (a group property cannot be
  reconstructed by summing individual impressions).
- **Reader's practical action**: name the decision before choosing metrics; keep a
  baseline; track concentration of participation, not only totals; test each "if"
  link separately rather than declaring blanket success or failure.
- **Personal story**: the TAK.Shtab volume-vs-capacity distinction (boxes and cars
  counted vs. "who returned, who could replace the coordinator").
- **Main case**: TCF (Wodify data caveats) and TAK.Shtab (measurement under crisis).
- **Palma tool connection**: **direct ancestor of two tools at once.** The
  "concentration of participation" and "test of one person's disappearance"
  material is the direct ancestor of the Handoff Test's diagnostic logic. Separately,
  the sentence "человек возвращается, отвечает другому участнику, рекомендует,
  предупреждает о проблеме, помогает или принимает роль" is an early, near-verbatim
  draft of the **Response Signals** list (responds/returns/helps/connects/
  coordinates/takes responsibility) later formalized in the tool spec.
- **Repeats from earlier chapters**: Weiss's theory of change, reused from Chapter
  13. **A genuine dangling reference**: the chapter states "в предыдущей главе
  исследование коллективной эффективности Роберта Сэмпсона... помогло отделить
  хорошие отношения от общей уверенности" — but a full-text search of the current
  manuscript snapshot finds **no other occurrence of "Сэмпсон" or "коллективной
  эффективности" anywhere in the book, including Chapter 14.** This is not an
  overlap but a missing citation: Chapter 15 references material as if it were
  covered in "the previous chapter," and it is not currently present there. Flagged
  as a **missing transition**, not resolved here (manuscript not edited).
- **Repeats in later chapters**: the "test исчезновения одного человека" (removal
  test) is echoed directly by Chapter 21's Handoff Test material and by Chapter 9's
  earlier "если убрать одну точку" framing (a three-chapter arc: 9 → 15 → 21).

### Chapter 16 — "Кому принадлежит разговор" (Own/Communicate)

- **Unique function**: distinguishes communication that flows only to/from the
  organization from communication infrastructure that participants use with each
  other; introduces four negotiated domains (participation, knowledge, boundaries,
  power) and the unpaid-labor-of-relationships question.
- **Unit of analysis**: the whole system's communication architecture, compared
  directly across two cases (Bombom vs. TCF) at the same physical distance (thirty
  meters apart).
- **New mechanism**: none newly introduced — this chapter is a synthesis chapter,
  explicitly built by recombining Wenger (community of practice, Chapter 2), Fine &
  van den Scott (temporary communities, Chapter 12), and the five Relationship
  Capital dimensions (introduced across Chapters 3-9).
- **Reader's practical action**: ask, for a given communication channel, whether
  participants create value *for each other* through it, not only whether the
  organization broadcasts through it.
- **Personal story**: none new.
- **Main case**: Bombom and TCF, directly compared.
- **Palma tool connection**: closes Part III by explicitly naming this as "the last
  question of the practical part," setting up Part IV's synthesis into a sequential
  cycle. Contains the confirmed-and-anonymized SC-097 (Anya) quote ("Не считаю, что
  я выпала из сообщества...").
- **Repeats from earlier chapters**: the Arnstein consultation-vs-influence
  distinction (Chapter 11); the CBO story (Chapter 14, "История CBO из предыдущей
  главы"); Wenger (Chapter 2); Fine & van den Scott (Chapter 12, near-identical
  framing reused almost verbatim — see Overlap note below); the five RC dimensions
  recapped in full.
- **Repeats in later chapters**: the ownership/ownership-of-structure theme recurs
  directly in Chapter 21's Transfer material (see Overlap 16↔21 below).

### Chapter 17 — "Диагноз" (Diagnose)

See `PART_IV_EVIDENCE_AND_ARCHITECTURE_AUDIT_v0.1.md` (TASK_CLAUDE_0018) for the full
existing audit of this chapter; summarized here for cross-reference only. Unique
function: diagnosis as a falsifiable discipline (name the alternative, name what
would disprove it). Unit of analysis: explicitly demonstrated at two different
scales (individual military-enlistment scene; TCF subgroup-vs-whole-gym scene).
New mechanism at this point in the book: Schein's process consultation; Croskerry's
premature closure; the Gerstner/IBM case. Main case: TAK.Shtab founding + TCF (SC-095,
confirmed per `PART_IV_CLAIMS_LEDGER_v0.1.md` item P4-006).

### Chapter 18 — "Дизайн" (Design)

Unique function: design as recognizing and bounding already-emergent structure, with
an explicit, named cost to narrowing. Unit of analysis: the organization's structure
as a whole (warehouse, duty roster) narrowing to a specific audience (IDP mothers).
New mechanism: Ostrom (institutional boundaries); the Abercrombie & Fitch case. Main
case: TAK.Shtab exclusively (no TCF in this chapter).

### Chapter 19 — "Запуск" (Launch)

Unique function: launching as recognizing that some necessary structure only
assembles during the action itself. Unit of analysis: a specific team/crew in a
specific real event (the convoy). New mechanism: Weick (Mann Gulch sensemaking
collapse); Dynes & Quarantelli (emergent-organization typology); the
CrowdStrike/Delta case. Main case: TAK.Shtab exclusively.

### Chapter 20 — "Совет" (Facilitate interlude)

Unique function: isolates the Facilitate principle using a case where the author has
no authority to decide anything. Unit of analysis: a single advisory relationship
(the author, Sandra, and Pedro). New mechanism: Brehm's psychological reactance;
Schein's expert-vs-process distinction and Humble Inquiry, and the Schein/DEC
detail. Main case: Bombom (its sixth appearance in the book — see Overlap: Bombom
below).

### Chapter 21 — "Передача" (Transfer)

Unique function: closes the cycle by testing whether structure now carries its own
meaning independent of the founder. Unit of analysis: the whole organization
(TAK.Shtab) at a later point in its life. New mechanism: Selznick
(institutionalization); the Zoho/Vembu case. Main case: TAK.Shtab exclusively.
