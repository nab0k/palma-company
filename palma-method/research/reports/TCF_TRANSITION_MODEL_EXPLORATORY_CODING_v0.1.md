exactly the five TCF interview cards existing as of the 2026-08-16 cutoff:
SC-095, SC-096, SC-097, SC-098, SC-099. No interview collected after this

participant's real first name appears in the underlying source card (all

generic label instead, consistent with each card's own no-real-names rule.

**This is a descriptive, exploratory coding pass by a single coder (this
task), against five cases. It produces counts, not statistics, and supports
no significance test, no correlation, no generalization to "TCF members" as
a class, and no validation claim of any kind.**

## Coding rules, as preregistered (restated, not modified)

- *Unscripted participant-initiated action*: present if the interview
  describes a specific, named episode where the participant or another
  member took a consequential action toward another member (not Miguel/
  Margarida acting toward them, and not requested or organized by Miguel/
  Margarida), judged by a reasonable coder to carry real cost or commitment
  rather than purely social pleasantries. Ambiguous cases coded "unclear."
- *Continued peer relationship outside scheduled training*: present if the
  interview describes contact, activity, or relationship with another member
  occurring outside scheduled class time and not brokered by Miguel/Margarida
  in that specific instance.
- *Organizer-contact strength* and *peer-contact strength*: three-point
  low/medium/high, per the participant's own described frequency and depth
  of interaction, as preregistered.
- Every variable additionally receives an evidentiary-status tag - present /
  absent / unclear / not asked - reporting whether the interview actually
  contains evidence bearing on the question, independent of the substantive
  code. **"Not asked" is never collapsed into "absent."**

**Coding note, flagged per this task's explicit request to expose construct
problems where found:** this variable proved the hardest of the four to code
cleanly. In two of five cases (C, D), the interview material blurs the line
between "peer contact that happens to occur near/around training" and
"peer contact confirmed to occur independent of the training schedule" - the
construct as preregistered assumes this distinction is observable in
ordinary interview material, and for these two cases it was not cleanly
separable. This is reported as a genuine coding/construct problem, not
resolved by forcing a present/absent call where the evidence did not support
one.

## What the pattern does and does not show

Per this task's explicit four-way framing, the results are assessed against
all four options, not defaulted to the most favorable one:

- **Does it merely illustrate the proposed mechanism?** Partially, yes -
  Participant E, the project's designed contrast case, shows the clean
  "absent everywhere" pattern the model would predict for someone who never
  crosses the participant-initiated-action threshold, and the other four
  participants each show at least one concrete instance of unscripted,
  peer-directed action. This is illustration, not confirmation: with five
  cases, one of them a deliberately selected contrast case, this is exactly
  the kind of pattern a hand-picked illustrative set would be expected to
  show even if the underlying model were false.
- **Does it provide a recurring exploratory pattern worth further
  investigation?** Marginally - the co-occurrence of "unscripted action
  present" (4/5) with "peer-contact medium-or-higher" (4/5) is suggestive and
  worth a larger, less-selected sample, but two of the four "present" cases
  for Variable 1 (A and D) carry real confidence caveats (prompted recall;
  thinner detail), and Variable 2's construct ambiguity in two cases weakens
  how much weight the pattern can bear.
- **Does it weaken any proposition?** Not directly - no case in this set
  falsifies P1, P3, P4, or P5 as stated (each of those propositions predicts
  a plausible pattern, not a universal one, and this small, non-random set
  cannot rule out the specific configurations they warn against). P6 and P7
  (route-quality and infrastructure-as-mediator) are not addressed at all by
  this coding pass - the five interviews do not contain the
  episode-by-episode route-tracking detail those two propositions would
  require to test even exploratorily.
- **Does it expose a coding or construct problem?** Yes, specifically for
  Variable 2 (see the coding note above) - "continued peer relationship
  outside scheduled training" is not cleanly separable, in ordinary interview
  material about a gym, from "peer contact that happens to occur around
  training." Any future coding pass or instrument targeting this construct
  should ask about it more precisely than the current interview guide
  family does - e.g., asking directly "have you and this person ever met,
  messaged, or spoken specifically outside a class or the gym itself" rather
  than relying on general relationship descriptions to yield this distinction
  on their own.

**Explicit statement, per this task's own requirement: none of the above is
validation or proof of any kind.**

## Threats to inference, stated explicitly

- **Selection bias.** All five participants were recruited by the author,
  four of them (A, B, C, D) specifically because they had *some* connection
  to report, and the fifth (E) was specifically selected as a contrast case
  after already being known (informally, before the formal interview) to
  have low connectivity. This is not a random or representative sample of
  TCF's membership in any sense, and no claim in this document should be
  read as describing "TCF members" as a class.
- **Interviewer influence.** All five interviews were conducted by the same
  person (the author), who also authored the underlying model being tested
  here. Several source cards already flag specific instances of this risk
  directly (e.g., SC-095's own limitations field notes that a general
  reciprocity question initially produced a false-negative answer that only
  changed once the interviewer supplied a specific remembered episode - a
  real risk of leading the witness toward the answer the model predicts,
  even when, as in that case, the underlying episode was independently
  verifiable).
- **Retrospective reconstruction.** All five accounts are retrospective
  self-report, collected once, not observed in real time - none of the
  "unscripted action" episodes coded above was independently witnessed by
  anyone other than the participant recalling it.
- **Absence of independent coding.** This coding pass was performed by a
  single coder (this task), with no second, independent rater - the
  inter-rater reliability check the preregistration protocol's own spirit
  calls for (and which `PALMA_TRANSITION_MODEL_STRESS_TEST_v0.1.md`'s
  Proposition 2 explicitly names as a prerequisite before treating Palma
  Moment identification as reliable) has not been performed. Every "present"
  and "unclear" call above should be read as one coder's judgment, not an
  agreed classification.
