## Merchandise coverage

The current material supports four distinct observations, which must not be merged
into a single success story:

| Source | What is supported | What is not supported |
|---|---|---|
| SC-142 | Merchandise appears within a premium service/brand offering | That it creates relationships |
| SC-099 | Asking about T-shirts was one of few conversations extending beyond a greeting | That it changed the relationship |
| SC-152 | A color suggestion was reportedly incorporated, creating a topic and followed by a purchase | That feedback caused belonging, loyalty, or purchase |
| SC-046 | Miguel often asks participants for opinions about merchandise | Measured frequency, shared authority, or representative response |

The defensible construct is **low-stakes participation and conversational
infrastructure**. “Co-design,” “ownership,” and “community governance” overstate the
evidence.

## Sensitive post-loss sequence

### What the primary interview supports

SC-098 records that members helped the participant with grief and helped the family
of a person she had lost. It does not identify the person and does not document a dog,
T-shirt design, sale, proceeds, food purchase, or who initiated any action.

### What exists only as a restricted verification lead

Project conversations separately report care for the deceased person's dog and a
merchandise-related collection for food. Earlier manuscript versions v0.2–v0.4
contained a specific anonymous narrative. That old prose is not an independent source
and does not verify its own claims. Current v0.8/v0.9 correctly omit the episode.

### Required checks before any publication decision

1. Ask the interview participant separately whether even an anonymous loss episode
   may be used.
2. Obtain separate permissions for mentioning death, the dog, caregiving, a
   collection, merchandise, and use of proceeds; consent to one does not clear all.
3. Verify who proposed and produced the item, whether it was sold or donations were
   collected, how money was handled, what it bought, who cared for the dog, and dates.
4. Cross-check the sequence with Miguel and any other directly involved adult without
   disclosing more than necessary.
5. Do not name the deceased person or use a recognizable portrait/design without
   explicit authorization from the appropriate people.

Miguel Crespo is the founder/coach. Miguel Alves is a participant. Accounts of a

## Editorial decision

No new manuscript version is created by this audit. Merchandise is promising but
still better supported as an internal mechanism hypothesis than as a polished book
scene. The post-loss sequence remains publication-blocked. The research layer now
preserves both without forcing either into the book.
