# Palma Transition Model — Prior-Art Closure v0.1

Task: TASK_CLAUDE_0026, Stream 1
Owner: Claude
Status: draft for editorial review (Codex / Serhii)

Continues, and does not repeat, `PALMA_TRANSITION_MODEL_STRESS_TEST_v0.1.md`
(TASK_CLAUDE_0025), which already checked Wenger/McDermott/Snyder's (2002)
community-of-practice lifecycle, Knapp's (1978) dyadic relational stages, and
the community-organizing practice tradition against the candidate model. This
document closes the one gap that task explicitly flagged as under-researched:
network formation, network interventions, and organizer dependence, plus the
adjacent literatures named in this task's brief (relational-event models,
stochastic actor-oriented models, shared/distributed leadership, network
governance, relational coordination, founder succession).

## Method and its limits — stated plainly before any finding

This search used targeted queries against each named literature, followed by
attempts to retrieve primary full text for the most relevant papers found.
**Full-text primary retrieval was attempted for four papers and failed in
every case** - two returned HTTP errors (404, 403) from the hosting
repository, and two PDFs fetched successfully but could not be parsed into
readable text by this session's available tooling (both returned binary/font
data rather than article text). This is reported here rather than masked:
where full-text page-level quotation was not achieved, the qualification
below says so explicitly, and no source is presented as more thoroughly
verified than it actually was. One exception achieved genuine primary
verification: the Provan & Kenis (2008) abstract was retrieved directly from
the publisher's own page (Oxford Academic), not a secondary aggregator - this
is noted as the strongest-verified source in this batch.

**This is not a systematic review.** It is a bounded, targeted search across
eleven named sub-literatures, prioritizing the single most load-bearing paper
or book in each, not an exhaustive survey. "No equivalent found" below means
exactly that: a bounded search, honestly reported, not a claim of exhaustive
coverage.

## Sources checked

### 1. Relational event models

- **Source:** Butts, C. T. (2008). A Relational Event Framework for Social
  Action. *Sociological Methodology*, 38, 155-200.
  DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-9531.2008.00203.x.
- **Qualification:** citation and abstract-level content confirmed across
  multiple independent secondary sources (Wiley, SAGE, Semantic Scholar
  listings); full text requested from an openly-hosted PDF (ETH Zürich
  repository) and failed with HTTP 404. Not independently verified against
  primary full text - **checked, not verified**, in this project's own
  established vocabulary.
- **Relevance:** relational event models treat individual, timestamped
  dyadic actions - not aggregated ties - as the fundamental unit of analysis,
  with named mechanisms including "recency," "persistence," and
  "participation shifts." This is the closest formal-methodological match
  found anywhere in this search to the Palma candidate's core intuition that
  a specific, dateable action (a Palma Moment) is the right unit to track.
- **Does it already propose P6/P7 or the organizer-independence threshold?**
  No, on the evidence available (abstract-level and multiple independent
  secondary summaries) - REMs are a statistical modeling framework for
  analyzing *whatever* event-generating process a researcher specifies; they
  do not, in themselves, propose that organizer-independence is the marker of
  durable infrastructure, nor do they specify readiness to respond as an
  outcome. They supply a *method* the Palma model's P1-P2 could in principle
  be tested with (see the preregistration protocol's limits, Stream 2 below,
  and `PALMA_METHOD_FALSIFIABLE_PROPOSITIONS_v0.1.md`), not a competing
  *theory*.

### 2. Stochastic actor-oriented models (SAOM)

- **Source:** Snijders, T. A. B., van de Bunt, G. G., & Steglich, C. E. G.
  (2010). Introduction to stochastic actor-based models for network
  dynamics. *Social Networks*, 32(1), 44-60.
  DOI: 10.1016/j.socnet.2009.02.004.
- **Qualification:** citation confirmed across multiple independent sources;
  full text not requested in this pass (time-bounded search; SAOM's general
  character is well-established enough from the confirmed citation and this
  project's own background familiarity with the method family that a direct
  fetch was judged lower-priority than the REM and network-governance checks
  above and below). **Citation-level only - lower confidence than item 1.**
- **Relevance:** SAOMs model network evolution as a sequence of individual
  actors' tie-change decisions over discrete observation waves - a different,
  complementary formalism to REMs (waves vs. continuous events).
- **Does it already propose P6/P7 or the threshold?** No basis to claim it
  does, and no reason from its known general character to suspect it -
  SAOMs are, like REMs, a statistical modeling family, not a substantive
  theory of organizer-independence or infrastructure distribution.

### 3. Network interventions

- **Source:** Valente, T. W. (2012). Network Interventions. *Science*,
  337(6090), 49-53. DOI: 10.1126/science.1217330.
- **Qualification:** citation and a real, multiply-corroborated abstract
  confirmed (four intervention strategies: individual-based induction,
  segmentation, alteration; structural approaches). Full text requested from
  two sources (a hosting-institution PDF and PubMed's abstract page) - the
  PDF fetch failed on parsing (binary/font data returned instead of text) and
  the PubMed page returned only a cookie-consent notice. **Checked, not
  verified**, same tier as item 1.
- **Relevance:** this is the literature closest in *name* to what this task
  asked to check, but it turns out to be the least relevant in substance -
  Valente's "network interventions" are deliberate, researcher- or
  practitioner-designed manipulations of an existing network (e.g., choosing
  which individuals to target for a health-behavior campaign), not a
  descriptive account of how a network organically transitions from
  organizer-dependence to distributed peer infrastructure. The orientation is
  interventionist and outcome-driven (spread a specific behavior faster),
  where the Palma candidate is developmental and structure-focused (does the
  network's own shape change).
- **Does it already propose P6/P7 or the threshold?** No - different
  question entirely, on the evidence available.

### 4. Network governance

- **Source:** Provan, K. G., & Kenis, P. (2008). Modes of Network
  Governance: Structure, Management, and Effectiveness. *Journal of Public
  Administration Research and Theory*, 18(2), 229-252.
  DOI: 10.1093/jopart/mum015.
- **Qualification:** **abstract retrieved directly from the publisher's own
  page** (Oxford Academic) - the strongest verification tier achieved in this
  search. Full text remains paywalled and was not accessed. Publisher
  abstract, quoted directly: "This article examines the governance of
  organizational networks and the impact of governance on network
  effectiveness. Three basic models, or forms, of network governance are
  developed focusing on their distinct structural properties. Propositions
  are formulated examining conditions for the effectiveness of each form. The
  tensions inherent in each form are then discussed, followed by the role
  that management may play in addressing these tensions. Finally, the
  evolution of governance is explored."
- **Relevance:** this is the closest match found in this entire search for
  the *organizer-independence-as-threshold* half of the Palma candidate.
  Provan & Kenis's three governance forms - participant-governed (shared,
  no central actor), lead-organization-governed (one organization directs the
  network), and network-administrative-organization-governed (a separate
  entity coordinates) - map onto a spectrum of exactly the kind of
  organizer-centrality question P4 and P5 (in
  `PALMA_TRANSITION_MODEL_STRESS_TEST_v0.1.md`) ask about, and their own
  abstract confirms they discuss "the evolution of governance" - movement
  between these forms over time, which is structurally adjacent to a
  transition from organizer-dependence toward distribution.
- **Does it already propose P6/P7 or the threshold?** Not the specific
  dyadic mechanism (P6/P7 are about which *kind* of participant-initiated
  action produces durable routes) - Provan & Kenis operate entirely at the
  interorganizational network level and do not model a dyadic-level
  generative event at all. But their three-form typology and its "evolution"
  discussion is close enough to the *outcome* side of the Palma candidate
  (distributed vs. organizer-centered infrastructure) that it should be
  named as this search's closest new nearest-neighbor, alongside the three
  already found in TASK_CLAUDE_0025. **This does not overturn the "no
  equivalent found" verdict** - it sharpens it: the outcome-side concept
  (organizer- vs. distributed-governance) is well precedented; the
  dyadic-mechanism-to-outcome causal link the Palma candidate proposes is
  still not found anywhere in this search.

### 5. Relational coordination

- **Source:** Gittell, J. H., & Weiss, L. (2004). Coordinating Networks
  Within and Across Organizations: A Multi-level Framework. *Journal of
  Management Studies*. (Exact issue/page numbers and DOI not independently
  confirmed in this pass - flagged as citation-incomplete below.)
- **Qualification:** conceptual content confirmed across multiple
  independent secondary sources describing the broader Relational
  Coordination research program (Gittell and collaborators); the specific
  2004 paper's DOI and page range were not independently confirmed - **this
  citation should be treated as provisional pending a direct check** before
  any manuscript or academic-facing use.
- **Relevance:** Relational Coordination theory's central and most useful
  distinguishing fact for this comparison: its ties are explicitly
  **role-based, not person-based** - "not personal relationships of 'liking'
  ... but task-based relationship ties" existing "between work roles rather
  than between individual participants." This is a clean, checkable
  difference from the Palma candidate, which is explicitly about actions
  between specific, individual people.
- **Does it already propose P6/P7 or the threshold?** No - different unit of
  analysis entirely (role-to-role coordination quality, not person-to-person
  event-driven infrastructure formation), and no organizer-independence
  threshold is part of its core three-dimension model (shared goals, shared
  knowledge, mutual respect) on the evidence reviewed.

### 6. Shared and distributed leadership

- **Source:** Pearce, C. L., & Conger, J. A. (Eds.). (2003). *Shared
  Leadership: Reframing the Hows and Whys of Leadership*. Sage.
  DOI: 10.4135/9781452229539.
- **Qualification:** citation and general scope confirmed via multiple
  independent secondary sources (an edited volume covering shared
  leadership's dynamics, moderators, and measurement); no specific chapter
  was retrieved or checked at full-text level. **Checked, not verified**,
  same tier as items 1 and 3.
- **Relevance:** shared leadership examines the general phenomenon of
  leadership functions distributed across team members rather than
  concentrated in one appointed leader - relevant to the *outcome* side of
  the Palma candidate (distributed infrastructure) at the leadership-function
  level specifically, narrower than Palma's broader "routes of continuation."
- **Does it already propose P6/P7 or the threshold?** No basis found to
  claim it does - the literature (on the evidence reviewed) addresses
  whether and how leadership functions distribute, not the dyadic-event
  mechanism the Palma candidate proposes as the driver of that distribution.

## Literatures named in the brief but not separately re-searched

**Broker, hub, and organizer dependence**: not separately re-searched, since
this project's own corpus already independently and extensively covers this
exact literature (Burt 2004, Krackhardt & Hanson 1993, the Barabási-family
robustness/fragility research) - see
`PALMA_METHOD_PRIOR_ART_MATRIX_v0.1.md`, rows 7, 9, 14. Re-searching it here
would have duplicated existing, already-verified work rather than closing a
genuine gap.

**Community self-organization and lifecycle models**: not separately
re-searched, since TASK_CLAUDE_0025 already checked the single most directly
relevant model (Wenger, McDermott & Snyder 2002) in this exact category.

**Leadership turnover and founder succession**: a bounded search was
conducted (not a full literature review). Confirmed, via multiple
independent sources, that this is a real, active, somewhat fragmented
research area (nonprofit-sector founder succession specifically shows
"great inconsistency in previous theoretical and empirical studies," per one
literature-review source found) with a recurring finding directly relevant
to Proposition 6 (`PALMA_METHOD_FALSIFIABLE_PROPOSITIONS_v0.1.md`): boards of
founder-led organizations commonly show "insufficient independence from the
founder," and - a genuinely useful, specific finding for this project -
"transitions that extend the role of a nonprofit founder [rather than
removing them abruptly] yield the best results" (Stanford Social Innovation
Review, cited via secondary aggregation, not independently verified against
the primary SSIR article in this pass). This is consistent with, and does
not contradict, Proposition 6's own framing (structural features, not a
single dramatic founder-exit, as the predictor) - but no single named
academic model integrating founder-succession research with a dyadic
interaction mechanism was found.

**Community-organizing handoff**: already checked in TASK_CLAUDE_0025 (the
mobilization-to-turnover practitioner sequence); not re-searched here.

**Emergent organization and collective action**: Ostrom's institutional
design principles (already extensively covered in this project's corpus) and
Weick's sensemaking (already cited directly in the manuscript, Chapter 19)
are the closest matches; no additional primary source was newly checked in
this task specifically for this sub-literature, since both are already
well-established in this project's existing work.

## Verdict

**No equivalent integrated model was found.** This search adds one genuine
new nearest-neighbor to the three already found in TASK_CLAUDE_0025 -
Provan & Kenis's (2008) network-governance typology, which is the closest
match found anywhere, across both tasks, for the *organizer-independence-as-
threshold* half of the candidate model specifically. It does not supply, and
no source checked in this task supplies, the dyadic mechanism (a
consequential, participant-initiated action producing a durable route) as
the proposed *driver* of that transition. Relational event models (item 1)
are the closest match for the *unit of analysis* (a discrete, dateable
action) but are a statistical method, not a substantive theory making this
claim.

**P6 and P7, specifically:** neither proposition (that route-forming quality
matters more than raw reciprocal-action frequency, and that infrastructure
mediates the reciprocal-action-to-readiness relationship) was found stated,
in this form or a close reformulation, in any source checked in this task.
Both remain, on the evidence gathered here, genuinely new and untested - not
contradicted by anything found, and not already established by anything
found either.

**This finding does not upgrade TASK_CLAUDE_0025's selected position**
(defensible conceptual novelty with untested propositions) **to a stronger
one.** If anything, it sharpens the case for staying at that position rather
than moving toward it being questioned: the search found real, substantive
adjacent literatures (network governance, relational event models,
relational coordination) that a careful reviewer would expect this project
to have checked before claiming novelty, and checking them did not surface a
prior equivalent - but it also did not surface any new evidence *for* the
model beyond conceptual adjacency, since none of these sources report
empirical tests of anything resembling P6 or P7.
