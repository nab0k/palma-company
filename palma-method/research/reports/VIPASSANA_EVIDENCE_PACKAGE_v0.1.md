# Vipassana evidence package v0.1

Stream 3 of `TASK_CLAUDE_0030`. `deep-research-report 1708.md` (supplied by
the founder on 2026-08-17, made accessible to this session via a folder
grant) was read as a discovery dossier, per instruction and per
`TASK_CODEX_0029`'s own external-research-intake boundary — not treated as
evidence in itself. Its key primary and academic sources were checked; see
"Independent verification performed" below for exactly what was and was not
re-confirmed directly by this task, rather than accepted from the dossier's
own citation trail.

## Independent verification performed

Six of the dossier's load-bearing claims were independently checked via
direct search in this task (not merely re-stated from the dossier):

1. UK Vipassana Trust's FY2025 financials (Charity No. 327798) — matched exactly (£1.423m income, £1.110m expenditure).
2. The 1969 first course (Bombay, 3-13 July, 14 participants) — matched.
3. Late-1981 assistant-teacher appointments and the March 1982 first assistant-teacher-led course (Bodh Gaya) — matched.
4. Pagis (2015) citation and core finding — matched, already carded (`SC-051`).
5. The Sayagyi U Ba Khin Village (bungalow units, committee governance, weekly courses) — matched.
6. The worldwide directory scale (~270 centres / 132 non-centre locations, August 2026) — an independent secondary source found ~261 centres / 130+ non-centres as of April 2025; consistent with the dossier's figure but not independently re-confirmed to the exact day or count.

The remainder of the dossier's citation table (Rahmani, Spirkina, Krygier et
al., Perelman et al., Bowen et al., Britton et al., Farias et al.) was not
independently re-verified line by line in this pass. These sources concern
meditation's clinical/psychological effects and religious-identity framing
generally, not the organizational mechanism Chapter 1 actually needs (per
DECISION_0007's own scope: "a case of a standardized event connected to
volunteer pathways, local organizations, durable assets, recorded knowledge,
and delegated roles") — consistent with instruction 10's caution against
padding, this report does not recommend carding them for Chapter 1's use
and flags this as a deliberate scope decision, not an oversight.

## The nine topics, each classified

### 1. Goenka chronology

**Verified fact** (independently confirmed, see above): first course Bombay
1969 (14 participants); first course outside India, France 1979; first
permanent centre, Dhamma Giri, first course November 1976; assistant
teachers appointed from late 1981; first assistant-teacher-led course March
1982, Bodh Gaya. **Open proposition, explicitly not claimed:** that Goenka
taught an "unchanged 2,500-year-old technique" — this is the tradition's own
lineage claim; historians of modern Buddhism (Erik Braun, cited in the
dossier, not independently re-verified by this task) describe substantial
modern Burmese-era systematization instead.

### 2. Permanent centres

**Verified fact:** Dhamma Giri (1976) was the first permanent centre,
replacing a prior pattern of rented/temporary venues. **Supported
interpretation:** a permanent centre reduces the need to rebuild logistics,
venue, and routine from scratch each time — the dossier's own analytical
point, adopted here as reasonable but not independently tested.

### 3. Recorded teaching and assistant teachers

**Verified fact:** systematic full-course audiovisual recording began May
1982 (continuing to 1986), explicitly stated by VRI as intended to "maintain
uniformity." Assistant teachers deliver courses using these recordings
under a delegated-authorization system. **Supported interpretation:** this
is a clean, documented example of "founder knowledge → codification →
recording → delegated local operator" as a scaling mechanism — the
dossier's own framing, which this report treats as well-supported by the
sequence of dated facts rather than as an independently modeled causal
claim.

### 4. Student → old student → server pathway

**Verified fact:** completing a course confers "old student" status, which
is the documented eligibility gate for course service (kitchen, management,
maintenance, and other roles), per multiple centres' own published service
pages (dossier's citation trail, not independently re-verified line by line
in this task). **Open proposition:** the actual conversion rate — what
share of first-time students ever become servers — is not established by
any study the dossier or this task located. The manuscript must not write
"students become volunteers" as a general claim; the accurate form is that
completing a course makes a person *eligible* for service, per the
dossier's own recommended phrasing, adopted here.

### 5. Donations, assets, volunteers, and paid labour

**Verified fact** (independently confirmed): the UK Vipassana Trust's
FY2025 filing reports £1.423m income (the large majority from donations and
legacies), £1.110m expenditure, and paid employees alongside volunteer
labour — directly disproving any claim that the system runs on volunteers
alone, at least in this jurisdiction. **Supported interpretation:** "no fee"
and "no funding" are not the same claim; the system is real, well-funded,
and asset-holding, financed through a donation loop rather than
point-of-participation pricing.

### 6. Local legal entities within a standardized transnational system

**Verified fact** (via the same UK filing): the Vipassana Trust is a
registered charity with its own trustees, committees, and financial
reporting obligations — a local legal structure, not a branch office of one
global corporation. **Supported interpretation, not independently verified
in full:** the dossier reports (not independently re-checked by this task
beyond the UK case) that Spain shows a similarly local structure, supporting
the general pattern of "centralized teaching content, decentralized legal
ownership and operations" — this report treats the pattern as plausible on
the strength of one directly-confirmed jurisdiction, not as independently
established across the whole network.

### 7. Pagis and silent sociality

**Verified fact:** Pagis (2015), already carded as `SC-051`, is a real,
correctly-described, peer-reviewed study finding a "sociality of
non-engagement" — coordinated, mutually-attuned silent co-presence — among
Vipassana retreat participants in Israel and the US. **Open proposition,
explicitly not established by this or any study found:** that this
attunement becomes a durable interpersonal tie. The manuscript's own
"sea guy" scene is a single, vivid, confirmed instance — not evidence that
the mechanism is typical or reliable.

### 8. Confirmed residential community beside Dhamma Giri

**Verified fact** (independently confirmed): the Sayagyi U Ba Khin Village,
beside Dhamma Giri, is a real, purpose-built residential settlement (45
bungalow-style units, a community centre, resident committees, weekly
one-day courses), established 1999. **Open proposition, explicitly
rejected:** that such residential communities are typical of the worldwide
network — no comparative evidence was found (by the dossier or by this
task) supporting that generalization, and the manuscript must not imply
"Vipassana villages" are a general pattern.

### 9. Safety and limits

**Verified fact:** the movement's own Code of Discipline states directly
that Vipassana is not a substitute for medical or psychiatric treatment and
that the organization, as an unpaid-volunteer-reliant body, is not equipped
to handle some serious psychiatric conditions — this is the movement's own
stated limitation, not an external critique. **Supported interpretation:**
a 2024 *Financial Times* investigative series documented participant
accounts of adverse psychological effects and raised questions about
screening and aftercare — real, citable journalism, but not peer-reviewed
prevalence research; it should be cited as documented external criticism,
not as an established risk rate. **Open proposition, explicitly not
claimed:** any specific adverse-event prevalence rate for Goenka-tradition
courses specifically — the clinical literature the dossier surveyed
concerns different populations (prisons, general mindfulness programs) and
does not support a Goenka-specific number.

## Claims chapter 1 can safely make, sorted by epistemic category

| Category | Claim |
|---|---|
| **Verified fact** | The retreat format, funding structure (no fee, donation-financed), and role pathway (student → eligible-for-service → server) are real, documented, and — for the UK jurisdiction specifically — independently confirmed against public regulatory filings. |
| **Verified fact** | Silence does not equal social absence — Pagis's peer-reviewed finding, matching the author's own "sea guy" account structurally. |
| **Supported interpretation** | The combination of codified rules, recordings, delegated teaching authority, a service pathway, donations, permanent assets, and local legal entities is what makes the system reproducible without Goenka's ongoing personal presence — a reasonable synthesis of dated, documented facts, not an independently modeled causal test. |
| **Palma synthesis** | Framing the retreat as "an entry point into a mechanism, not the mechanism itself" is this book's own analytical move, applying language the dossier itself half-suggests but does not use in exactly this form. |
| **Open proposition, must not be stated as fact** | That a retreat creates community; that most students become volunteers; that the technique is unchanged since antiquity; that the practice is simply non-religious; that residential Vipassana villages are typical; that any specific harm rate applies to Goenka courses specifically. |

## What this chapter should not claim (carried forward from the dossier's own discipline)

Do not write: "Vipassana is free" (say: no course fee; financed through
donations, assets, and volunteer labour). Do not write: "the system runs
without funding" (it does not rely on compulsory fees, which is different).
Do not write: "everything is run by volunteers" (at least the UK Trust
reports paid staff too). Do not write: "a retreat creates community" (say:
a retreat creates structured repeated co-presence from which some
relationships and communities may develop — an important, deliberately
weaker claim). Do not write: "students become volunteers" (say: completing
a course makes a person eligible for service; the actual conversion rate is
not established).
