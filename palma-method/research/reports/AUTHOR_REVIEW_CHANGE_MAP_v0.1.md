## Approved wording anchors

These are working anchors accepted in discussion, not canonical manuscript text.
They may be adjusted locally during the full pass without weakening their meaning.

### Palma Method status

Palma Method is currently a working method of observation and intervention, not a
proven technology for building relationships. It combines established research,
field observations, and testable hypotheses. Its full cycle has not yet been tested
as one validated system.

### TCF causality

If TCF participants increasingly meet outside the gym, that does not demonstrate
Palma effectiveness: Palma has not yet been applied to the system. Schedule,
founder participation, existing relationships, moves, chance encounters, and other
causes remain live alternatives.

### Diagnosis

The author arrived at the enlistment office with a ready solution — a weapon. The
real question was where his actual skills could be useful. Organizations similarly
often arrive with a selected answer (a chat, event, community, or role) before the
problem has been established.

## Palma Infrastructure Canvas audit

### Current coverage

The Canvas appears in three appropriate places:

1. Chapter 18 introduces it as the artifact produced during Design.
2. The Chapter 18 TAK.Shtab reconstruction fills all five fields retrospectively
   and correctly states that the Canvas did not cause the historical result.
3. Appendix 1 repeats the five definitions and the validation limit.

The manuscript currently defines the five fields consistently:

- routes — how people find or reach one another without a fresh introduction by
  the founder;
- roles — what participants can do beyond receive or watch;
- rhythms — what repeats without a fresh call;
- channels — where interaction actually continues;
- memory — what the system can preserve and use without a new explanation from
  the center.

The scientific boundary is also honest: the five-part combination is Palma's own
operational grouping, not a validated scale or a literature-established single
model.

### Verdict

The reader can understand what the Canvas is for, but cannot yet confidently use
it without outside help. The book supplies definitions and a retrospective story,
not a fillable working procedure.

Main usability gaps:

1. `Route` and `channel` remain easy to confuse. A route is the path connecting
   people; a channel is the medium or place in which their interaction continues.
   The book needs one paired example.
2. `Memory` remains abstract. It needs examples such as a shared log, a repeated
   practice, an instruction the group can apply, or knowledge carried by more than
   one person.
3. The reader is not shown when to fill the Canvas, at what unit of analysis, or
   how to revise it after Launch.
4. There is no blank one-page template with prompts and observable evidence.
5. The retrospective TAK.Shtab example helps interpretation but does not show how
   to design prospectively under uncertainty.
6. Chapter 13's six design elements and the Canvas's five fields remain a cognitive
   burden. The caveat that one does not replace the other is accurate but not yet
   easy to use; a visible mapping or a simplified single vocabulary is needed.

### Required upgrade before publication

Add a one-page worksheet to Appendix 1. For each field, the reader should record:

| Field | Current condition | Proposed minimum change | Observable sign it was used | Risk or ethical limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Route | | | | |
| Role | | | | |
| Rhythm | | | | |
| Channel | | | | |
| Memory | | | | |

Instructions should say explicitly:

1. Complete the Canvas only after fixing the diagnosed unit and desired change.
2. Fill it for one bounded intervention, not for the whole organization.
3. Treat every field as a hypothesis until observed in use.
4. Revisit it after Launch and mark what functioned, what depended on the founder,
   and what should be stopped or redesigned.

A prospective worked example may use the hypothetical TCF newcomer intervention
already accepted during review: an opt-in introduction after one class, a volunteer
host role, a recurring coffee window, a clearly bounded channel, and a privacy-safe
way for the group to retain what it learned. It must remain explicitly hypothetical
until actually run.
