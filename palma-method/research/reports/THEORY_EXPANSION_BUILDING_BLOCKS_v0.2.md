## Why this version exists and why it has 7 items, not 8-12

`THEORY_EXPANSION_PLACEMENT_MAP_v0.2.md` re-verified all 11 concepts against
`MANUSCRIPT_SNAPSHOT_2026-08-15.md` (the sole authoritative current manuscript
text - not the stale `manuscript/part-*` files v0.1 was built from) and found that
6 of the 11 concepts are already fully resourced with no genuine gap, most
significantly Craig (1999) and Feld (1981), both of which v0.1 wrongly treated as
absent from Chapters 1 and 5 when they are in fact fully and explicitly cited
there. That leaves 7 items carrying a nonzero recommended word count. The original
task brief asked for 8-12 items; this version reports 7 because that is the honest
number that survived verification against the real text, not because of a
different selection standard. Manufacturing an 8th item to hit the range would be
the same error this whole repair task exists to correct.

Each item uses the same ten fields as v0.1: concept; home chapter; anchor point in
existing text; core academic finding; citation; mechanism; study design and
confidence; permissible claim; forbidden claim; raw facts for drafting. Anchor
points below cite `MANUSCRIPT_SNAPSHOT_2026-08-15.md` line numbers directly.

## Block 1

1. **Concept:** Social/relational capital - legitimizing precedent for combining
   structural and relational analysis
2. **Home chapter:** Chapter 6 (Диалог ни о чём длиной в двадцать шесть лет)
3. **Anchor point:** Line 182, where the chapter states that the fifth dimension
   (network structure) "нельзя увидеть внутри одной пары. Для него приходится
   отойти дальше" (can't be seen within a single pair - you have to step back) -
   immediately after listing the four dimensions visible within one relationship
   at line 181.
4. **Core academic finding:** Social capital can usefully be separated into
   structural (network configuration), relational (trust, norms, obligations
   built through interaction history), and cognitive (shared language,
   narratives) dimensions.
5. **Citation:** Nahapiet, J., & Ghoshal, S. (1998). Social Capital, Intellectual
   Capital, and the Organizational Advantage. Academy of Management Review,
   23(2), 242-266.
6. **Mechanism:** Not causal - a legitimizing academic precedent for treating
   network-level structure and relationship-level quality as dimensions of one
   framework, which is exactly the move the chapter already makes at lines
   181-182 without citing anyone who has done it before.
7. **Study design and confidence:** Theoretical/conceptual synthesis, not an
   original empirical study. Status: checked, not verified (unchanged from
   TASK_CLAUDE_0022's original assessment - this source's status is not affected
   by the manuscript-reading correction).
8. **Permissible claim:** Combining a dimension assessed at the level of a single
   relationship with a dimension assessed at the level of the whole portfolio, in
   one framework, is an established academic move, not a Palma invention.
9. **Forbidden claim:** That Nahapiet & Ghoshal's three dimensions are the same
   as, or validate, Palma's five dimensions - the paper was built for
   organizational knowledge creation specifically.
10. **Raw facts for drafting:**
    - Three named dimensions: structural, relational, cognitive.
    - The paper's own application: organizational knowledge creation, not general
      relationship diagnosis.
    - Widely cited as a bridge between classical sociological social-capital
      theory (Bourdieu, Coleman - both already cited elsewhere in this
      manuscript) and organizational/management research.

## Block 2

1. **Concept:** Social/relational capital - terminology positioning (academic
   "relational capital" vs. commercial "Relationship Capital")
2. **Home chapter:** Chapter 6
3. **Anchor point:** Lines 190-191, where the chapter already interrogates the
   word "капитал" itself (implies ownership it doesn't have; implies fungibility
   relationships don't have) - a natural place to also acknowledge that closely
   related terms are already in academic and commercial use elsewhere.
4. **Core academic finding:** "Relational capital" is an established academic
   term in interorganizational strategic-alliance research, denoting trust and
   interaction between alliance partners - narrower than, and predating, this
   book's own construct. Separately, "RELATIONSHIP CAPITAL" is a registered U.S.
   trademark (Serial No. 77198983), owned by The Nour Group, Inc.
5. **Citation:** Kale, P., Singh, H., & Perlmutter, H. (2000). Learning and
   Protection of Proprietary Assets in Strategic Alliances: Building Relational
   Capital. Strategic Management Journal, 21(3), 217-237. Nour, D. (2023).
   Relationship Economics (3rd ed.). Wiley. USPTO trademark record,
   "RELATIONSHIP CAPITAL," Serial No. 77198983, The Nour Group, Inc.
6. **Mechanism:** Not causal - a terminology-positioning fact. Three distinct uses
   of closely related terms currently coexist without acknowledgment anywhere in
   the manuscript: the academic term (narrow, alliance-specific), the commercial
   term (a registered trademark), and this book's own broader construct.
7. **Study design and confidence:** Kale et al.: large-sample survey study,
   verified via independent WebSearch. Nour trademark: institutional/registry
   record, verified via independent WebSearch. (Both unchanged from
   TASK_CLAUDE_0022's original assessment.)
8. **Permissible claim:** This book's "Relationship Capital" is a broader
   practitioner synthesis that draws on, but is not identical to, either the
   narrower academic term or Nour's commercial one.
9. **Forbidden claim:** That this book coined "Relationship Capital" or that its
   use of the phrase is legally unencumbered - the trademark finding specifically
   argues against using the exact phrase as a primary book title or brand name
   without separate legal clearance review (not performed here, outside this
   project's scope).
10. **Raw facts for drafting:**
    - Kale et al.'s "relational capital": mutual trust and interaction between
      alliance partners, combined with integrative conflict management, lets
      firms learn from partners while protecting proprietary knowledge.
    - Nour's "Relationship Economics" is in its 3rd edition (Wiley, 2023).
    - "RELATIONSHIP CAPITAL" trademark: Serial No. 77198983, The Nour Group,
      Inc., registered for consulting services.
    - No legal opinion on enforceability is offered by this project - a
      professional trademark-clearance review is a separate, unaddressed task.

## Block 3

1. **Concept:** Interaction rituals - emotional-energy measurement caution
2. **Home chapter:** Chapter 5 (Событие - не инфраструктура)
3. **Anchor point:** Line 151, where the chapter already states, in its own
   voice, that the fourth ritual condition (shared emotional mood) "нельзя
   установить по расписанию или фотографии после занятия" (can't be established
   from a schedule or a photo after the session) - Baker's academic caution would
   back this existing sentence, not introduce a new idea.
4. **Core academic finding:** Collins's emotional-energy construct is
   theoretically well-developed but remains ahead of its empirical
   operationalization; existing measurement approaches capture only partial,
   imperfect proxies for the underlying construct.
5. **Citation:** Baker, W. (2019). Emotional Energy, Relational Energy, and
   Organizational Energy: Toward a Multilevel Theory. Annual Review of
   Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior, 6, 373-395.
6. **Mechanism:** Not causal - a measurement-validity caution that the manuscript
   already independently expresses in its own words at line 151. Baker's review
   would let the chapter attribute this caution to an academic source instead of
   only the author's own judgment.
7. **Study design and confidence:** Systematic literature review. Status:
   checked, not verified (unchanged from TASK_CLAUDE_0022's original assessment).
8. **Permissible claim:** A visible behavioral sign is a reasonable observable
   signal consistent with emotional energy having occurred - not proof, a point
   the manuscript already makes independently.
9. **Forbidden claim:** That any specific observed reaction at TCF, Bombom, or
   any other case in this project constitutes measured or proven emotional
   energy in Collins's technical sense.
10. **Raw facts for drafting:**
    - Baker (2019) is a peer-reviewed review article synthesizing the emotional/
      relational/organizational-energy literature.
    - Its conclusion on Collins's construct specifically: theoretically rich,
      empirically under-operationalized.
    - This is the lowest-priority item in this document - genuinely optional,
      since the manuscript already protects itself against the overclaim this
      source would guard against.

## Block 4

1. **Concept:** Community of practice - sharper "practice" test
2. **Home chapter:** Chapter 2 (Когда люди приходят друг к другу)
3. **Anchor point:** Lines 76-77, immediately after the domain/community/practice
   triad is introduced and before it is applied to the Kyiv office case - the
   natural place for a compact, transferable test a reader could apply to their
   own ambiguous case.
4. **Core academic finding:** A recurring group qualifies as a genuine community
   of practice only where members visibly exchange know-how, build a shared
   repertoire together, and help newcomers develop competence - mere recurring
   co-presence plus friendliness does not qualify.
5. **Citation:** Lave, J., & Wenger, E. (1991). Situated Learning: Legitimate
   Peripheral Participation. Cambridge University Press. Wenger, E. (1998).
   Communities of Practice: Learning, Meaning, and Identity. Cambridge University
   Press.
6. **Mechanism:** The "practice" leg specifically requires an evolving, shared,
   transmissible repertoire that members actively build and pass on - not simply
   shared interest (domain) or repeated contact (community), both of which the
   chapter already tests for at lines 76-77.
7. **Study design and confidence:** Ethnographic and case-based qualitative
   research. Status: verified (citation chain upgraded to official Cambridge
   University Press DOI records in TASK_CLAUDE_0022; unaffected by this repair).
8. **Permissible claim:** A recurring group is not automatically a community of
   practice simply because members show up regularly and get along - it requires
   visible knowledge exchange and a growing shared repertoire.
9. **Forbidden claim:** That any specific named case in this project (TCF,
   Bombom, the Kyiv office) has been formally tested against and confirmed to
   pass this sharper test - any such claim would need to trace to a specific
   interview, not a general theoretical illustration.
10. **Raw facts for drafting:**
    - Worked illustrative example (from the source dossier, generic, not
      case-specific): a training gym qualifies where shared technique/know-how
      exchange between members is visible.
    - Worked illustrative example: a café's regulars do not qualify merely by
      being regulars - no shared repertoire is being built.
    - Worked illustrative example: a volunteer logistics team is plausible but
      can dissolve before a stable practice forms.
    - The chapter's own cases (Kyiv office, Harley-Davidson HOG) already show
      domain and community; the missing piece is a general test for the third
      leg specifically, not another worked example.

## Block 5

1. **Concept:** Network structure - redundancy as concentration-risk mitigation
2. **Home chapter:** Chapter 9 (Если убрать одну точку)
3. **Anchor point:** Line 317, where the chapter already recommends, as one of
   several practical responses to a single-point-of-failure diagnosis:
   "Познакомить людей напрямую... Создать второй канал в важный круг" (introduce
   people directly... build a second channel into an important circle) - stated
   as design judgment, with no empirical backing cited.
4. **Core academic finding:** When a boundary-spanning, client-facing manager
   leaves a firm, a client is more likely to remain with the firm than follow the
   departing manager - and this default is significantly strengthened when the
   client also has an independent, genuine relationship with a second person
   inside the same firm.
5. **Citation:** Bermiss, Y. S., & Greenbaum, B. E. (2016). Loyal to Whom? The
   Effect of Relational Embeddedness and Managers' Mobility on Market Tie
   Dissolution. Administrative Science Quarterly, 61(2), 254-290.
6. **Mechanism:** A second genuine internal relationship measurably increases the
   odds a client stays after the primary contact's departure - a direct,
   evidenced version of the redundancy Chapter 9 already recommends by design
   judgment alone.
7. **Study design and confidence:** Longitudinal, full-population observational
   study of Texas state-level lobbyists and clients, 2001-2009. Status: verified,
   independently spot-checked via WebSearch in TASK_CLAUDE_0022 (unaffected by
   this repair).
8. **Permissible claim:** Building a second, genuine internal relationship with
   an important external party is a specific, evidenced way to reduce the
   concentration risk this chapter's "test of removal" is designed to surface.
9. **Forbidden claim:** That this Texas-lobbying-industry finding has been
   directly measured in publishing, TCF, Bombom, or any other case named in this
   book - extending it there remains an analogy, not a direct finding.
10. **Raw facts for drafting:**
    - Default finding: clients are more likely to stay with the firm than follow
      a departing manager.
    - Moderator: a second, independent, genuine internal relationship increases
      the odds a client stays.
    - Single-industry (lobbying), observational, not experimental - no random
      assignment of which managers leave.
    - This citation would strengthen, not introduce, a recommendation the
      chapter already makes in its own voice.

## Block 6

1. **Concept:** Dark side of social capital - the tautology caution
2. **Home chapter:** Chapter 10 (Кого не познакомили)
3. **Anchor point:** Line 326, the chapter's own opening sentence: "Изучение
   социального капитала долго тяготело к перечислению его выгод" (the study of
   social capital long tended toward listing its benefits) - immediately before
   Portes's four-part typology is introduced.
4. **Core academic finding:** Part of Portes's critical contribution is
   conceptual discipline against defining social capital *by its good outcomes*
   - if social capital is defined as whatever produces good outcomes, the claim
   that social capital produces good outcomes becomes circular.
5. **Citation:** Portes, A. (1998). Social Capital: Its Origins and Applications
   in Modern Sociology. Annual Review of Sociology, 24, 1-24. (Already cited in
   this chapter; this sharpens the existing citation, it is not a new source.)
6. **Mechanism:** Logical/definitional, not empirical - a caution about circular
   reasoning in how a construct is defined and then tested.
7. **Study design and confidence:** Critical literature review and conceptual
   synthesis. Status: verified_via_primary_source_pdf_and_secondary_summaries
   (unchanged).
8. **Permissible claim:** Social capital should not be defined circularly as
   "whatever produces good outcomes" - naming this risk explicitly sharpens the
   chapter's own opening framing.
9. **Forbidden claim:** That this reframing adds a fifth item to Portes's
   four-part typology - it does not; it is a definitional caution about how the
   field talked about social capital before Portes's corrective, not a fifth
   downside.
10. **Raw facts for drafting:**
    - The tautology risk: defining a construct by its claimed good effects, then
      citing those effects as proof the construct is good.
    - This sharpens the chapter's own opening sentence about the field's
      "boosters," rather than adding new material.
