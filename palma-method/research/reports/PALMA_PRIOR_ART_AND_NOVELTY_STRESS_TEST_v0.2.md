# Palma Prior-Art and Novelty Stress Test v0.2

## What this document is, and what it is not

This is v0.2 of a stress test that continues, and deliberately does not
repeat, three earlier prior-art documents already in this repository:
`PALMA_METHOD_PRIOR_ART_MATRIX_v0.1.md` (TASK_CLAUDE_0024, component-by-
component matrix for the Method's tools), `PALMA_METHOD_NOVELTY_AND_
SCIENTIFIC_STATUS_AUDIT_v0.1.md` (TASK_CLAUDE_0025, scientific-status
classification of the Method's propositions), and `PALMA_TRANSITION_MODEL_
PRIOR_ART_CLOSURE_v0.1.md` (TASK_CLAUDE_0026, a targeted closure search on
network formation and organizer dependence). Readers who want component-
by-component detail should use those three documents; this one runs the
whole eight-step book sequence end-to-end against everything found across
all four tasks (0024/0025/0026/0031) plus this task's own new sweep of 11
sources, and updates the "formulations to avoid" list accordingly. Nothing
in the three earlier documents is revised or retracted here.

## The sequence under test

сообщение (message) → встречное действие (counter-action, the Palma
Moment) → повторяющееся взаимодействие (repeated interaction) → отношения
(relationships) → сеть (network) → сообщество (community) → инфраструктура
отношений (relational infrastructure) → способность продолжать действие
без постоянного толчка организатора (continuation without the founder).

## Step-by-step check against the nearest existing models

### Step 1→2: message to counter-action (the Palma Moment)

**Nearest models:** Collins (2004) interaction ritual chains (already
carded); Gouldner's norm of reciprocity (already carded); relational event
models (Butts 2008, checked in TASK_CLAUDE_0026, citation-level only,
never independently primary-verified). **[Q1]** What is known: a
substantial literature exists on reciprocal and responsive action generally
(reciprocity norms, turn-taking in conversation analysis, helping behavior,
voice), and on the emotional-energy payoff of successful ritual interaction
(Collins). **[Q2]** How the Palma Moment differs: none of reciprocity,
responsiveness, turn-taking, helping behavior, voice, or prosocial behavior
is defined as a single, dateable, *consequential* event that changes what
either side subsequently expects - they are behavioral patterns or norms,
not diagnostic single-event markers. Relational event models supply the
closest *formal apparatus* for treating a single action as the analytic
unit, but (per TASK_CLAUDE_0026's own finding) do not themselves propose
which events are "meaningful" the way a Palma Moment claims to identify.
**Overlap:** substantial, at the level of underlying behavioral and
emotional mechanisms. **Difference:** the specific "one countable, dateable
event with recognized before/after consequences" framing is not matched by
a named predecessor. **Surviving novelty:** an original diagnostic naming
device built on established mechanisms (classification 3/1 combined, per
the existing Prior-Art Matrix, row 5) - unchanged by this task's sweep.

### Step 2→3: counter-action to repeated interaction

**Nearest models:** Feld (1981) social foci; Collins (2004) interaction
ritual chains. **[Q3]** When repeated co-presence becomes a relationship
rather than mere neighboring: per this task's discipline-4 finding
(`ADJACENT_DISCIPLINES_FOR_PALMA_v0.1.md`), the clearest available marker
is Collins's bar for a successful ritual - shared focus of attention, a
boundary against outsiders, and mutual emotional entrainment that produces
symbols and solidarity outlasting the specific encounter. Feld's social
foci explain the *opportunity structure* (why repeated co-presence happens
at all - shared schedules, places, activities) but not the tipping point
itself. **Overlap/difference:** unchanged from prior tasks' findings - both
sources already correctly cited in the manuscript. **Surviving novelty:**
none claimed at this step; Palma correctly presents this as direct
application of established theory (classification 1, per the existing
Prior-Art Matrix, rows 12-13).

### Step 3→4: repeated interaction to relationships, and step 4→5: relationships to network

**Nearest models:** Granovetter (weak ties), Burt (structural holes),
Heider-Simmel (balance theory), Krackhardt & Hanson (multiple informal
networks). **[Q4]** Mechanisms translating individual relationships to
network/community level: tie aggregation (many dyads forming a graph),
transitivity/triadic closure (balance theory), and brokerage (Burt) are
the established mechanisms; this task's sweep adds Wegner's transactive
memory (SC-128) as a mechanism specifically for what a *group*, not just a
dyad, retains once relationships aggregate (a distributed retrieval index),
and McCarthy & Zald's resource mobilization (SC-132) as the mechanism by
which aggregated relationships convert into organized collective capacity.
**Overlap:** high for tie-aggregation and brokerage mechanics (already well
covered). **Difference:** neither Wegner nor McCarthy & Zald was previously
carded in this project; both are genuine, non-duplicative additions.
**Surviving novelty:** unchanged - Palma's contribution here remains
adaptation/recombination (classification 2), not new mechanism discovery.

### Step 5→6: network to community

**Nearest models:** Wenger (1991/1998, communities of practice); McMillan
& Chavis (1986, sense of community, SC-120, added TASK_CLAUDE_0030); Muñiz
& O'Guinn (2001, brand community, SC-121, added TASK_CLAUDE_0030); Swann et
al. (2009, identity fusion, SC-130, added this task). **This remains, as
TASK_CLAUDE_0030 found and this task confirms, the corpus's single weakest
theoretical link** - not because sources are missing (four independent,
partially-overlapping definitional tests now exist) but because
DECISION_0007 itself still reserves the final operational choice among
them, including the three-person threshold question, as an open Founder
decision. This task's addition (identity fusion) sharpens rather than
resolves the gap: it adds a second *axis* (how personally, individually-
directed is the commitment) rather than a competing definition, which
means the corpus now has more raw material for the Founder's decision but
not a resolution of it. **Surviving novelty:** none claimed - this is
explicitly the sequence's most exposed point, and the manuscript should
not imply the community-definition question has been settled by research
when it has been settled, if at all, only by editorial choice.

### Step 6→7: community to relational infrastructure

**Nearest models:** Ostrom (1990, commons governance); Provan & Kenis
(2008, network governance typology - checked TASK_CLAUDE_0026, strongest-
verified source in that closure search, publisher abstract directly
retrieved); this task adds Valente (2012, network interventions, SC-133)
and Kraut/Resnick/Kiesler (2012, online-community design, SC-134).
**Important correction carried forward from TASK_CLAUDE_0026 and
reinforced in this task:** Valente's framework, on closer reading, is
interventionist and outcome-driven (deliberately engineering faster spread
of a specific behavior), not a descriptive account of organic
infrastructure formation - it is useful Design-stage vocabulary, not
evidence that Palma's own developmental sequence has been independently
validated. This distinction is now recorded in both the Valente source
card (updated this task) and here, so it cannot be silently lost in later
editing. **Overlap:** Provan & Kenis's governance-form typology remains the
closest match anywhere in this project's research for the organizer-
dependence-to-distribution outcome. **Surviving novelty:** unchanged from
TASK_CLAUDE_0026's verdict - no integrated model combining a dyadic
generative mechanism with this outcome-side typology was found in this task
either.

### Step 7→8: infrastructure to continuation without the founder

**Nearest models:** Selznick (institutionalization); this task adds
Stinchcombe (1965, organizational imprinting, SC-129) as the direct
academic anchor for founder-dependence this project deliberately lacked
after TASK_CLAUDE_0030 rejected "founder's syndrome." TASK_CLAUDE_0026's
own bounded search on founder succession (nonprofit-sector literature,
secondary-aggregated) found a directly relevant, specific result worth
repeating here: "transitions that extend the role of a founder [rather
than removing them abruptly] yield the best results" (Stanford Social
Innovation Review, via secondary aggregation, not independently primary-
verified) - consistent with, not contradicting, the existing Proposition 6.
**Genuine remaining gap, confirmed again by this task's sweep:** neither
Selznick nor Stinchcombe, nor anything found in this task's discipline-9
check, supplies a validated model of *how to execute a good handoff* -
both explain persistence of founding structure, not transition quality.
**Surviving novelty claim, and the strongest one in the whole sequence:**
the Handoff Test (Chapter 15) can legitimately claim to address a gap nothing
in four tasks' worth of searching has closed - provided the manuscript
states this as an untested proposition, not as an application of existing
succession-planning science.

## What the international-case candidates do and do not add to novelty

None of the recommended cases (Harley-Davidson H.O.G., CrossFit, Google+,
Alcoholics Anonymous, Wikipedia/Benkler, the Apple/Google/Intel/Adobe
no-poach agreement, Airbnb-vs-hotel trust) constitute prior art for the
Palma Method itself - they are illustrations of specific mechanisms
(company-built vs. decentralized-licensed community infrastructure;
radical decentralization vs. content standardization as alternate routes
to founder-independence; power concentration despite open participation;
coercive network closure; peer- vs. brand-based trust), not competing
integrated theories of the eight-step sequence. This is stated explicitly
because it would be easy, in later editing, to let a well-known case's
familiarity create an impression of validation it does not provide -
Domino's Pizza Turnaround was specifically evaluated and is **not
recommended** for this reason among others (see
`INTERNATIONAL_CASES_SHORTLIST_v0.1.md`): it is a company-to-customer
responsiveness case, not a peer-to-peer or community-infrastructure case,
and using it would risk exactly this kind of borrowed-credibility overclaim.

## The surviving novelty claim, restated

Unchanged across four tasks of searching (TASK_CLAUDE_0024, 0025, 0026,
0031): **Palma has defensible conceptual novelty with untested
propositions.** No integrated academic or practitioner model was found
anywhere in this project's now-138-card corpus that proposes the same
eight-step sequence, the same Palma Moment definition, or the same
organizer-independence threshold mechanism. Individual steps and
individual tools are, almost without exception, adaptations or
recombinations of well-established prior work (classification 1 or 2 in
the existing Prior-Art Matrix), correctly and mostly-already-correctly
attributed in the manuscript. This task's sweep neither strengthens nor
weakens that verdict - it closes several specific citation gaps
(discipline 7, 9, 14, 16, 18, 20) without surfacing a prior integrated
equivalent at the sequence level.

## Formulations the book must avoid (consolidated across all four tasks, updated this task)

1. That the five-dimension Relationship Capital model is empirically
   validated or the field's consensus taxonomy (TASK_CLAUDE_0024).
2. That Palma coined the phrase "Relationship Capital" (TASK_CLAUDE_0024).
3. That mobilizability, the Palma Moment, or the Response Signals are
   independently validated psychological/sociological constructs
   (TASK_CLAUDE_0024).
4. That the Infrastructure Canvas is the definitive framework for
   relationship-infrastructure design (TASK_CLAUDE_0024) - and, per this
   task, that Valente's network-interventions typology or Kraut/Resnick/
   Kiesler's design-claim translations constitute independent validation
   of the Canvas rather than useful adjacent vocabulary.
5. That the Handoff Test is grounded in existing succession-planning
   research (TASK_CLAUDE_0024) - even with Stinchcombe now carded, the
   *test itself* remains untested.
6. That Ostrom's commons-governance principles have been empirically
   validated in an organizational-community context the way they were in
   her own common-pool-resource studies (TASK_CLAUDE_0024).
7. That any Palma proposition (P1-P6, or the two additional propositions in
   this task's falsifiable-propositions update) has been confirmed by
   research rather than remaining untested (TASK_CLAUDE_0025/0026).
8. That the TCF exploratory coding pass (TASK_CLAUDE_0026 addendum)
   constitutes a pilot study or instrument-validated finding - it was a
   single-coder, five-case, non-random, descriptive pass only.
9. New this task: that Gittell's relational coordination theory supports a
   person-to-person claim without qualification - the theory's own ties are
   explicitly role-based, not person-based, a distinction Palma's model
   does not share and must state plainly.
10. New this task: that Valente's network-interventions research supports
    or validates Palma's developmental (organic, structure-focused)
    sequence - it is an interventionist, outcome-driven framework studying
    a different question.
11. New this task: that Wikipedia's "iron law of oligarchy" finding is
    precisely quantified or independently primary-verified by this project
    - the framework (Benkler) is solid; the specific empirical studies
    behind the "iron law" claim have been confirmed to exist but not yet
    individually read (SC-138 is flagged as this task's weakest-sourced
    card).
12. New this task: that Alcoholics Anonymous's case card says anything
    about twelve-step programs' clinical effectiveness as addiction
    treatment - the card is scoped strictly to governance/continuation
    structure and must not be extended into the separate, more contested
    clinical literature.
13. New this task: that CrossFit's community strength predicts its
    business durability - independent evidence found in this task's own
    research shows the opposite complication (widespread affiliate
    closures in the 2020s despite documented community strength), which
    should be stated alongside the case, not omitted.
