# Infrastructure Canvas — case stress test v0.1

Stream 3 of TASK_CLAUDE_0028. Fills the provisional five-field Canvas
(routes, roles, rhythms, channels, memory) for four bounded cases, using
only facts already recorded in this repository's own source cards. No case
detail is invented. Per AGENTS.md's privacy rule, no real name of a
personal acquaintance, TCF member, or Bombom customer appears anywhere in
this document beyond names already used directly in the current
manuscript text itself (Sandra/Pedro — see Case C, privacy note). Where a
source card records a real first name under the standing "no real names"
rule, this document uses the same generic reference the card itself
specifies.

## Case A — TAK.Shtab, bounded to the first weeks, retrospective

### Why this bounding, and why it differs from every existing worked example

Every existing Canvas fill for TAK.Shtab in this repository (the manuscript's
Chapter 18 body, `PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_v0.2.md`'s worked example, and
`PALMA_METHOD_FIELD_GUIDE_APPENDIX_v0.1.md` section 12.2) spans the full
2022-2024 arc in a single fill — mixing initial mobilization, convoy
missions, the 2023 narrowing decision, the Taktion program, and the
2023-2024 research waves. This is precisely the compression the Founder
flagged as a problem (see `INFRASTRUCTURE_CANVAS_INTERNAL_AUDIT_v0.1.md`,
Finding F2). This case deliberately excludes everything after the initial
mobilization period.

The bounding line is not arbitrary: SC-049 itself states a confirmed,
author-supplied "two-act structure" — "2022 (emergency volunteer
mobilization, convoy missions...) and 2023 onward (pivot to formal program
delivery)." This case bounds further, inside act one, to exclude the
convoy missions specifically, because convoy missions required the
liberation of Kyiv-oblast settlements (publicly dated to approximately
early-to-mid April 2022) and are a materially different function
(transport into recently contested territory) from the case's chosen
function. The manuscript's own Chapter 18 prose independently supports this
narrower line: it states that "almost the entire structure of the
headquarters in the first weeks" (в первые недели) — the warehouse, the
duty roster, the request-tracking log — formed in reaction to problems that
had already occurred, before naming convoys or Taktion as later
developments in the same paragraph.

### Filled Canvas

**Unit**: the TAK.Shtab office as a single processing-and-delivery point
for humanitarian requests, considered only as one organizational function.

**Function**: receiving requests for help and delivering aid (goods, food,
transport coordination) in response.

**Time window**: from 24 February 2022 (the office's conversion into a
humanitarian coordination point) through the point at which convoy
missions into liberated settlements began (approximately early-to-mid
April 2022, per the public dating of the liberation of Irpin/Hostomel/Bucha
that made those missions possible) — an outer bound inferred from public
events, not a date directly stated in SC-049, and flagged as such.

**Organizer dependency to be reduced**: whether a request could be received,
routed, and closed by someone other than the two co-founders personally
each time.

| Field | Filled, bounded to this window only | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Routes | Personal contacts and existing media contacts (from the co-founders' pre-war think-tank work) brought the first specific inbound requests. Growth from there was, in the author's own words, "roughly exponential," because each new volunteer multiplied the reachable network further. A specific international personal contact (friends in Israel) supplied two body-armor vests — this sourcing event is in-window; their later use on convoy missions is explicitly out of window and not included here. | SC-049 |
| Roles | Low-threshold roles open to new arrivals immediately: receiving requests, unloading, sorting, packing, finding vehicles. A duty roster began forming because requests and aid vehicles did not stop even overnight, and one person could not physically staff it alone. An informal, joking nickname for one recurring role ("Chief Box Officer") emerged through repetition — the manuscript places this naming "at some point" without a precise date; treated here as plausibly, not certainly, in-window, since it describes the same "repetition creates a named function" mechanism the manuscript explicitly locates in the first weeks for the warehouse and duty roster. The same volunteer's later move to a formal, paid role is explicitly out of window. | SC-049; manuscript Chapter 18 body; manuscript Chapter 14 body |
| Rhythms | The duty roster itself (recurring shifts covering day and night, because requests did not stop). No evidence in this window of any other recurring rhythm — the three-wave research study is a 2023-2024 fact and is excluded here. | SC-049; manuscript Chapter 18 body |
| Channels | Personal, word-of-mouth requests and direct messages — no formal program existed yet. The shift to the formal Taktion program is a 2023-2024 fact and is excluded here. | SC-049 |
| Memory | A request-tracking log, created specifically because without one "the same request was sometimes closed twice, and another not at all" — an in-window, functionally precise memory mechanism. The pre-war domestic-violence research study's reactivation is a 2023 fact and is excluded here. | Manuscript Chapter 18 body |

**Predicted behavior (Response Signals, checked against what SC-049
actually documents in-window)**: responds/returns is directly documented —
"first a small delivery, then a larger one, then cars," arriving before any
system existed to solicit it (the organization's own founding Palma Moment,
per the Tool Spec's own labeling). Helps is directly documented (unloading,
sorting, packing performed by new arrivals). Coordinates and takes
responsibility are only partially observed in-window: the record shows the
beginning of one volunteer's arc toward responsibility, not its paid-role
completion, which this case deliberately excludes as out-of-window.

**Falsifier**: if it were shown that, within this window, only the two
co-founders personally could receive, route, and close a request — i.e.,
that the duty roster and request log existed on paper but every actual
request still required one of the two founders personally — this would
falsify the claim that distributed infrastructure existed even this early,
and would instead support a pure founder-bottleneck reading of the same
period.

**Confounds and alternative explanations**: (1) wartime urgency itself,
independent of any specific design choice, plausibly explains rapid
volunteer mobilization — SC-049's own `counterarguments` field states this
directly and this case does not resolve it. (2) The "exponential" network
growth may be substantially explained by a pre-existing, unrelated local
community (the pre-war TAK books/think-tank/Friday-gathering network at the
same physical office) being reactivated for a new purpose, rather than by
any new infrastructure the headquarters itself created — SC-049 states this
directly as the author's own working interpretation, not a settled fact.
This case's "roughly exponential" routes claim should be read with this
alternative explanation attached, not as evidence of an organizer-designed
mechanism.

**Privacy/ethical risk**: Moderate. SC-049 is `privacy_level: public` but
`personal_story_use: pending_author_approval`, and the standing house rule
(recorded in SC-049 itself) prohibits any personal acquaintance's real name
in any output, including internal research reports. This case fill uses no
name (generic "co-founders," "a volunteer") and excludes the one detail
SC-049 itself flags as unclearable (a third party's secondhand wartime
account, told without consent). Numerical figures (people reached, boxes,
timeframes) are excluded per SC-049's own explicit instruction that
"exact figures must not enter the manuscript until the underlying reports
are reconciled" — the same discipline is applied here.

**Did the fields actually help distinguish this case?** Partially. Roles,
Rhythms, Memory, and Channels each produced a distinct, checkable,
in-window fact once forced to a bounded window — this is itself informative,
since the unbounded versions of this same case (in the manuscript and Tool
Spec) blur these same facts together. Routes and Channels, however, are
difficult to keep apart even in this narrower window: "personal contacts"
(Routes) and "personal, word-of-mouth requests" (Channels) describe the
same underlying mechanism from two angles, reproducing Finding F3 from the
internal-consistency audit even after bounding fixes the period-mixing
problem. Bounding the window fixed F2's problem for this case; it did not
fix F3's problem.

## Case B — TCF newcomer-introduction intervention, hypothetical and prospective

**This entire case is hypothetical.** No newcomer, peer host, or specific
intervention described below has actually been run. This case exists to
test whether the Canvas can be filled prospectively for a real,
not-yet-attempted design, using only realistic assumptions grounded in
existing TCF evidence — not to report a result.

### Grounding assumptions (stated, not asserted as fact)

Drawn from existing TCF cards, used here only to make the hypothesis
realistic, not as claims about what would happen: the founder's own account
describes a welcome sequence and an informal high-five ritual that
currently routes through him and his coaches (SC-043, SC-044); coffee
already functions as a channel members use before or after training
(SC-044); the booking app makes familiar names visible before a class but
one member states explicitly it is "not used for social purposes" (SC-096);
regular, synchronized group-class attendance is where this project's
existing positive cases (SC-095, SC-096, SC-097, SC-098) consistently
locate real connection; a member with six months of low-regularity,
largely solo attendance formed zero gym-originated connections and traces
this specifically to the absence of repeated, synchronized joint activity
with the same people, not to the absence of a channel or rhythm as such
(SC-099).

### Design hypothesis

**If** a specific, named regular member ("peer host") is asked to
introduce themselves to any newcomer in their own group class, sit with
them at the existing post-class coffee, and is not replaced by Miguel for
that specific newcomer's first session, **then** a newcomer will initiate
at least one contact with that peer host — not only with Miguel — within
the newcomer's first several weeks, without Miguel personally brokering
that specific contact.

### Filled Canvas (prospective — describes a design, not an observation)

**Unit**: one newcomer's first group-class cycle at TCF (proposed: four to
six weekly sessions — a window sized to the class's own weekly rhythm, not
a fixed 30-day default, consistent with the Handoff Test's own stated
principle that a window should match the system's natural cadence).

**Function**: the newcomer's first peer-to-peer (not newcomer-to-founder)
contact.

**Organizer dependency to be reduced**: Miguel personally noticing,
greeting, and connecting each newcomer, per the founder's own account of
the current welcome sequence.

| Field | Proposed design | Assumption this rests on | Would need to be true for the field to be "real" per its own test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routes | The peer host, not Miguel, is the specific person the newcomer is introduced to first. | That a member can be asked to take on this role and will follow through without being asked a second time for the next newcomer. | The newcomer contacts or is contacted by the peer host again, later, without Miguel re-introducing them each time. |
| Roles | "Peer host" becomes a real, recognized role — not a one-off favor. | That recognition by other members (not just Miguel's assignment) is what makes a role "real," per the Canvas's own definition. | The same member volunteers again for a second newcomer without being asked a second time. |
| Rhythms | Piggybacks on the class's existing weekly cadence — no new rhythm is invented; the intervention adds a role to an existing rhythm. | That the rhythm itself is not the missing ingredient (SC-099 already shows rhythm can be present without producing connection) — the missing ingredient is joint, synchronized activity with the same people, which the peer-host pairing is designed to add. | The next class occurrence happens without a fresh invitation — already true of the class itself; this field is not really being tested by this intervention. |
| Channels | Post-class coffee, which already exists as a channel per the founder's own account. | That the channel's mere existence is not sufficient (per the Tool Spec's own explicit caution) and needs a specific, assigned reason for the newcomer to use it with a specific person. | The newcomer and peer host are seen using the coffee area together without Miguel present, more than once. |
| Memory | Weakest-designed field, honestly flagged as such: the only proposed mechanism is that the peer-host role itself becomes something other members know about and can point a newcomer toward, without Miguel re-explaining it each time. | No existing evidence (TCF or otherwise) that this specific mechanism works; this is the least evidence-grounded part of the design, consistent with Memory being the weakest-grounded field generally (internal-consistency audit, Finding F1 row). | A different member, not Miguel, can explain what a "peer host" is to a newcomer without checking with Miguel first. |

**Predicted success signal**: an observed Palma Moment between the
newcomer and the peer host specifically — a participant-to-participant
counter-action, not a newcomer-to-Miguel one, consistent with the
project's own standing caution that only participant-to-participant action
evidences distributed infrastructure.

**Predicted failure signal**: the newcomer reports Miguel as their only
point of contact after the window closes; the peer-host role quietly
reverts to Miguel because no second member volunteers for it; the coffee
channel is used but only alongside Miguel, not independently.

**Falsifier**: if, across several newcomer cycles, zero newcomers report
any peer-initiated contact outside the assigned first session, and the
peer-host role cannot be sustained past one instance without reverting to
Miguel, the hypothesis is falsified for this population and format — not
merely "not yet confirmed."

**Confounds and alternative explanations**: SC-099's own dispositional/
cultural finding is a direct, named confound here — some newcomers may have
a personal or cultural threshold for initiating contact that no design
change addresses, and this design cannot distinguish "the design failed"
from "this particular newcomer was never going to initiate contact
regardless of design" without a larger sample than one case. Selection
effects in who volunteers as peer host (likely already the most socially
active members) may make results non-representative of what a
less-socially-active member could sustain in the same role. Observation
itself (a newcomer or peer host aware they are part of a study) could
produce a Hawthorne-style effect independent of the design's real
mechanism.

**Privacy/ethical risk**: Low as stated here, since no real person is
named or described specifically — this is a fully hypothetical design. If
actually run, it would require the same consent process already used for
every other TCF interview in this project (see SC-095 through SC-099's own
consent and privacy-level fields) before any real participant's account
could enter a source card.

**Did the fields actually help distinguish this case?** Yes, and also no,
in an informative way. Roles, Channels, and Rhythms each produced a
genuinely different, checkable design decision. Routes and Channels blur
again here, exactly as in Case A: "the peer host greets the newcomer near
the coffee area" is simultaneously a Route (a specific person to reach) and
a use of an existing Channel (the coffee area) — the same overlap problem
recurs in a freshly designed, non-retrospective case, which is independent
confirmation that Finding F3 is a structural property of the two fields'
current definitions, not an artifact of sloppy retrospective reconstruction.

## Case C — Bombom, negative and center-dependent case

### Why Bombom rather than the publishing-house case

The task permits either. The publishing-house material (the pre-war TAK
books / Institute of Constructive Journalism prequel embedded within
SC-049) is thinner for this specific purpose: it is framed around the
domain/community/practice (Wenger) test for Chapter 2, not around
center-dependence, several of its facts are deliberately genericized for
chapter use (real venue and person names withheld even in the working
record for some details), and it is entangled with the same privacy-
sensitive TAK.Shtab founding narrative already used in Case A. Bombom
(SC-045) is a dedicated, single-organization founder interview with its
own explicit, already-stated editorial diagnosis ("owner-centred
relationship network... evidence does not yet exist for durable
customer-to-customer relationships") and a cleaner, single bounded crisis
episode. It is the better-evidenced choice for a center-dependent negative
case specifically.

### Filled Canvas

**Unit**: Café Bombom's owner-to-customer relationship system, specifically
around the closure-and-rescue crisis episode.

**Function**: whether the café's relationships could produce coordinated
customer support without being entirely mediated by the owner personally.

**Time window**: from the first sign of financial trouble through
reopening. SC-045 itself flags that the exact dated sequence is
unconfirmed (its own "Priority verification," item 1) — this gap is
preserved here, not filled in.

**Organizer dependency being examined (not "to be reduced" — no
intervention was designed or run here; see the ethical note below)**:
whether response and coordination could occur without the owner as the
sole connecting point.

| Field | Filled from confirmed SC-045 facts | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Routes | The route that actually produced the decisive resource (rescue capital) ran through four or five people who were already close friends or family of the owner before the crisis — not through customers generally. A separate, confirmed attempt to reach restaurant groups and potential partners with no prior relationship produced no result. | The only route that worked was a pre-existing personal one; a genuinely cold route (no prior tie) failed outright — a real, if single-case, data point consistent with the project's own untested P6 proposition that route quality (an existing tie) may matter more than the number of attempts. |
| Roles | One employee moved from dishwashing toward cooking after expressing that ambition — an owner-granted role. No customer role beyond "customer" is evidenced anywhere in the record. | Roles exist only inside the owner-employee relationship, not among customers. |
| Rhythms | Regularity produced personal recognition — the owner remembers regulars' orders and notices changes in their routine (the two-croissants-to-one example). | The rhythm that exists is owner-customer, not peer-to-peer; no evidence of any rhythm among customers themselves. |
| Channels | No dedicated customer-to-customer channel is evidenced anywhere in the record (no shared table system, no group chat, no recurring format connecting customers to each other). The closure itself made the owner-centered structure visible: customers approached the owner directly with offers of help. | Every channel that functioned during the crisis routed through the owner; this is the clearest field-level evidence for center-dependence in this case. |
| Memory | Whatever order history, relationship history, and context exists is held by the owner personally — there is no evidence of it being distributed to or carried by any group. | The textbook negative instance of this field's own definition ("real when the group, not only the organizer, carries and applies it") — memory here is concentrated in exactly one person, the opposite condition. |

**Observed behavior (this already happened; not a prediction)**: response
occurred — customers offered contacts, professional help, and possible
financial assistance — but the confirmed, decisive response (the four or
five investors) ran entirely through pre-existing personal ties to the
owner, and no confirmed instance exists anywhere in the record of two
customers coordinating with each other without the owner as connector.
This is a directionality case exactly matching the project's own standing
caution: action toward the founder/owner is real and important for that
dyad, but does not by itself show distributed peer infrastructure.

**Falsifier — already asked, not yet answered**: the unanswered
continuation questionnaire sent to the owner (`interview-guide-sandra-
continuation.md`) asks directly whether any two regulars ever coordinated
or did something together independent of the owner. A confirmed "yes, and
here is what happened" answer would revise this case from a clean negative
toward a partial one. As of this audit, that question remains open — this
case's negative classification is the current best evidence, not a proven
absence.

**Confounds and alternative explanations**: single retrospective,
owner-only account with no customer interviews at all — SC-045 states this
limitation directly; owner-reported warmth and informant bias cannot be
ruled out. The crisis itself is a stress-test dynamic ("the closure made
the structure visible") that may reveal different things than ordinary,
non-crisis operation would. This was never an actual Palma Method
engagement — the manuscript's own Chapter 20 interlude states plainly that
the author was "not anyone's consultant" here, only a customer and
bystander — so nothing about this case tests whether a designed
intervention would have changed the outcome.

**Privacy/ethical risk — a specific inconsistency worth flagging, not
resolved here.** SC-045's own frontmatter states `personal_story_use:
prohibited`. The current manuscript (Chapter 20 interlude) nonetheless
names the owner and her business partner directly by first name ("Сандра
и Педро"), including a specific detail — that cold outreach to unfamiliar
restaurant groups produced no result, while help came through four or five
pre-existing ties — that matches SC-045's own confirmed follow-up data
precisely. Either this use was separately authorized outside SC-045's own
metadata (plausible, since the manuscript passage is framed as the
author's own first-person recollection as a customer, not a citation of
the interview), or SC-045's `personal_story_use` field is stale and should
be updated to reflect an actual authorization. This audit flags the
inconsistency; per AGENTS.md ("when instructions conflict, stop and ask"),
it does not resolve it, and this report uses the names only because they
already appear in the current manuscript text itself, not by independent
disclosure.

**Did the fields actually help distinguish this case?** Yes, clearly.
Memory is the single most discriminating field of the whole stress test —
concentrated in one person here versus at least partially distributed in
Case A. Channels is the second most discriminating — entirely
owner-mediated here versus a mix of personal and eventually formal
channels in Case A. This case shows the Canvas doing real diagnostic work
when the underlying case is genuinely different in kind from Case A, which
is a meaningful check against the non-falsifiability risk named in the
task brief.

## Case D — Contrast case: formal infrastructure present, predicted action absent

### Case selection

SC-099 (a TCF member of approximately six months' standing, low-regularity
attendance) is used here as the contrast case, per the task's own
definition: a route, role, rhythm, or channel appears to exist formally,
but the predicted participant action does not occur. This case was
identified during source-card review, not constructed for this task — it
already exists in the corpus as the project's first documented "false
case" for the adjacent domain/community/practice test, and it independently
serves the same falsification purpose for the Canvas specifically.

### Filled Canvas (retrospective, already-observed, not designed)

**Unit**: one TCF member's own six-month membership and attendance
pattern.

**Function**: whether TCF's existing infrastructure produces peer
connection for a member who attends regularly but mostly outside the
synchronized group-class format.

**Time window**: approximately six months (the member's tenure to date at
time of interview).

**Organizer dependency being examined**: not applicable in the usual sense
— no intervention was designed for this member; the question is whether
TCF's existing, already-built infrastructure functions for her absent any
targeted design.

| Field | Formal status | Actual, confirmed outcome | Passes the field's own "real" test? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routes | None exist to any gym-formed contact; the only route to the founder is present but explicitly transactional ("he owns a business, you greet the customer"). Pre-existing relationships she brought into the space (including with the interviewer himself) "go quiet" there — she reports they interact less at the gym than elsewhere. | Zero gym-originated routes confirmed, explicitly and directly ("no, never, like, no, nothing"). | No — by her own direct account, not inferred. |
| Roles | She holds no role beyond generic member; no evidence of ever being asked to do anything beyond attend. | No role observed. | No. |
| Rhythms | A real, recurring weekly pattern exists — one group class plus roughly two solo open-gym sessions — and she keeps returning without a fresh invitation each time. | Present and functioning by the field's own literal test (next occurrence does not require a fresh invitation) — **and still produced no connection.** | **Yes, the field's own observable-sign test is satisfied, and the predicted downstream action is still absent.** This is the case's central finding — see below. |
| Channels | The open-gym space and the booking app both formally exist and she uses both. | She confirms, unprompted and directly, that acknowledgment from other open-gym attendees is not merely thin but literally absent. | Formal existence and use are confirmed; the Tool Spec's own separate caution — "existence and membership count are not sufficient evidence... check directly" — is exactly what this case demonstrates in a real instance, not hypothetically. |
| Memory | No evidence that any group-held knowledge ever reaches her, or that she needs it to — no relationship exists that would require it. | Not applicable / absent. | Not testable in this case. |

### Why this case matters for the Canvas's falsifiability, specifically

The Rhythms and Channels rows above are the important finding. Rhythms
passes its own stated observable-sign test (repetition without a fresh
invitation) while the outcome the tool implicitly exists to produce
(connection) still does not occur. This is good news for the Canvas's
falsifiability, not bad news: it shows that a field's own "real" test and
the deeper relational outcome are two separable observations, not one
disguised as two — satisfying the Canvas's own criteria for a field being
"real" is a necessary but not sufficient condition for the Response
Signals the Method ultimately cares about, and this case is direct,
confirmed evidence of exactly that gap. No document currently states this
distinction explicitly anywhere in the Canvas's own materials; Stream 4
recommends adding it.

A softer, residual non-falsifiability risk remains, but it attaches to
Memory specifically, not to the Canvas as a whole: Memory's observable
sign ("applied by the group, not only the organizer") is the vaguest of
the five and — as Finding F1 in the internal-consistency audit already
shows — the only field with no predecessor theory and no TCF grounding.
Across all four cases in this stress test, Memory was populated richly
only once (Case A, and only by mixing periods before this case bounded
it), and was essentially empty or not-applicable in the other three. A
field that is either absent or maximally narrativized, with nothing in
between, is the one part of the Canvas most at risk of being filled
post hoc to fit whatever the case's outcome already was, rather than
constraining the outcome in advance — this is the specific, scoped
non-falsifiability flag this stress test raises, not a claim about the
Canvas overall.

**Falsifier for the general claim tested here**: if a future low-regularity
member with the same attendance pattern did form gym-originated
connections, that would not falsify this specific member's account, but
it would weaken "regularity format, not raw frequency" as a general
explanation and would need to be reconciled with SC-099's own finding.

**Confounds and alternative explanations**: a real, named dispositional/
cultural factor coexists with the structural explanation — raised in the
Philippines, she describes approaching strangers as not normalized growing
up, and reports the identical low-cross-group-friendship pattern in school
regardless of TCF or gyms at all. SC-099 itself states both operate
together, not that the structural account fully explains her case. A
social-desirability effect cannot be ruled out, since the interviewer is
also a personal acquaintance. No corroboration yet exists from the founder
or another member on whether her account of literally zero open-gym
interaction matches what others observe of the same format generally.

**Privacy/ethical risk — the highest of the four cases.** This
participant's real name is not recorded anywhere in this project, not even
as a discussion alias, per SC-099's own explicit instruction. This report
refers to her only as "the low-regularity member (SC-099)," matching the
card's own required practice. `personal_story_use` is `pending_author_
approval` and `privacy_level` is `restricted`. Explicit consent for any
use beyond internal research remains outstanding — this report treats that
as unchanged and does not use this case for anything beyond the internal
governance audit it was commissioned for.

**Did the fields actually help distinguish this case?** Yes — this is the
single most informative case in the stress test for the Canvas's own
internal logic, precisely because it separates "the field's test is
satisfied" from "the hoped-for outcome occurred," a distinction nothing
else in the existing documentation states explicitly.

## Cross-case summary

| Case | Outcome | Most discriminating field(s) | Least discriminating field(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — TAK.Shtab (bounded) | Positive, distributed-early | Roles, Rhythms, Memory (once bounded) | Routes vs. Channels (still blur) |
| B — TCF newcomer (hypothetical) | Not yet run | Roles, Channels | Routes vs. Channels (blur again); Memory (weakest-designed) |
| C — Bombom | Negative, center-dependent | Memory, Channels | Roles (thin on both sides) |
| D — TCF low-regularity member | Negative despite formal presence | Rhythms and Channels (pass their own test, outcome still absent) | Memory (not applicable) |

The Canvas does not describe every outcome equally well after the fact —
the four cases above produced materially different field-by-field
pictures, which is evidence against a blanket non-falsifiability verdict.
The two specific, scoped risks that do stand are: (1) Routes and Channels
converge on the same underlying facts in every case tested, including the
fresh hypothetical (Case B), confirming Finding F3 as structural rather
than a retrospective-reconstruction artifact; and (2) Memory is either
richly narrativized (only when a period-mixing shortcut is available, Case
A unbounded) or empty (Cases B through D), making it the field most at
risk of post-hoc fitting rather than genuine prediction. Both risks carry
directly into `INFRASTRUCTURE_CANVAS_SPEC_OPTIONS_v0.1.md`.
