## Acceptance criteria

### 1. TAK.Shtab is not translated

**Pass.** Chapter 14 contains no occurrence of TAK.Shtab, `Штаб`, `HQ`, or
`Headquarters`, so no replacement was invented. The book-wide English rule to
apply after acceptance is:

- first relevant mention: `TAK.Shtab (Ukrainian: “Так. Це штаб”)`;
- every later mention: `TAK.Shtab`;
- never shorten or translate the proper name as `HQ` or `Headquarters`.

### 2. No unsupported “usually”

**Pass.** The candidate contains neither `обычно` nor `как правило`. Its opening
asks what should count as a launch result and identifies immediately visible
data as a starting point. Research claims are attributed to named authors or to
the stated TCF material.

### 3. No omniscient instructional tone

**Pass.** The Method appears as the author's proposed
way of looking: `я замечаю`, `я использую`, `я называю`, `я предлагаю`, `для
меня`. The text does not claim that all launches follow one law. Procedural
sentences remain where the chapter genuinely offers a test, measurement rule,
or privacy boundary. Serhii still needs to confirm that the closing manifesto
paragraph sounds like him rather than like an editor speaking in his name.

### 4. Minimum unjustified “not X, but Y” constructions

**Pass.** The candidate contains no sentence built as the house formula `это не
X, а Y` and does not open an argument by refuting an unasked reader objection.
Three local contrasts remain because both sides are evidentially necessary:

- coordination was found “из интервью, а не из таблицы”;
- Rogers's model is “карта вопросов, а не список ... инфлюенсеров”;
- an early adopter needs a social example, “а не азарт первенства.”

These contrasts distinguish data sources or mechanisms; they are not rhetorical
corrections of the reader.

### 5. Scholars enter through question, material, finding, and boundary

**Pass at paragraph level; chapter-level density remains open.**

- **Dave Snowden / Cynefin:** enters through the loss of context in IBM's
  knowledge systems; the Welsh name, memorable origin, HBR publication, and
  three relevant domains are explained; the TCF station-order scene shows the
  bounded `probe, sense, respond` use without adding an unused coauthor's name.
- **Everett Rogers:** enters when one observed response has to spread to other
  people; Iowa farmers supply the starting material; adoption stages and
  perceived qualities of an innovation supply the finding; pro-innovation bias,
  the label `laggards`, and local reputation limit the transfer.
- **Carol Weiss:** is identified as an American researcher of program
  evaluation and enters through the ambiguous result; program interests and
  uses of evaluation supply the material; direct and conceptual use supply the
  finding; the block does not present evaluation as causal proof.
- **Robert Sampson, Stephen Raudenbush, and Felton Earls:** enter through the
  friendly-neighborhood/action gap; 8,782 Chicago residents in 343 neighborhood
  clusters supply the material; cohesion plus expected intervention supplies
  the finding; observational design and the non-transferability of 1995 Chicago
  violence are stated explicitly.

### 6. Concrete scenes and examples

**Pass.** The chapter gives the reader:

- a real TCF station-order scene and one participant's anonymous explanation of

- the observable difference between a message, an exchanged contact, and a

### 7. Authorial research manifesto, not universal doctrine

**Pass, pending voice approval.** The candidate makes Palma Method a vocabulary
developed after lived experience, not the cause of that experience. It treats
launch as a place where the Method may prove wrong, separates observation from
interpretation, and ends with a question for the next chapter. It does not claim
a golden rule for communities or human relationships.
