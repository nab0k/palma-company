# v0.12 scholar and theory integration audit

## Editorial test

At a scholar's first substantial appearance the reader should be able to answer:

1. What question in the preceding text made this work necessary?
2. Who is the researcher, and which biographical detail helps explain or remember
   this particular work?
3. What did the researcher actually observe, compare, collect, or build?
4. What was found in plain language?
5. What changes in the current Palma Method argument?
6. Where does the transfer stop?

A title, university, award, citation count, or adjective such as “famous” earns
space only when it helps with one of those six answers. Later appearances use the
idea or a short memory cue instead of introducing the person again.

## Contemporary-research rule

Classic work remains when it supplies the clearest original distinction. A newer
study enters only if it changes the population, medium, mechanism, causal
confidence, boundary, or practical decision. A recent publication date by itself
does not make a chapter more current.

## Depth check

The substantial first-appearance blocks now extend beyond a name-and-definition
reference. Each audited group receives, in continuous prose, the question that
earns its entrance, material from the work, a finding, a use in the chapter, and
a boundary. Later reminders are intentionally shorter and are not treated as new
introductions. The remaining issue is not block length but publication clearance
for the anonymous TCF episode used beside Morrison, Edmondson, and Cynefin.
