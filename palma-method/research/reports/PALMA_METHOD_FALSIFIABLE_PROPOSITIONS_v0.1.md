# Palma Method — Falsifiable Propositions and Validation Path v0.1

Task: TASK_CLAUDE_0024, Stream 4
Owner: Claude
Status: draft for editorial review (Codex / Serhii)

Six propositions below convert the strongest original components identified in
`PALMA_METHOD_NOVELTY_AND_SCIENTIFIC_STATUS_AUDIT_v0.1.md` and
`PALMA_METHOD_PRIOR_ART_MATRIX_v0.1.md` into explicit, falsifiable form. None of

project has not done before - it is the first step of a validation path, not a

## Proposition 1 — The Palma Moment threshold

**Statement:** A single observed counter-action meeting the Palma Moment
definition (a counter-action that measurably changes what either side expects
from the next interaction) predicts a higher likelihood of a second interaction
within the system's natural next cycle, compared to a matched interaction with
no observed counter-action.

- **Unit of analysis:** the dyad (two specific people, at a specific point in
  time).
- **Independent variable:** whether a Palma-Moment-qualifying counter-action was
  observed in interaction 1.
- **Dependent variable / observable condition:** whether a second, initiated-by-
  either-side interaction occurs within the system's next natural cycle (e.g.,
  the next week for a weekly-cadence group).
- **Expected relationship:** positive association between observing a
  qualifying counter-action and a subsequent second interaction.
- **Competing explanation:** both the counter-action and the subsequent
  interaction could be explained by a third factor (e.g., both parties simply
  attend the same recurring class regardless of any specific counter-action) -
  Feld's foci mechanism alone would predict repeated co-presence without
  requiring a Palma Moment at all.
- **Evidence that would weaken or falsify it:** a finding that pairs with an
  observed qualifying counter-action are no more likely to interact again than
  pairs without one, once shared-foci exposure (same class, same shift) is
  controlled for.
- **Possible qualitative test:** paired interviews with both sides of several
  observed counter-actions, asking each independently whether their expectation
  of the other changed - checking for the "changes what either side expects"
  criterion directly, not just recording the behavior.
- **Possible quantitative test:** a coded-observation study at a bounded site
  (e.g., one gym's group classes over one season) recording qualifying
  counter-actions and subsequent contact between the same pairs, compared
  against a baseline rate of subsequent contact among pairs sharing the same
  foci with no recorded counter-action.
- **TCF/TAK.Shtab/Bombom status:** TCF's SC-097 (Anya's co-signing episode) and
  SC-095 (Francisco's microphone/itinerary episodes) each illustrate a single
  instance meeting the definition, each drawn from one interview - these can
  **generate** the hypothesis (they show the mechanism is possible and give it
  a concrete shape) but cannot **test** it, since no comparison group (pairs
  without an observed Palma Moment) or systematic follow-up exists in the
  current interview set.

## Proposition 2 — Redundancy mitigates single-point-of-failure risk

**Statement:** When an external relationship's primary contact departs, the
relationship's continuation is more likely if a second, independent, genuine
internal relationship already existed than if it did not.

- **Unit of analysis:** the organization-to-external-party tie (e.g., a client,
  a donor, a partner).
- **Independent variable:** presence or absence of a second, independent
  internal relationship with the same external party before the primary
  contact's departure.
- **Dependent variable:** whether the relationship (measured by continued
  contact, continued transaction, or continued support) persists after the
  departure.
- **Expected relationship:** positive - a second relationship increases the odds
  of continuation.
- **Competing explanation:** organizations that build redundant relationships
  may simply be more relationship-oriented overall, making the redundancy a
  marker of a broader organizational trait rather than an independently
  causal mechanism (this is close to the confound Bermiss & Greenbaum's own
  paper flags for the analogous professional-services finding this project has
  already sourced).
- **Evidence that would weaken or falsify it:** relationships with a documented
  second internal contact showing no higher continuation rate than those
  without, once overall organizational relationship-density is controlled for.
- **Possible qualitative test:** structured post-departure interviews with
  affected external parties at TAK.Shtab or a comparable organization, asking
  directly what, if anything, kept the relationship going after a specific
  person left.
- **Possible quantitative test:** a retrospective audit of an organization's own
  CRM or contact records, comparing continuation rates for relationships with
  and without a documented second internal contact at the time of a staff
  departure.
- **TCF/TAK.Shtab/Bombom status:** no case in this project's current evidence
  base documents an actual departure-and-continuation sequence with a
  redundancy comparison. The publishing-house story (Chapter 4) is a single
  case of the *opposite* condition (no redundancy, and the relationship did not
  survive) - illustrative of the risk, not a test of the mitigation, since no
  comparable case with redundancy present exists in the book's own material to
  compare it against.

## Proposition 3 — The six Response Signals are not equally likely to co-occur

**Statement:** Observing a "later," more demanding signal (e.g., takes
responsibility) in a given relationship does not reliably predict that "earlier,"
less demanding signals (e.g., responds, returns) were also observed - i.e., the
signals do not form a cumulative, ladder-like sequence, contrary to how such
checklists are often informally read.

- **Unit of analysis:** the dyad or small group, observed for a defined period.
- **Independent variable:** which signals are present, coded independently of
  each other.
- **Dependent variable:** the co-occurrence pattern across the six signals.
- **Expected relationship (as currently stated in the tool-spec):** no
  guaranteed cumulative order - each signal is an independent check.
- **Competing explanation:** the signals could, contrary to the tool-spec's own
  claim, actually form a real developmental sequence in most cases, simply
  because more demanding behaviors (coordinating with a third party) are
  behaviorally harder to do without first having done easier ones (responding).
- **Evidence that would weaken or falsify the tool-spec's "nonlinear, not a
  ladder" claim specifically:** a systematic finding that, across many
  observed relationships, "later" signals almost never appear without
  "earlier" ones - which would mean the checklist behaves like a ladder in
  practice even if not intended as one.
- **Possible qualitative test:** interview-based reconstruction of several
  relationships' full signal histories, checking whether any case shows a
  "later" signal (e.g., helps) without an "earlier" one (e.g., returns) ever
  having occurred.
- **Possible quantitative test:** a coded dataset of many dyads' observed
  signal histories, checking the actual co-occurrence and ordering statistics
  against both a strict-ladder model and a fully-independent-signals model.
- **TCF/TAK.Shtab/Bombom status:** the current interview set explicitly does
  not include confirmed TCF examples of "coordinates" or "takes responsibility"
  at all (per `PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_v0.2.md`'s own honest disclosure), so the
  existing material cannot test this proposition either way - it can only
  illustrate the "earlier" signals.

## Proposition 4 — The five Relationship Capital dimensions are empirically discriminant

**Statement:** Trust, reciprocity, access, relationship strength, and network
structure, when independently measured for the same set of relationships, will
show low-to-moderate inter-correlation - i.e., they behave as genuinely separate
constructs, not as one underlying "relationship quality" factor with five
different names.

- **Unit of analysis:** the dyadic relationship (for the first four dimensions)
  and the portfolio (for network structure).
- **Independent/observable conditions:** independent ratings or behavioral
  measures for each of the five dimensions, collected for the same set of
  relationships.
- **Expected relationship:** the five dimensions correlate with each other
  (relationships are not randomly assembled from independent parts) but not so
  strongly that they collapse into one factor.
- **Competing explanation:** a single "relationship warmth" halo effect could
  drive all five ratings together, meaning the five-dimension model is
  face-valid but not empirically discriminant - a real risk given that four of
  the five (all but network structure) are typically rated by the same person
  about the same relationship, inviting common-method bias.
- **Evidence that would weaken or falsify it:** a factor analysis or
  correlation matrix showing the four dyadic-level dimensions load onto a
  single factor rather than four distinguishable ones.
- **Possible qualitative test:** structured interviews asking respondents to
  separately describe a relationship high on one dimension and low on another
  (e.g., high trust, low access) - checking whether people can readily generate
  such combinations, which a genuinely discriminant model should allow.
- **Possible quantitative test:** develop simple multi-item scales for each
  dimension, administer to a sample rating multiple relationships each, and run
  a confirmatory factor analysis testing the five-factor model against
  simpler one- or two-factor alternatives.
- **TCF/TAK.Shtab/Bombom status:** the manuscript already contains anecdotal
  examples of dimensions moving independently (Chapter 6's account of trust
  without full reciprocity accounting, line 186; Chapter 9's account of high
  dyadic trust with poor structural access, line 299) - these are useful,
  concrete illustrations of the *possibility* of discriminant behavior, and can
  motivate the hypothesis, but a handful of narrative anecdotes cannot test a
  quantitative discriminant-validity claim.

## Proposition 5 — Participant-to-participant value creation predicts reduced organizer dependency

**Statement:** As the proportion of value-creating interactions occurring
between participants (rather than between the organizer and each participant)
increases, the system's continued function after the organizer's temporary
absence increases correspondingly.

- **Unit of analysis:** the whole system (a gym, a chat group, a client
  community) observed over time.
- **Independent variable:** the proportion of observed value-creating
  interactions that are participant-to-participant vs. organizer-to-participant.
- **Dependent variable:** system function during a period of organizer absence
  (e.g., a defined multi-week gap).
- **Expected relationship:** positive - more participant-to-participant activity
  predicts more resilient continuation.
- **Competing explanation:** both variables could be driven by system age or
  size alone (older, larger systems naturally accumulate more peer ties AND
  more redundant capability, without one causing the other) - a genuine
  confound this proposition does not currently control for.
- **Evidence that would weaken or falsify it:** systems with high measured
  participant-to-participant activity that nonetheless collapse or stall
  sharply during organizer absence, or the reverse (low peer activity, strong
  continuation).
- **Possible qualitative test:** the three-participant "if Miguel disappeared
  for three months" prediction exercise already conducted for TCF (SC-095,
  SC-096, SC-097) - expanded systematically across more participants and,
  critically, followed up with an actual observed absence rather than only a
  prediction.
- **Possible quantitative test:** a longitudinal study coding interaction
  proportion over time for one or more systems, paired with a natural or
  deliberate organizer-absence period and objective continuation metrics
  (attendance, message volume, event recurrence).
- **TCF/TAK.Shtab/Bombom status:** TCF interviews so far contain *predictions*
  about what would happen if Miguel disappeared, explicitly labeled as
  predictions, not observed results (`PALMA_METHOD_TOOL_SPEC_v0.2.md`'s Handoff
  Test section is explicit about this). This is hypothesis-generating material,
  not a test - no actual organizer-absence period has been observed and
  recorded for TCF. TAK.Shtab's 2023-2024 evolution is a stronger case (real
  organizational change over real time, independently corroborated via SC-110)
  but was not designed or recorded as a controlled test of this specific
  proposition - it can illustrate consistency with the hypothesis, not confirm
  it against a real alternative explanation.

## Proposition 6 — Institutionalization is observable through specific, listed structural features, not through founder sentiment

**Statement:** An organization's relational value has institutionalized (in
Selznick's sense, as the manuscript's own Chapter 21 applies it) when a
specific, checkable list of structural features is present - independent roles,
recorded procedures, resource commitments not tied to one person's continued
presence - and this list predicts continuation better than the founder's own
subjective sense of "how ready" the organization is.

- **Unit of analysis:** the organization or program.
- **Independent variable:** presence/absence of the specific structural
  features (named roles, documented procedures, non-founder-dependent resource
  flows).
- **Dependent variable:** actual continuation of function after a founder
  steps back, tested against the founder's own prior subjective prediction.
- **Expected relationship:** the structural checklist predicts continuation
  better than founder sentiment alone.
- **Competing explanation:** founder sentiment could be a reasonably good
  predictor precisely because founders have tacit knowledge the checklist
  misses - this proposition assumes, without yet testing, that the explicit
  checklist beats the tacit judgment.
- **Evidence that would weaken or falsify it:** cases where the structural
  checklist is fully satisfied but the organization nonetheless collapses or
  reverts to founder-dependency once the founder actually steps back, at a rate
  no better than founder-sentiment predictions alone.
- **Possible qualitative test:** comparing Selznick's own qualitative test
  ("would participants have discarded this structure if a more efficient
  alternative appeared?") against the structural checklist for the same
  organization, checking whether they agree.
- **Possible quantitative test:** a multi-organization comparative study
  scoring the structural checklist and founder-sentiment predictions in
  advance, then tracking actual continuation outcomes.
- **TCF/TAK.Shtab/Bombom status:** TAK.Shtab is the only case with enough
  independently corroborated detail (SC-110's public-page confirmation) to
  illustrate the structural-features side of this proposition concretely
  (named program, recurring research, external funding tied to institutional
  process, not solely the founder). It is a single case, not a comparative
  test, and per the standing project restriction, must not be described as
  having undergone a formal test of this proposition - see the flagged
  overclaim risk in `PALMA_METHOD_NOVELTY_AND_SCIENTIFIC_STATUS_AUDIT_v0.1.md`,
  section B.4.

## Validation path

### Before publication (within the current book schedule)

Only changes achievable without new research:

- **Wording corrections**: replace any bare "scientifically grounded,"
  "validated," or "proven" language attached to any of the five tools with the
  qualified language in `PALMA_METHOD_DEFENSIBLE_CLAIMS_PACKAGE_v0.1.md`.
- **Clearer boundaries**: state explicitly, wherever the Response Signals or
  Handoff Test are introduced, that they are observational checklists without
  established transition probabilities or a fixed protocol (this language
  already exists in the tool-spec drafts and should be preserved verbatim if
  and when those tools enter manuscript prose).
- **Removal of overclaims**: review Chapter 21, line 620 (the TAK.Shtab
  "apparently passed" sentence) against the standing project restriction and
  decide, editorially, whether it needs softening.
- **Transparent labeling of hypotheses**: continue the practice already
  well-established in this project's source cards and tool-spec (labeling every
  TCF claim to its specific interview) into any future manuscript prose
  introducing the five tools by name.
- **Small descriptive analyses using already-available material**: a
  straightforward, low-cost addition - tabulate, across the existing TCF and
  TAK.Shtab interviews already collected, which of the six Response Signals
  each documents, making the current evidentiary gaps (no confirmed
  "coordinates" or "takes responsibility" examples) visible to a reader rather
  than only to this project's internal source cards.

### After publication (a credible research program, not already-completed validation)

- **Pilot study**: a single bounded site (one client engagement, or a
  continuation of the TCF/TAK.Shtab relationship with structured, prospective
  data collection rather than retrospective interviews) testing Proposition 1
  or 5 above as a first, low-cost pilot.
- **Instrument development**: build multi-item scales for the five
  Relationship Capital dimensions, professionally piloted for basic reliability
  (e.g., Cronbach's alpha) before any claim of measurement is made.
- **Reliability and validity testing**: confirmatory factor analysis for
  Proposition 4; test-retest reliability for any new instrument.
- **Comparative cases**: deliberately recruit at least one organization outside
  the author's own network, to test whether findings generalize beyond
  founder-observed cases.
- **Longitudinal testing**: track at least one system's participant-to-
  participant ratio and continuation outcomes over a multi-year window, rather
  than relying on retrospective or single-timepoint interviews.
- **Potential academic collaborators**: organizational-behavior or economic-
  sociology researchers working on social capital measurement, network
  redundancy, or institutionalization theory - specific names are not
  recommended here, since that would exceed this task's research scope and
  risk an uninformed endorsement claim.
- **Pre-registration or publication options**: a pre-registered pilot study
  (e.g., via OSF) for Proposition 1 or 2 would be the lowest-cost credible next
  step; a practitioner-oriented working paper describing the five tools as
  hypotheses, not findings, would be a reasonable interim publication target
  before any peer-reviewed submission.

**This program is a set of proposed next steps, not a description of work
already done.** No claim anywhere in this project's material should describe
any part of this section as completed.

## Addendum (2026-08-16, TASK_CLAUDE_0026) — first exploratory pilot executed

Proposition 1 ("participant-initiated actions... predict persistence better
than attendance frequency, satisfaction, or emotional intensity alone")
received its first exploratory, non-statistical check: see
`TCF_TRANSITION_MODEL_EXPLORATORY_CODING_v0.1.md`, which coded four related
variables (unscripted participant-initiated action; continued peer
relationship outside scheduled training; organizer-contact strength;
peer-contact strength) against the five TCF interviews existing as of
2026-08-16 (SC-095 through SC-099). This was a single-coder, five-case,
non-random, descriptive pass - explicitly not the "pilot study" or
"instrument development" stage this document's own validation path describes
as a future step, and it does not satisfy that stage's requirements (no
second rater, no comparison group, no pre-registered sample beyond the five
cases already collected for other purposes). It found the model's basic
elements present in four of five cases and absent in the fifth (a
deliberately selected contrast case), and separately exposed a genuine
construct-clarity problem in the "continued peer relationship outside
scheduled training" variable, which proved hard to separate cleanly from
ordinary gym-adjacent contact in two of five interviews. Neither finding
changes this document's original propositions or validation path above - both
remain accurately described as untested. This addendum records that a first,
small, exploratory step happened; it does not upgrade any proposition's
status.
