# Palma Canon 0.1

## Unit of analysis

Palma Test reads a relationship system: the relationship between an owner or organisation and identifiable people around its work, plus relationships among those people where evidence exists. It does not diagnose a person, rank a business or measure communication quality in general.

## The six constructs

### 1. Reciprocity — Does it clap back?

Definition: observable exchange in which people respond, contribute, return, introduce, teach, challenge, purchase, help or otherwise affect the relationship, and receive something they value in return.

Indicators: unsolicited replies; introductions; repeat contribution; peer help; repeated purchase linked to trust; information or access flowing both ways.

Counter-indicators: direct evidence that communication is consistently one-way; requests repeatedly receive no response; value flows only from one side.

Do not infer: low reciprocity from follower count, low posting frequency or missing response data.

### 2. Belonging — Who gets to say “we”?

Definition: a recognised boundary, identity or shared consciousness through which people can distinguish membership or affiliation and recognise one another.

Indicators: self-applied group language; shared symbols or references; recognised insiders and boundaries; relationships that continue beyond the owner.

Counter-indicators: evidence that people relate only to the owner and do not recognise one another or any common identity.

Do not infer: belonging from demographic similarity or being on the same mailing list.

### 3. Agency — Can people change what happens?

Definition: demonstrated capacity of participants to influence decisions, priorities, formats, norms or outcomes individually or collectively.

Indicators: suggestions adopted; member-led initiatives; shared governance; changed offers or decisions; collective action producing an effect.

Counter-indicators: explicit evidence that input is solicited but never affects decisions; participation is limited to reactions without influence.

Do not infer: agency from engagement alone.

### 4. Rhythm — What happens again and again?

Definition: recurring practices and expectations that make the relationship repeatable and create shared history.

Indicators: reliable return; recurring encounters; rituals; repeated roles; references to earlier interactions; expected cadence that survives individual campaigns.

Counter-indicators: explicit evidence that every interaction is isolated and must be rebuilt from zero.

Do not infer: rhythm from a publishing calendar unless people demonstrably return or participate around it.

### 5. Dissent — Can disagreement survive?

Definition: capacity of the relationship to contain challenge, criticism and difference without retaliation, expulsion or collapse.

Indicators: concrete disagreement followed by continued participation; criticism changes a decision; minority views remain visible; repair after conflict.

Counter-indicators: documented punishment, silencing, exit or relationship collapse after dissent.

Do not infer: safety from civility, positive comments or absence of visible conflict.

### 6. Mutual value — Would both sides miss it?

Definition: consequential value and dependence that run in both directions, such that removal would create a recognisable loss for more than one side.

Indicators: specific value each side names; repeated investment; switching costs that are relational rather than merely technical; outcomes neither side can produce alone.

Counter-indicators: direct evidence that either side can disappear without changing behaviour, access, decisions or outcomes for the other.

Do not infer: mutual value from reach, revenue or satisfaction alone.

## Evidence ladder

- `strong`: two or more concrete observations, including repetition or evidence across more than one source.
- `emerging`: at least one concrete behaviour, not yet shown to be repeated or dependable.
- `thin`: concrete counter-evidence shows a failed, one-sided or fragile pattern.
- `unknown`: direct evidence is absent, ambiguous or only aspirational.

These are qualitative evidence states, not scores and not a maturity ladder.

## Relationship forms

Audience, customer base, network, membership and community are descriptive forms that can overlap. Classification must include a reason and uncertainty. “Community” requires evidence across belonging, influence/agency, recurring shared practice and mutual value; a single strong lens is insufficient. “Unclear” is a valid outcome.

## Inference contract

Every material claim in a reading must be traceable to one of three buckets:

1. Observed: a faithful restatement of submitted evidence.
2. Inferred: a cautious interpretation explicitly marked as provisional.
3. Unknown: information required before a stronger conclusion.

The app must never convert aspiration into observation, missing evidence into failure, or attention into community.
